# KS3 Science Vocabulary & Morphology

Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary mined from the three **Springboard: KS3 Science Teacher Handbooks** (Hodder Education, 2024), plus a morphology teaching guide that flags the roots and affixes with the highest "unlocking" power — the words worth teaching because learning their Greek/Latin building blocks lets students decode many others.

## The two documents

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| [KS3_Science_Vocabulary_Tier2_Tier3.md](KS3_Science_Vocabulary_Tier2_Tier3.md) | The full vocabulary list, grouped **subject → unit → sub-topic**. Every word has an in-context gloss and, where useful, a `[root = meaning]` morphology tag. |
| [KS3_Science_Morphology_Guide.md](KS3_Science_Morphology_Guide.md) | The high-leverage analysis: **Part 0** ranks the 20 cross-curricular super-roots (span 2–3 sciences); then per-subject guides walk every sub-topic in order, listing the words whose roots unlock the most others, plus each subject's ranked "power roots." |

## Tiers (Beck & McKeown)

- **Tier 1** — everyday words (*water, push, cell-as-in-prison*). Excluded.
- **Tier 2** — high-utility **academic** words that recur across subjects (*structure, transfer, factor, proportion, evidence, absorb*). These carry the most cross-curricular reading payoff.
- **Tier 3** — **subject-specific** technical terms (*mitochondria, electrolysis, refraction, biodiversity*).

*Counts are per-sub-topic entries: a hard-working word (e.g. `structure`, `diffusion`, `photosynthesis`) is listed in every sub-topic where it is genuinely taught, so the same word appears more than once. This is deliberate — it shows where each word does its work.*

## The single best teaching bet

If you teach only a handful of roots, teach the cross-curricular super-roots from Part 0 of the morphology guide — the ones that pay off in biology **and** chemistry **and** physics: **trans-, -gen, con-/com-, re-, -meter, therm-, -duct, hydro-, photo-, bio-**. Each one turns a single lesson into decoding power across the whole curriculum.

## How this was built

The three handbook PDFs were text-extracted and split into nine subject×year slices. Nine agents extracted and tiered the vocabulary sub-topic by sub-topic with morphology tags; three further agents synthesised the high-leverage morphology per subject; the cross-curricular super-root index was assembled from the union. Source PDFs live outside the repo in the Springboard working directory.
