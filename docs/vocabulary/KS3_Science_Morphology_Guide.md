# KS3 Science — High-Leverage Morphology Guide

*Which words are worth teaching for their **roots**? This guide answers that. Learn one Greek or Latin building block and a student can decode a whole family of words they have never met — in science, across other subjects, and in everyday English. Built from all Tier 2 and Tier 3 vocabulary in the three Springboard KS3 Science Teacher Handbooks (Hodder, 2024).*

**How to read the "unlocks" lists:** each root is followed by curriculum words *and* everyday words, so students see the payoff. `photo- = light` → photosynthesis **and** photograph, photocopy, telephoto.

---

## PART 0 — The cross-curricular super-roots (teach these first)

These morphemes recur across **two or three of the sciences**. That makes them the highest-leverage of all: the same root pays off in biology *and* chemistry *and* physics, plus in English, geography and maths. If you teach only twenty roots in KS3, teach these.

**Two decoding lessons hidden in this table:**
- **Same meaning, shifting spelling** — `syn-→sym-` before b/m/p (symphony, symbiosis); `con-→com-/col-/cor-` before certain letters (combine, collide, corrode); `in-→im-` (impermeable). Teaching the shift stops students seeing them as different prefixes.
- **Opposition pairs** worth teaching back-to-back — `endo-/exo-` (within/out → endothermic vs exothermic), `aero-/an-` (air/without → aerobic vs anaerobic), `uni-/multi-` and `mono-/poly-` (one vs many).

The rest of this guide breaks the same analysis down **by subject, unit and sub-topic**, so you can pull the high-leverage words for exactly the topic you are teaching.

## PART 2 — Biology's power roots (ranked)

*Ranked by leverage: how many units they recur in AND how large a word family they generate (science + general English).*

### 1. bio- / bios — "life" (Greek)
**Appears in:** B1 (microbe), B7 (aerobic, anaerobic), B8 (bioaccumulation), B9 (biodiversity, amphibian). Also underlies the whole subject name.
**Word family:** biology, biography, biosphere, biodegradable, antibiotic, symbiosis, microbe, aerobic, anaerobic, biodiversity, biochemistry, biopsy, autobiography, amphibian, probiotic. *The single highest-leverage morpheme in the subject — it names biology itself and pairs with almost every other root (aero-, an-, micro-, sym-, anti-).*

### 2. photo- — "light" (Greek)
**Appears in:** B1 (photosynthesis), B6 (photosynthesis — the flagship reaction), B9 (photosynthesise).
**Word family:** photosynthesis, photograph, photocopy, photon, photogenic, telephoto, photojournalist, photoelectric, photosensitive, photography. *One tiny root and students instantly own "light" across science and everyday tech.*

### 3. -gen / genes / genos — "producing" AND "birth/origin" (Greek)
**Appears in:** B1/B4/B6/B7 (oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, carcinogen), B5/B9 (gene, genetic, gamete-adjacent). *Two closely related senses students should meet together.*
**Word family:** oxygen, hydrogen, nitrogen, carcinogen, generate, generator, genesis, genetics, gene, generation, genealogy, gender, pathogen, antigen, genre. *Double-value: it explains both where gases are "produced" and how life is "generated".*

### 4. -synthesis / syn- / sym- — "together, putting together" (Greek)
**Appears in:** B1 (protein synthesis), B6 (photosynthesis), plus synovial (B2).
**Word family:** photosynthesis, synthesis, synthetic, synonym, synchronise, symphony, symmetry, symbiosis, system, syndicate, sympathy. *Note the spelling shift syn- → sym- before b/m/p (symphony, symbiosis) — a great decoding lesson.*

### 5. chloro- / -phyll / -plast — the leaf-cell cluster (Greek)
**Appears in:** B1 (chloroplast), B6 (chlorophyll, chloroplast, mesophyll).
**Word family:** chlorophyll, chloroplast, chlorine, chloride, chloroform (chloro- = green); mesophyll, chlorophyll (phyllon = leaf); chloroplast, plastic, plaster, protoplasm (plast/plasm = moulded/formed). *Three morphemes that together build the machinery of photosynthesis — teach as a set.*

### 6. cyto- / -plasm / -some — the cell-part cluster (Greek)
**Appears in:** B1 (cytoplasm, ribosome), B3/B5/B7 (cytoplasm), B9 (chromosome).
**Word family:** cytoplasm, cytology, leucocyte, erythrocyte, phagocyte (cyto- = cell); cytoplasm, protoplasm, plasma (-plasm = formed); ribosome, chromosome, lysosome, somatic (-some/soma = body). *Unlocks the internal vocabulary of every cell diagram.*

### 7. respir- / -spir- — "to breathe" (Latin)
**Appears in:** B1/B2/B5 (respiration), B4 (respiratory), B7 (respiration — the flagship reaction).
**Word family:** respiration, respire, inspire, expire, perspire, conspire, aspire, transpire, spirit, respirator, spiration. *A gift for prefixes: the same stem plus re-/in-/ex-/per-/con- teaches four prefixes at once.*

### 8. micro- / -scope / -metron — the measurement/observation cluster (Greek)
**Appears in:** B1 (microscope, microbe, magnification-adjacent), B3 (microvilli), B2 (dynamometer).
**Word family:** microscope, microbe, microwave, microphone, microchip, microplastic (micro- = small); telescope, periscope, stethoscope, kaleidoscope (-scope = look); thermometer, barometer, diameter, perimeter, kilometre (-metron = measure). *Bridges biology to the whole science lab and to everyday tech words.*

### Runners-up worth teaching (rank 9–15)

9. **-vore / vorare — "to devour" (L)** — B8: carnivore, herbivore, omnivore, insectivore, voracious. Clean, memorable, whole-family-in-one-lesson.

10. **hydro- / hydor — "water" (Gk)** — B3/B4 (carbohydrate, hydrochloric): hydrate, dehydrate, hydrogen, hydroelectric, hydrant, hydraulic.

11. **-cide / caedere — "to kill" (L)** — B8: pesticide, insecticide, herbicide, fungicide, homicide, suicide, genocide, bactericide.

12. **-itis — "inflammation" (Gk)** — B4: bronchitis, appendicitis, tonsillitis, arthritis, dermatitis, meningitis. Instantly reads any "-itis" as swelling/inflammation.

13. **trans- — "across" (L)** — B1/B5 (transport, transfer): translate, transparent, transmit, transform, transfusion, transatlantic.

14. **therm-/therme — "heat" (Gk)** — B6 (endothermic, exothermic): thermometer, thermal, thermostat, hypothermia, geothermal, Thermos.

### 1. `duc-/duct-` = to lead (Latin)

- **Appears in:** C4.2, C5.1, C5.2, C6.1, C8.4 (thermal, thermometer, exo/endothermic)
- **Chemistry family:** thermal, thermometer, exothermic, endothermic, thermal decomposition, thermal store
- **General family:** thermostat, thermos, geothermal, hypothermia, thermodynamics, thermonuclear
- *Pairs with the exo-/endo- prefixes below — teach all three together.*

### 3. `exo-` = out / `endo-` = in, within (Greek)
- **Appears in:** C5.2, C8.4 (exothermic, endothermic)

- **General family (exo-):** exit, exodus, exoskeleton, exotic, exhale, extract; **(endo-):** endoskeleton, endoscope, endoplasmic, endogenous, endocrine

### 5. `-lysis` = a loosening, splitting (Greek)
- **Appears in:** C4.1 (catalyst), C7.2 (electrolysis)

- **General family:** analysis, paralysis, dialysis, analyse, palsy
- *Splitting = -lysis. Electro-lysis (split by electricity), cata-lyst (loosener). Recurs heavily at GCSE.*

### 6. `-gen` = producing, giving rise to (Greek)
- **Appears in:** C4.5, C6.2, C8.3 (hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, halogen)
- **Chemistry family:** hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, halogen, hydrogen carbonate
- **General family:** generate, genesis, genetic, gene, generator, oxygenate, pathogen, antigen, genre
- *Names four gases at once: hydro-gen (water-maker), oxy-gen (acid-maker), nitro-gen, halo-gen (salt-maker).*

### 7. `con-/com-` = together, with (Latin)
- **Appears in:** C1.2, C2.1, C2.3, C4.1, C4.2, C4.3, C7.3, C8.1 (compound, combustion, concentration, conserve, composite, composition)
- **Chemistry family:** compound, composite, composition, compose/decompose, concentration, conserve, condensation, combine, combustion
- **General family:** connect, combine, collect, correspond, compress, contain, conference, community
- *The most frequent prefix in chemistry. "Putting/holding TOGETHER" is the theme of compounds, mixtures and composites.*

### 8. `oxy-` = sharp, acid (Greek)
- **Appears in:** C2.3, C4.2, C6.1, C7.2, C8.3, C8.4 (oxide, oxidation, oxygen, dioxide)
- **Chemistry family:** oxygen, oxide, oxidation, dioxide, monoxide, hydroxide, antioxidant, peroxide
- **General family:** oxymoron, paroxysm, acute/acid (related "sharp" sense)
- *Ties oxygen to every oxide and the whole oxidation/reduction story.*

### 9. NUMBER PREFIXES `mono- = 1`, `di- = 2`, `tri- = 3` (Greek) — teach explicitly for GCSE nomenclature
- **Appears in:** C2.3, C7.3, C8.4 (monoxide, dioxide, monomer)
- **Chemistry family:** monoxide, dioxide, trioxide, monomer, dimer, monatomic, diatomic, carbon dioxide, sulfur trioxide
- **General family:** monorail, monologue, monopoly; dilemma, dioxin, diode; triangle, tripod, trilogy, trident
- *Essential for reading formulae by name: CO = carbon monOXIDE (one O), CO2 = carbon diOXIDE (two O).*

### 10. NAMING AFFIXES `-ide`, `-ate`, `-ite` — teach explicitly for GCSE nomenclature
- **Appears in:** C2.3, C4.2, C4.5, C6.1, C7.2 (oxide, chloride, carbonate, nitrate, sulfate)
- **What they signal:** `-ide` = a simple two-element compound (oxide, chloride, sulfide, hydroxide, fluoride); `-ate` = a salt containing that element PLUS oxygen, usually the higher-oxygen form (sulfate, nitrate, carbonate, phosphate); `-ite` = a salt with the element and oxygen, the LOWER-oxygen form (sulfite, nitrite)
- *Not an etymological "root that unlocks other words" so much as a decoding key: the ending tells you what is in the compound. `sodium chloride` = Na + Cl only; `sodium chlorate` = Na + Cl + O. This single distinction unlocks naming across the whole GCSE course. (Note `-ite` also = "mineral/rock": magnetite, haematite, graphite, granite.)*

### 11. `kyklos` → cycle = circle, wheel (Greek)
- **Appears in:** C8.2, C8.4, C8.5 (rock cycle, carbon cycle, recycle)
- **Chemistry family:** cycle, rock cycle, carbon cycle, recycle, recycling
- **General family:** bicycle, tricycle, cyclone, cyclical, encyclopedia, cyclist, Cyclops
- *The three "cycle" topics in C8 share one Greek root — a repeating wheel of change.*

### 12. `morph-` = form, shape (Greek)
- **Appears in:** C8.2 (metamorphic, metamorphism)
- **Chemistry/science family:** metamorphic, metamorphism, amorphous, polymorph, allotrope (related idea), morphology
- **General family:** morph, metamorphosis, morpheme, endomorph, anthropomorphic, Morpheus
- *Meta-morph-ic literally = "changed-form" rock. Great pairing with meta- (= change) and poly- (many forms).*

### 13. `poly- = many` + `-mer = part` (Greek)
- **Appears in:** C7.3, C8.5 (polymer, monomer)
- **Chemistry family:** polymer, monomer, dimer, isomer, polymerisation, polyester, polythene
- **General family (poly-):** polygon, polygraph, polytheism, polyglot, polygamy, Polynesia
- *Poly-mer = many parts; mono-mer = one part. Directly reusable when polymers return at GCSE.*

### 14. `press-` = to press (Latin)
- **Appears in:** C1.2, C1.4, C7.3, C8.1 (pressure, compress, compression)
- **Chemistry family:** pressure, gas pressure, compress, compression
- **General family:** impress, express, depress, oppress, suppress, espresso, printing press, imprint
- *Underlies gas pressure (C1), compressing gases, and pressure inside the Earth (C8).*

### 15. `vapor-` = steam (Latin) & `dens-` = thick (Latin) [paired change-of-state roots]
- **Appears in:** C1.3, C6.2, C8.3 (evaporate, vapour); C1.3, C8.1 (condense, density)

- **General family:** vapid, vaporiser; dense, condensed, densely
- *Two small roots that carry the whole language of changes of state and of why layers of the Earth sink or rise.*

## PART 2 — Physics's power roots (ranked)

Ranked by leverage: how many units the morpheme recurs in × how large a word family it generates.

### 1. `-meter / -metry` — measure (Gk, *metron*)
**Appears in:** P7 (ammeter, voltmeter), P3/P11 (newtonmeter), and every measuring instrument students meet.
**Family:** thermometer, barometer, speedometer, odometer, voltmeter, ammeter · diameter, perimeter, geometry, symmetry, trigonometry · metric, metre, kilometre. *The single highest-payoff affix: instantly signals "a device or act of measuring."*

### 2. `trans- / -vers- / -vert-` — across, turn (Lat, *trans, vertere*)
**Appears in:** P1 (transfer), P5 (transverse, transmit), P6 (transparent, translucent, transmit), P5 (transmit).
**Family:** transfer, transport, translate, transmit, transform, transparent, transplant · transverse, convert, reverse, divert, universe, version, controversy, vertical. *Two linked ideas — "across" (trans-) and "turn" (vert-) — that between them cover motion, waves, and light.*

### 3. `electro-` — amber/electric (Gk, *elektron*)
**Appears in:** P5 (electromagnetic), P7 (electron), P8 (electric field, electrostatic), P9 (electromagnet).
**Family:** electron, electric, electricity, electronic, electrode, electrocute, electrolysis, electromagnet, electrostatic. *Amber rubbed with fur was the first known static charge — the etymology IS the physics.*

### 4. `magnet-` — lodestone (Gk, from Magnesia)
**Appears in:** P5 (electromagnetic), P8 (electromagnetic), P9 (magnetic field, electromagnet, whole unit).
**Family:** magnet, magnetic, magnetism, magnetise, electromagnet, magnetosphere, magnetite. *Pairs with electro- to build the compound electromagnet — teach the two together.*

### 5. `-duc- / -duct-` — to lead (Lat, *ducere*)
**Appears in:** P7 (conductor, conduct), P9 (induced magnetism), P10 (reproduce).
**Family:** conduct, conductor, produce, reduce, introduce, deduce, induce, educate, aqueduct, viaduct, duke, duct. *A conductor "leads" current through; an aqueduct "leads" water.*

### 6. `re-` — back / again (Lat)
**Appears in:** everywhere — P1 (release, represent, renewable), P3 (resist, repel, resultant, reflect, refract), P7 (resistor, rearrange), P8 (repel), P10 (reproduce, repeatable).
**Family:** reflect, refract, resist, repel, resultant, renew, represent, reproduce, return, react. *The commonest prefix in the whole subject; the "back/again" sense explains reflect (bend back), repel (drive back), resist (stand back).*

### 7. `-sist- / -stat- / -stant-` — to stand (Lat/Gk, *sistere, stare, stat-*)
**Appears in:** P2 (distance, stationary, constant), P3 (resist), P6 (specular—no), P7 (resistor), P8 (static, electrostatic), P10 (states of matter).
**Family:** resist, insist, persist, consist, assist, exist · static, station, stationary, statue, stable, status, constant, instant, distance, obstacle. *One "stand" idea underlies resistance (standing against current) and static (standing charge).*

### 8. `-scop- / -scope` — to look/watch (Gk, *skopein*)
**Appears in:** P7 (microscopic, macroscopic), and P5 conceptually (oscilloscope).
**Family:** microscope, telescope, periscope, stethoscope, oscilloscope, horoscope · microscopic, macroscopic, scope. *Pairs neatly with micro-/macro- (small/large) and -meter (measure) as the "instrument" cluster.*

### 9. `photo- / lum- / luc-` — light (Gk *phos/photo-*; Lat *lumen/lucere*)
**Appears in:** P6 (translucent), P8 (illustrate), P11 (illuminate, illuminate).
**Family:** photo: photograph, photon, photosynthesis, photocopy, telephoto · lum/luc: luminous, illuminate, lumen, lucid, translucent, elucidate, Lucifer. *Two roots, one idea (light) — students meet luc- in translucent and lumen in illuminate.*

### 10. `therm-` — heat (Gk, *therme*)
**Appears in:** P1 (thermal energy store), P10 (energy in matter, temperature link).
**Family:** thermometer, thermostat, thermal, geothermal, hypothermia, thermodynamics, Thermos, isotherm. *Small root, huge cross-subject reach into chemistry and geography.*

### 11. `-tend- / -tens-` and `-flect-/-fract-` — stretch; bend/break (Lat *tendere*; *flectere*, *frangere*)
**Appears in:** P1/P3 (extension, tension), P6 (reflect, refract).
**Family:** tend: extend, extension, tension, tendon, tent, intense, tendency · flect: reflect, deflect, flex, flexible, circumflex · fract: refract, fracture, fraction, fragment, fragile. *The "bend vs break" contrast is exactly the reflect vs refract distinction students must learn.*

**Appears in:** sphere/-sphere P4 (atmosphere), P9 (sphere), P11 (hemisphere); grav- P1/P3/P11 (gravity, gravitational); kine- P1/P10 (kinetic).
**Family:** sphere: hemisphere, atmosphere, biosphere, stratosphere, spherical · grav: gravity, gravitate, grave, aggravate · kine: kinetic, cinema, kinesthetic, telekinesis. *Three compact roots that each recur across three or more units.*

**Family:** pel: repel, expel, propel, compel, dispel, propeller · tract: attract, extract, subtract, contract, tractor, protractor · press: compress, express, impress, pressure, print. *The three "force verbs" of the Forces unit — push (pel), pull (tract), squeeze (press).*

## Teach-together set: the eponym cluster (units named after scientists)

These are NOT decodable by root — they honour people — so teach them as one memorised set, tagging each with its quantity so students never confuse the unit with the quantity:

| Unit | Symbol | Measures | Named after |
|---|---|---|---|
| **newton** | N | force | Isaac Newton |
| **joule** | J | energy / work | James Prescott Joule |
| **watt** | W | power | James Watt |
| **volt** | V | potential difference | Alessandro Volta |
| **amp/ampere** | A | current | André-Marie Ampère |
| **ohm** | Ω | resistance | Georg Ohm |
| **coulomb** | C | charge | Charles-Augustin de Coulomb |
| **hertz** | Hz | frequency | Heinrich Hertz |
| **pascal** | Pa | pressure | Blaise Pascal |

*Teaching tip: capital-letter symbols (N, J, W, V, A, C, Hz, Pa) are a giveaway that a unit is an eponym — contrast with lower-case m, s, kg for the "ordinary" units. The quantity a unit measures is the thing worth over-learning: joule↔energy, watt↔power, volt↔potential difference.*
