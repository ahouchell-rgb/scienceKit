## 1. The routes, honestly assessed

There are four ways this school comes to exist (or an existing school becomes it). They are not alternatives to choose between today — they are a sequence, because the first two are the entry fee for the last two.

**Route A — Found the school (LA route).** The central free-school wave programme is dead (see `research/07-governance-structures.md`): the October 2024 review cancelled most unopened projects, and from 1 September 2026 the free-school presumption itself is abolished. What remains: (i) an LA-run competition for a new academy where basic need exists, and (ii) — new from September 2026 — the LA proposing its own maintained school. Both require an LA with demonstrated basic need, a site, and capital. Thurrock is a plausible long-run candidate: large housing growth (Purfleet-on-Thames regeneration, Grays town centre) will eventually generate secondary basic need, and the blueprint's inclusion-first design is precisely what a post-s114 council being measured on high-needs spend wants to commission (every child kept out of a £61,500 independent placement is the LA's saving). But nobody hands a 1,500-pupil school to an ECT. This route opens ~2033 at the earliest, and only to someone with headship credibility or a trust behind them. **Verdict: the destination, not the starting move.**

**Route B — Lead an existing school into the blueprint.** Become a senior leader, then a head, of a school serving this catchment — ideally one in difficulty, where a governing body or trust is actively looking for someone with a plan — and implement the blueprint's school-sized subset (the instructional core, the SEND model, the behaviour-with-backfill position, the extended-day tiering). This is how almost every operator in the research base started: Dixons, Star and Ark grew leaders inside schools before they ran them. From ECT2 this takes 8–10 years to headship on an honest timeline. **Verdict: the main road. Everything in §2 feeds it.**

**Route C — Influence many schools through the tools and the evidence.** Springboard, the lesson engine and the question bank are, almost exactly, the delivery mechanism for Bet 5 of the blueprint (knowledge-rich curriculum, daily retrieval, self-quizzing homework, centrally-resourced materials — see `blueprint/1-vision-principles.md`). The 2025 EdTech evidence (see `research/09-edtech-ai.md`) and the trust-centralisation evidence (Dixons OpenSource, Ark's network curriculum, Oak) say the winning shape is *curriculum-embedded, teacher-workload-reducing, fidelity-protecting* — which is what you have built. This route needs no permission, no seniority and no waiting for induction to finish. It is also the great equaliser for your career stage: **a teacher whose tools demonstrably move retention and mastery data outranks their job title.** The TLR3 makes this concrete — the KS3 curriculum you are now paid to lead is the natural home of the pilot. **Verdict: the active route right now, run as evidence-generation for Routes B and A, not just as a product.**

**Route D — Institutional partnership.** Bring the blueprint to people already building versions of it: the Reach Foundation's Cradle-to-Career Partnership works with schools and places nationally to replicate the Feltham model (`research/12-reach-feltham.md`); Dixons OpenSource publishes and network-shares its systems (`research/13-dixons.md`). Joining these communities costs little, has no seniority requirement, and buys pattern-matching, mentors, and — eventually — the trust partner Route A requires. **Verdict: run permanently in the background from this term.**

### Recommendation

**Sequence C → B → A, with D always on.** For the next 12 months the tools-and-evidence route is the priority because it is the only one you can act on this week, it lives inside your TLR3 rather than on top of it, and its outputs (pilot data, a rebuilt KS3 curriculum, a published case study) compound directly into leadership credibility. The explicit ambition — kept written down and reviewed annually — is headship of a high-deprivation Essex/Thurrock school by **~2034–36**, with the LA new-school route as the branch taken if and when Thurrock's basic need and your credibility intersect.

### And the cradle-to-career question, answered straight

Can an ECT2 build a cradle-to-career pathway? Not the 2–18 institution — that is the year-8-plus destination. But cradle-to-career is a *practice* before it is a building, and there is a real version of it at every rung:

1. **Now (TLR3 scale):** KS2→KS3 is the first cliff in the cradle-to-career chain, it is *inside your curriculum remit*, and two-thirds of the blueprint school's Year 7 would face it too (`blueprint/0-canon.md`). Feeder-primary curriculum continuity, shared retrieval content into Year 6, transition data handover, a Year 7 curriculum that builds from what primaries actually teach — this is genuine cradle-to-career work an ECT can lead.
2. **Years 2–4 (department/school scale):** the destination end — KS4 pathways, Gatsby benchmarks, alumni tracking — via a T&L or inclusion brief.
3. **Years 5+ (school/place scale):** the family hub, nursery and all-through structure, via headship or the LA partnership. The Reach Foundation network (Route D) is where you learn this layer years before you can run it.

## 2. The credibility ladder

What the person who gets to build this school has, mapped against where you are — ordered by acquisition:

1. **A track record with numbers attached (start now).** The September 2026 pilot is the first rung: golden-set outcomes, retention/mastery deltas, teacher-workload testimony. Write it up properly (baseline, comparison, effect, limitations — the EEF's own reporting style) whether the result is good or bad.
2. **Middle leadership — ✅ partially banked already.** The KS3 curriculum TLR3 is the rung the first draft told you to go and ask for. The task now is different: **deliver it measurably.** A KS3 science curriculum rebuilt on retrieval/mastery principles, with the question bank as its Do-Now spine and Springboard as its homework layer, plus before/after data — that artefact is worth more at AHT interview than the title. Expect the next rung (second-in-department, then HoD) around 2028–29.
3. **Complete induction (2026–27) — the gate everything passes through.** No NPQ this year: the golden thread sequences NPQs after induction, and ECT2 assessment + TLR3 delivery is the right full load.
4. **NPQs (from autumn 2027).** NPQ Leading Teaching or Leading Teacher Development first (it matches the coaching-led blueprint, your TLR evidence and your actual strengths), applying in spring/summer 2027 for the October 2027 cohort; NPQSL ~2029–30; NPQH in the DHT years. Check scholarship eligibility at application — DfE funding rules have tightened since 2025 and now prioritise disadvantage-heavy schools and specific roles. Providers to prefer: **Ambition Institute** or **Teach First** — both double as networks.
5. **SEND credibility (years 2–4).** The blueprint's distinctive claim is inclusion-and-attainment-solved-once. A science teacher who becomes genuinely expert in SEND delivery — the EEF "Five-a-day", structured TA deployment, adaptive teaching, eventually the NPQ SENCO — is rare, and is exactly the profile the 2026 White Paper decade (tiered support, £4bn mainstream inclusion money) will promote fast.
6. **Senior leadership with an inclusion or T&L brief (~2030–31).** AHT in your school or — often faster — in a school in difficulty nearby, where turnaround experience is available cheap.
7. **Relationships (continuous, no seniority required).** Named, real, worth cultivating deliberately:
   - **Local trusts:** Ortu Federation, Osborne Co-operative Academy Trust, Harris Federation's Thurrock academies — the realistic employers for the middle rungs, and the realistic bidding partners for Route A.
   - **LAs:** Thurrock Council education/SEND commissioning (the buyer of base places and, post-2026, a potential school proposer); Essex County Council as the larger neighbour.
   - **National:** Reach Foundation (Cradle-to-Career Partnership), Dixons OpenSource, EEF (as a pilot-evidence consumer and eventual evaluation partner), Challenge Partners, CST events, researchED (cheap visibility among exactly the people who run knowledge-rich trusts).
8. **A public artefact (year 1–2).** The blueprint itself, edited into a readable public document or site, is a calling card almost nobody at ECT stage has. Publishing it (with the honest evidence-flags kept in) does more for credibility than any CV line — and neutralises the thinness of the CV itself.

## 5. Department-level aims (2026–27 — your TLR3 vehicle)

The department manual in `department/` (00–05) is written as the blueprint's operational layer *and* as your implementation manual. The TLR3 is a fixed-term KS3-curriculum project reporting to the HoD, not the headship and not a coaching caseload — so these aims are scoped to what that role can actually move. Each names what is **in your gift** (you can just do it) versus what **needs HoD/SLT/SENCO permission** (you propose and pilot; someone else mandates), the **evidence** it throws off, and a one-line success measure. Every aim is chosen because the same artefact is *simultaneously* ECT2 induction evidence (against the Teachers' Standards) and NPQ-application material (LT/LTD, from autumn 2027) — you are not doing this work three times.

1. **Make the retrieval Do-Now the KS3 science routine, from the tagged bank.** *In your gift:* select and sequence the 4–6-question cumulative Do-Now (current + prior + 6-months-back) from the 1,960-question bank for every KS3 unit, and run it to automaticity in your own classes. *Needs HoD:* a department-wide mandate so every teacher runs it. *Evidence:* class-average self-mark scores logged per unit. **Success measure:** by October half-term, every one of your KS3 classes opens on the cumulative Do-Now daily with class-average data captured; a department-wide version proposed to the HoD with your classes as the proof. *(Induction: TS2, TS4, TS6. NPQ: retrieval-practice implementation case.)*

2. **Stand up the half-termly mastery checkpoint and objective-level RAG tracker.** *In your gift:* design the closed-book checkpoint (unit + 20% cumulative) and the per-objective RAG tracker for the mapped KS3 units. *Needs HoD:* agreement to route below-threshold objectives into the following week's Do-Now rotation and persistent gaps into the 15:30–16:30 support block. *Evidence:* objective-level RAG data each half-term, showing responsive reteach. **Success measure:** every mapped KS3 unit produces objective-level RAG data each half-term, and at least one department-wide reteach decision is made from it. *(Induction: TS6 assessment. NPQ: assessment-and-responsive-teaching evidence.)*

3. **Install whole-class feedback as the KS3 workload ceiling.** *In your gift:* model WCF and live marking on all your own KS3 extended writing and 6-mark answers, and write the one-page departmental WCF rationale (with the EEF, DfE-2016 and Ofsted lines quoted verbatim). *Needs HoD/SLT:* adoption of WCF as the stated KS3 marking policy so no teacher is expected to write per-book comments. *Evidence:* WCF sheets plus the termly 6-books-per-class QA sample. **Success measure:** your KS3 classes run zero written-comment marking with WCF sheets on file, and a WCF policy paragraph is tabled for the department handbook. *(Induction: TS6. NPQ: workload/implementation evidence.)*

4. **Run the arrival-to-exit behaviour routine inside the whole-school system, not a private one.** *In your gift:* line-up/meet-and-greet, Do-Now-within-60-seconds, scripted lab transitions, deliberate exit, ≥4:1 praise ratio, and the lab-safety override — all drilled in your own room and documented so a cover teacher could run it. *Needs SLT:* the C1–C4 ladder, centralised detention and on-call rota are whole-school machinery you operate, not author. *Evidence:* your own C-code log split by reason, and the reward:sanction ratio tracked weekly. **Success measure:** your classes run the routine to automaticity with a weekly ≥4:1 praise ratio and a flat-or-falling C3/C4 rate; the lab-safety override is written down. *(Induction: TS1, TS7. NPQ: behaviour-and-culture implementation.)*

5. **Build the department SEND crib sheet for your KS3 classes.** *In your gift:* one side of A4 per class — primary need, current scaffold from the EEF five-a-day, and a review date for every SEND pupil — driven by your own exit-ticket and live-marking evidence. *Needs SENCO + HoD:* sign-off on any pre-agreed behaviour adjustment before it goes on the sheet. *Evidence:* the crib sheets themselves, reviewed at each half-termly APDR point. **Success measure:** every one of your KS3 classes has a current crib sheet where no SEND pupil sits without a scaffold and a review date. *(Induction: TS5. NPQ / later NPQ SENCO: adaptive-teaching evidence.)*

6. **Open the KS2→3 transition thread as the first cradle-to-career move.** *Needs HoD/SLT and the transition lead:* permission to contact 2–3 feeder primaries — you cannot self-authorise external school contact. *In your gift once cleared:* map what Year 6 science actually covers, agree shared vocabulary/retrieval content, and specify the science transition-data handover. *Evidence:* a Year 6→7 content-and-vocabulary map and an agreed data-handover format. **Success measure:** by summer 2027, 2–3 feeder primaries are engaged and a shared-vocabulary + data-handover plan is agreed for the September 2027 Year 7. *(Induction: wider-professional-responsibilities evidence. NPQ: curriculum-continuity leadership.)*

7. **Deploy Springboard as the monitored KS3 homework layer and close the mastery-graph gap.** *In your gift:* set Springboard self-quizzing on the knowledge organiser as KS3 homework, tracking minutes-per-week split by PP/SEND (completion, not access), and add objective rows for the 10 still-unmapped KS3 units. *Needs HoD:* endorsement of Springboard as the department homework platform. *Evidence:* completion data by cohort; a fully mapped KS3 mastery graph. **Success measure:** Springboard homework completion is monitored for your KS3 classes with the PP/SEND split visible, and all 10 unmapped units gain objective rows. *(Induction: TS4, TS6. NPQ + Springboard case study: dosage-tracked-practice evidence.)*

8. **Write the year up as one evidence artefact.** *In your gift entirely.* Package aims 1–7 into a single before/after write-up (baseline, comparison, effect, limitations — EEF reporting style) covering the rebuilt KS3 curriculum and its data trail. **Success measure:** by summer 2027 a single document exists that serves, unaltered, as ECT2 induction evidence, the spring-2027 NPQ LT/LTD application backbone, and Springboard's flagship case study — one body of work, three audiences. *(This is the artefact §2 rung 2 and §3 Year 1 both call for.)*

Standing constraint on all eight: **none may threaten passing induction** (the §6 induction override applies), and none is a licence to mandate department-wide what is only in your gift to pilot — you run these in your own classroom first and propose the department-wide version with your data as the evidence.
