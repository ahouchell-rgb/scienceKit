# 05 — Staffing and Workforce in a Hard-to-Staff, High-Deprivation Catchment

*Research brief for the perfect-school design project. Compiled 17 July 2026. Policy status verified against 2025–26 sources.*

## What the evidence says

**The recruitment market is structurally against us.** NFER's [Teacher Labour Market in England Annual Report 2025](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/teacher-labour-market-in-england-annual-report-2025/) found trainee recruitment below target in all but five secondary subjects in 2024/25, unfilled vacancies at ~6 per 1,000 teachers in 2023/24 (double pre-pandemic, six times the 2010/11 rate), and — critically for us — shortages concentrating in schools with disadvantaged intakes, which rely more heavily on unqualified and non-specialist teachers. The [DfE School Workforce Census](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/school-workforce-in-england/2025) recorded 2,200 unfilled posts in November 2024, easing to ~1,600 in November 2025 — improvement, not resolution. In the most disadvantaged schools, 34% of teachers have under five years' experience vs 20% in the least disadvantaged ([Commons Library CBP-7222](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-7222/)). Experience inequality, not just headcount, is the core problem.

**Why teachers leave — and it's mostly not pay.** In DfE's [Working Lives of Teachers and Leaders](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/working-lives-of-teachers-and-leaders) survey series (wave 4, Nov 2025, n≈10,800), leavers and would-be leavers cite high workload (84%), poor wellbeing (75%), inspection/outcomes pressure (64%), pupil behaviour (44%) and lack of flexible working (39%) ahead of pay. Teachers reporting they spend "too much time" on behaviour incidents rose from 50% (2022) to 59% (2025), and teachers in schools with good behaviour and strong behaviour support are measurably more likely to stay ([Government evidence to the STRB, Oct 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69034343fabc9f10a832a838/Government_evidence_to_the_STRB_2026.pdf)). The [National Institute of Teaching's review of practice in disadvantaged schools](https://niot.org.uk/research-projects/teacher_recruitment_retention_schools_socioeconomically_disadvantaged_areas_england) found teachers rank balanced workload, respect, and effective leadership above financial incentives; working environment was the most emphasised retention lever.

**Money works for attraction, but only in large doses.** The [EEF's evidence review on recruitment and retention](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/financial-incentives-and-reducing-workload-could-help-teacher-and-retention-according-to-evidence) concludes financial incentives are the best-evidenced strategy for attracting teachers to disadvantaged schools — but small or merely-eligible incentives do little, and money does not outweigh poor working conditions. Causal international evidence: a Chilean bonus worth ~16% of salary raised retention of high-performers in disadvantaged schools by 17–21pp ([Elacqua et al., JPAM 2022](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1338424)); a $1,800/yr North Carolina shortage-subject bonus cut turnover ~17%. England's live equivalent is the [targeted retention incentive](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/targeted-retention-incentive-payments-for-school-teachers): £3,000–£6,000/yr (tax-free equivalent varies) for teachers in their first five years teaching maths, physics, chemistry or computing, with the top rate reserved for the most disadvantaged 30% of schools — our catchment should qualify for the maximum.

**Instructional coaching: strong but honestly contested evidence.** [Kraft, Blazar & Hogan's 2018 meta-analysis](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654318759268) of 60 causal studies found pooled effects of 0.49 SD on instruction quality and 0.18 SD on attainment — leading [Sam Sims to call coaching "the best-evidenced form of CPD"](https://samsims.education/2019/02/19/247/), a position operationalised in England by Ambition Institute, Ark and [Steplab](https://steplab.co/resources/the-evidence-and-rationale-behind-steplab/66d9c87f0982810001156bd9). **Caveats to take seriously:** effects shrink at scale; more coaching hours did not predict bigger effects (quality and focus beat dosage); and [Sims et al.'s 2025 RER meta-analysis of teacher PD](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543231217480) found PD overall adds ~1.5 months of pupil progress but the average effect in *preregistered* trials is not significantly different from zero. Coaching remains the best bet available, but we should treat it as a well-theorised mechanism (their "IMTP" model: instil insight, motivate, teach techniques, embed practice) to implement carefully, not a guaranteed effect size.

**Early career support: the framework alone doesn't retain.** From September 2025 the ECF became the [Early Career Teacher Entitlement (ECTE)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/early-career-teacher-entitlement/early-career-teacher-entitlement-changes-for-2025--2), built on the combined [ITTECF framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/initial-teacher-training-core-content-framework-for-iqts/using-the-initial-teacher-training-and-early-career-framework-applies-from-september-2025) (stronger SEND, oracy and cognitive-development content — relevant to our SEND-heavy intake), with mentor training cut from two years to one to reduce workload. But NFER's [early roll-out evaluation](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/evaluation-of-the-early-roll-out-of-the-early-career-framework/) found a null effect on retention overall (though participating ECTs were more likely to stay in their induction school), and mentor workload was the standout implementation problem. Retention of the 2023/24 ECF cohort into year two was [89.7%](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/teacher-and-leader-development-ecf-and-npqs/2024-25). Lesson: the entitlement is a floor; school-level mentoring quality and mentor time are what move retention.

**Teaching assistants: powerful in structure, harmful when informal.** The [EEF's updated Deploying Teaching Assistants guidance (March 2025)](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/teaching-assistants), synthesising 52+ studies into five recommendations, is unambiguous: TAs must supplement, not replace, the teacher; the lowest-attaining pupils should get *at least as much* teacher time as others; TAs should scaffold towards independence, not velcro to individual pupils; and TA-delivered [structured interventions](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/teaching-assistant-interventions) with training and scripts deliver +3–4 months (1:1/small-group trials range +4–6 months; all seven EEF TA-intervention trials showed positive effects). Deployed informally as a substitute teacher for SEND pupils, TAs can produce *negative* effects. For a high-EHCP intake this is the single most consequential deployment decision we will make.

## What the best operators actually do

- **Dixons Academies Trust (Bradford/Leeds/Liverpool/Manchester — deprived catchments)** introduced a [nine-day fortnight](https://www.dixonsat.com/articles/how-were-giving-teachers-a-nine-day-fortnight) for all teaching staff on full pay from September 2024, with no reduction in pupil teaching time, an "anchor day" when all staff are in, and school-level autonomy on the model. After year one Dixons reports [lower turnover and sickness absence](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/nine-day-fortnight-schools-teachers-dixons/); an [EEF Teacher Choices trial is now evaluating it](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/teacher-choices-ndwf). Honest flags: some ECTs reported reduced access to senior support, it demands sophisticated timetabling, and critics claim it depends on [large-group lecture-style lessons to cover release](https://educationuncovered.co.uk/news/academy-trust-that-brought-in-the-nine-day-fortnight-needs-classes-of-120-to-allow-it-to-continue). Evidence: promising self-report, RCT pending — adopt as a pilot, not an article of faith.
- **Michaela / high-performing urban schools** strip workload at source: no written marking (whole-class feedback instead — [Teach Like a Champion write-up](https://teachlikeachampion.org/blog/reducing-teacher-workload-re-thinking-marking-michaela-files-part-1/)), fully centralised detentions and behaviour systems so individual teachers never chase sanctions ([TeacherToolkit on centralised detentions](https://www.teachertoolkit.co.uk/2022/10/12/centralised-detentions-the-pros-and-cons/)), and shared centrally-produced curriculum resources so teachers plan delivery, not materials.
- **Ambition Institute / Ark / Steplab schools** run weekly or fortnightly low-stakes instructional coaching for *every* teacher (not just strugglers), with granular action steps, deliberate practice, and coaching load timetabled into middle leaders' directed time — [Steplab's implementation guidance](https://steplab.co/resources/the-evidence-and-rationale-behind-steplab/66d9c87f0982810001156bd9) and [Ambition's scaling work](https://www.ambition.org.uk/blog/supporting-schools-to-maximise-the-impact-of-instructional-coaching/) both stress protected coach time as the binding constraint. Steplab now extends coaching to [TA practice development](https://steplab.co/resources/developing-ta-practice-with-instructional-coaching-part-1/66d9c87b0982810001156bbd).
- **Successful disadvantaged-area schools** (NIoT review, 24 schools) combine supportive working environments, visible career pathways, welfare provision, targeted financial incentives and work-life balance measures — with leadership quality and manageable workload as the two consistent retention differentiators.

## Design implications for our school

1. **Design retention around working conditions, then top up with money.** Centralised behaviour system (single sanctions/detention operation run by SLT, on-call staffed every period), no written-marking policy with whole-class feedback, and centrally resourced curriculum from day one. These attack the top three leaving drivers (workload, wellbeing, behaviour) simultaneously and cost less than salary premiums.
2. **Bank the DfE money, add our own.** Maximise the £6,000 targeted retention incentive (our deprivation profile qualifies), and budget a school-funded retention supplement (~10–16% of salary, per the Chilean evidence) for shortage subjects and SEND-specialist roles — but only alongside, never instead of, the conditions above.
3. **Accept a young workforce; engineer around it.** We will run ECT-heavy (expect ~a third of staff under five years' experience). Counter with: mentors given timetabled release (not goodwill time — the ECF's known failure mode), weekly instructional coaching for every teacher via a Steplab-style system, and coaching capacity costed into every middle-leadership role (roughly 2–3 periods/week per coach for 5–6 coachees).
4. **TA model straight from EEF 2025:** TAs never the default provision for EHCP pupils; lowest attainers timetabled *more* teacher time; TA hours concentrated in a scheduled programme of evidence-based structured interventions (with training, scripts and coaching); joint teacher–TA preparation time built into the timetable. For our SEND population, hire fewer general TAs and more trained intervention specialists.
5. **Pilot flexible working deliberately.** Offer PPA-from-home and part-time-friendly timetabling immediately; pilot a Dixons-style nine-day fortnight in year two with an anchor day, evaluating against the forthcoming EEF trial — and protect ECT access to senior staff on flex days.
6. **Exploit the 2025–26 policy environment.** ECTE (from Sept 2025) gives us funded, SEND-strengthened early-career training; Ofsted's [report-card framework (from Nov 2025)](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ofsted-confirms-changes-to-education-inspection-and-unveils-new-look-report-cards) explicitly inspects staff workload and wellbeing — our workload guardrails become an inspection asset, though sector commentary warns the new framework may itself add leader workload.

**Evidence-strength flags:** financial incentives (attraction) and structured TA interventions — strong causal evidence; coaching — good but weakening under preregistration scrutiny; nine-day fortnight and flexible working — promising, RCT pending; NIoT practice review — descriptive, not causal.
