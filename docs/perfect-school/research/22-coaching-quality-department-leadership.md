# 22 — Department Leadership, Instructional Coaching and Quality Assurance

*Research brief for the perfect-school design project. Compiled 17 July 2026. Operational layer beneath the whole-school blueprint (see `0-canon.md`); science department used as the worked example. Policy status verified against 2025–26 sources.*

## What the evidence says

**Coaching is the best-evidenced lever we have, but the evidence has real edges.** Kraft, Blazar & Hogan's meta-analysis of 60 causal studies found teacher coaching improves instruction by **0.49 SD** and pupil achievement by **0.18 SD** ([Kraft et al. 2018, RER](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/mkraft/files/kraft_blazar_hogan_2018_teacher_coaching.pdf)) — the basis for Sam Sims calling it ["the best-evidenced form of CPD"](https://samsims.education/2019/02/19/247/). Two caveats change how we should build the department layer, not whether we adopt coaching. First, **effects shrink at scale** (large at-scale programmes fall to roughly 0.10 SD on achievement) — most of the underlying studies are US elementary literacy, coached by dedicated external coaches, not English secondary HoDs running a full timetable. Second, **more hours of coaching did not predict bigger effects**; quality and focus of the coaching beat dosage — an argument for short, weekly, tightly-scoped sessions over occasional long ones. Sims et al.'s 2025 review-of-reviews on teacher PD found PD overall adds only ~1.5 months of pupil progress, and the effect in *preregistered* trials is not significantly different from zero ([Sims et al. 2025, RER](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543231217480)) — a sobering counterweight to enthusiasm, and reason to treat coaching as a well-theorised mechanism to implement with discipline, not a guaranteed effect size.

**The EEF's mechanism-based model tells us *why* coaching beats generic INSET.** The EEF's Effective Professional Development guidance report distils the evidence into 14 mechanisms across four categories — Build Knowledge, Motivate Teachers, Develop Teaching Techniques, Embed Practice ([EEF guidance report](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/effective-professional-development/EEF-Effective-Professional-Development-Guidance-Report.pdf)). Coaching is structurally suited to deliver the mechanisms one-off training cannot: modelling, rehearsal, and monitoring-with-feedback require an observer watching real practice and returning to it, repeatedly, over weeks. This is the theoretical case for weekly departmental coaching cycles rather than termly CPD days.

**Deliberate practice, applied honestly.** Ambition Institute and Steplab operationalise coaching as a weekly cycle built around a single, granular "action step" — small, observable, high-leverage teaching techniques (e.g. "cold-call before revealing the answer") rather than vague goals like "improve questioning" ([Steplab evidence rationale](https://steplab.co/resources/the-evidence-and-rationale-behind-steplab/66d9c87f0982810001156bd9)). But a 2025 critique in the *British Journal of Educational Studies* is worth taking seriously: teaching is not a "performance profession" reducible to decomposable skills the way music or sport is, and coaching models that over-mechanise practice risk missing goals that are not solely about measurable attainment gains ([Perry, *BJES* 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0305764X.2025.2516524)). Read against Kraft's dosage finding, the design implication is: keep action steps concrete and few, but don't let a coaching system substitute for professional judgement.

**Low-stakes QA is now also the regulatory direction of travel.** Ofsted's 2025 framework explicitly moves away from high-stakes subject "deep dives" toward learning walks and professional conversations alongside leaders, and states inspectors look for "what is typical," not a performance to impress ([Schools inspection toolkit, updated 5 Nov 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b26c69456634d9795fde0/Schools_inspection_toolkit.pdf); [Tes summary](https://www.tes.com/en-gb/advice-hub/understanding-new-ofsted-framework-2025)). This licenses — indeed now expects — internal QA that mirrors coaching's ungraded, developmental logic rather than a snapshot-and-judge model, closing the gap between "what Ofsted wants" and "what actually improves teaching."

**The ECT/mentoring reform is a workload fix, not (yet) a proven retention fix.** From September 2025 the ECF was folded into the single [ITTECF framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/initial-teacher-training-core-content-framework-for-iqts/using-the-initial-teacher-training-and-early-career-framework-applies-from-september-2025), spanning ITT through year 2, with strengthened SEND and cognitive-science content and mentor training cut from two years to one specifically to reduce mentor workload ([Ambition Institute explainer](https://www.ambition.org.uk/what-is-the-ittecf-and-what-does-it-mean-for-the-early-career-framework/); [Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/dfe-combines-two-flagship-schemes-into-new-initial-teacher-training-and-early-career-framework-after-ecf-review/)). NFER's evaluation of the early ECF roll-out found a **null effect on retention overall**, with mentor workload the standout implementation failure ([NFER evaluation](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/evaluation-of-the-early-roll-out-of-the-early-career-framework/)); year-2 retention of the 2023/24 cohort was 89.7% ([DfE stats](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/teacher-and-leader-development-ecf-and-npqs/2024-25)). Lesson: the national entitlement is a floor. What moves an ECT's actual practice and retention is protected mentor time and mentor skill inside the department — which is a HoD staffing and timetabling decision, not a DfE one.

## What the best operators actually do (named, concrete)

- **Dixons Academies Trust** replaced performance appraisal entirely with universal instructional coaching for every member of staff (not just strugglers or ECTs), run on Steplab, plus **twice-weekly whole-staff deliberate practice of shared techniques in staff morning meetings** ([dixonsat.com/why/coaching-and-practice](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/coaching-and-practice)). The trust employs dedicated Evidence Leads so the model stays research-honest rather than drifting into folklore ([Bradford Research School](https://researchschool.org.uk/bradford/news/making-the-most-of-instructional-coaching)). Coaching is deliberately run *within* departments so HoDs, not a separate CPD team, own engagement ([Steplab case studies](https://steplab.co/resources/launching-steplab-in-incremental-steps-at-harris-st-johns-wood/66d9c88c0982810001156c22)).
- **Steplab schools generally** fix feedback conversations to a named slot in the school week (commonly a weekly after-school slot) so coaching isn't squeezed out by whatever else is happening that week, and give coaches a bank of pre-written "Action Steps" — a curriculum for coaches, mirroring the pupil curriculum — so every coaching conversation has the same granular, technique-level shape rather than being improvised ([Steplab resources](https://steplab.co/resources/how-can-subject-leads-use-lesson-drop-ins-to-design-great-pd/66d9c8620982810001156b0a)). Steplab has extended the same model to TA practice development, treating TAs as a coached professional group rather than an unmanaged resource ([Steplab TA coaching](https://steplab.co/resources/developing-ta-practice-with-instructional-coaching-part-1/66d9c87b0982810001156bbd)).
- **Michaela Community School** strips department-meeting time of everything that isn't teaching quality: no written marking (whole-class feedback instead, freeing HoD and teacher time for coaching rather than marking scrutiny — [Teach Like a Champion write-up](https://teachlikeachampion.org/blog/reducing-teacher-workload-re-thinking-marking-michaela-files-part-1/)) and fully centralised behaviour/detentions so department time is never consumed chasing sanctions ([TeacherToolkit](https://www.teachertoolkit.co.uk/2022/10/12/centralised-detentions-the-pros-and-cons/)). Shared, centrally-produced curriculum resources mean department meetings discuss delivery and pupil misconceptions, not "what shall we teach on Tuesday."
- **Ambition Institute** frames the HoD's job explicitly as running the department as an "improving system" — protected weekly coaching, subject-specific action steps, and coaching load timetabled into middle leaders' directed time, with protected coach time named as *the* binding constraint on whether coaching survives contact with a real school week ([Ambition scaling blog](https://www.ambition.org.uk/blog/supporting-schools-to-maximise-the-impact-of-instructional-coaching/)).
- **The EEF's Deploying Teaching Assistants guidance (updated March 2025)** gives the QA layer its sharpest rule for department leaders of any subject, science included: TAs must supplement, not replace, the teacher; the lowest-attaining pupils should get *at least as much* teacher contact time as others; and TA-delivered structured interventions with training and scripts (the department's job to supply) deliver +3–4 months, while informal "velcro" deployment to individual SEND pupils can be *negative* ([EEF guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/teaching-assistants)). For a science department with lab technicians and TAs shared across a EHCP-heavy cohort, this is the department's single highest-stakes deployment call.

## Design implications for our department layer

1. **Every teacher gets weekly, not occasional, coaching — including experienced staff, not just ECTs.** Follow Dixons: coaching replaces appraisal as the primary mechanism of teacher development, run inside the department by the HoD or a trained lead practitioner, not by a separate central CPD function. In science, the HoD (Adam, TLR3) is the default coach for KS3, with department second-in-charge or a lead practitioner picking up overflow so no coach carries more than ~6–8 teachers.
2. **One granular action step per teacher per week, drawn from a shared bank, not invented fresh each time.** Mirror Steplab's model: maintain a departmental action-step library (e.g. cold-calling before reveal, checking for understanding via mini-whiteboards, retrieval starters) so coaching conversations are consistent, low-preparation for the coach, and cumulative — this term's step builds on last term's, rather than restarting the theme each cycle.
3. **Fix the coaching slot in the timetable, protected from admin creep.** Name a specific weekly slot (e.g. Tuesday after school, following Steplab's common pattern) for feedback conversations, separate from the general department meeting. This is the binding constraint the evidence flags — coaching that depends on "whenever there's time" does not survive a hard term.
4. **Repurpose the department meeting itself around teaching, not logistics.** Following Michaela's principle of stripping admin from teacher-facing time: centralise behaviour/detention admin, resourcing, and data entry outside the department meeting (school-level systems, not HoD time), so the weekly or fortnightly meeting is spent on shared misconceptions from the coaching cycle, moderated work samples, and curriculum sequencing — the department's version of Rosenshine/EEF-aligned teaching techniques applied to that week's actual lessons.
5. **QA is ungraded, developmental, and frequent — deliberately mirroring the coaching logic, not a snapshot judgement.** Learning walks and work scrutiny by the HoD should follow Ofsted's own 2025 shift: look for "what is typical," feed findings straight back into the coaching action-step bank, and never produce a single graded verdict on a lesson. This closes the historic gap where internal QA modelled high-stakes inspection rather than the developmental practice we actually want teachers using.
6. **ECT and struggling-teacher support is a staffing decision before it's a training-content decision.** The ITTECF gives content; it does not give time. Timetable mentors with a real reduction elsewhere (not added on top), assign the department's strongest available coach — not necessarily the most senior teacher — to ECTs and any teacher flagged through QA as struggling, and treat "protected mentor time actually happening" as the department's leading indicator, since NFER's evaluation shows the national entitlement alone does not move retention.
7. **TA and technician deployment is planned by the department, against the EEF's rules, not left to informal classroom habit.** In science specifically: technicians are timetabled for practical prep/H&S, not folded into pupil-facing TA roles; TAs are deployed to scaffold the lowest-attaining pupils toward independence across the group (never permanently "velcroed" to one EHCP pupil), and any TA-led intervention (e.g. pre-teaching key vocabulary before a practical) is scripted and trained by the HoD, not improvised — the difference the EEF shows between a measurable positive effect and a negative one.
8. **Treat effect-size claims honestly with staff.** Communicate coaching as "our best bet, well-implemented" rather than "proven to add months of progress" — Sims et al.'s near-zero preregistered PD effect and Kraft's at-scale shrinkage are real, and overclaiming to teachers erodes the buy-in the mechanism depends on.
