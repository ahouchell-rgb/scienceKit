# Curriculum design for disadvantaged learners

Research brief 02 — Perfect School project. Focus: knowledge-rich curriculum, mastery, cognitive science, reading and oracy, cultural capital, and the 2025 Curriculum & Assessment Review (CAR). Written July 2026; policy status verified against post-2024 sources.

## What the evidence says

**Knowledge-rich curriculum.** The strongest causal evidence yet is Grissmer et al.'s (2023) kindergarten-lottery evaluation of Core Knowledge charter schools: a sustained, content-rich curriculum produced reading gains of 0.24 SD (intent-to-treat) across grades 3–6 — roughly 16 percentile points — with a *much larger* effect (ITT ≈ 0.94 SD) in the one school serving a low-income community ([EdWorkingPapers 23-755](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai23-755); [Hechinger Report critique](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-inside-the-latest-reading-study-that-everyone-is-talking-about/)). Caveats: it is one US study, mostly middle-income schools, and the low-income result rests on a single school — treat the direction as solid, the magnitude as uncertain. The mechanism is well-established: reading comprehension depends heavily on background knowledge and vocabulary, so a coherent, cumulative curriculum is itself a literacy intervention — the argument Ofsted's curriculum research and E.D. Hirsch both make.

**Mastery learning.** The EEF Toolkit estimates [+5 months](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/mastery-learning) average progress, with larger effects for lower attainers, at very low cost. Honest flag: the underlying evidence is dated and variable (some studies show no or negative effects when implemented badly), and England's own RCTs of Singapore-style "Mathematics Mastery" found small effects (~+1 month, not statistically significant). Mastery works when the core loop is real — frequent low-stakes checks, rapid corrective teaching, and not moving on before ~80% mastery — not when it is a scheme-of-work label.

**Cognitive science (retrieval, spacing, interleaving, worked examples).** Laboratory evidence is exceptionally strong: Dunlosky et al. (2013) rate practice testing and distributed practice as the two "high utility" techniques ([Psychological Science in the Public Interest](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266)); Adesope et al.'s meta-analysis puts retrieval practice at g ≈ 0.50–0.61 ([Review of Educational Research](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654316689306)). But the [EEF's 2021 applied review](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/evidence-reviews/cognitive-science-approaches-in-the-classroom) is the key honest corrective: in realistic classrooms effects are smaller and more mixed, and of 21 retrieval-practice classroom studies only one used a regular class teacher. Implication: adopt these techniques (they are cheap and directionally right), but invest in *fidelity and teacher understanding*, not slogans. [Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction](https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/Rosenshine.pdf) — daily/weekly/monthly review, small steps, worked examples then faded practice, high success rate — remain the best practitioner synthesis, and explicit instruction is also the first of the EEF's "[five-a-day](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/send)" strategies for SEND learners in mainstream classrooms, which matters for our catchment.

**Reading and oracy across the curriculum.** EEF Toolkit: [reading comprehension strategies +6 months](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/reading-comprehension-strategies), [oral language interventions +6 months](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/oral-language-interventions), [metacognition +7 months](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/metacognition-and-self-regulation) (revised to +8 in the 2025 update) — all comparatively high-security strands. The EEF's [Improving Literacy in Secondary Schools](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/literacy-ks3-ks4) guidance puts *disciplinary literacy* first: every subject explicitly teaches its own reading, writing, vocabulary and talk. The [Oracy Education Commission's "We Need to Talk" (Oct 2024)](https://oracyeducationcommission.co.uk/oec-report/) argues oracy should be the "fourth R" and an entitlement through all phases; its evidence base is more expert-consensus than trial data, but it converges with the RCT-backed oral-language strand.

**Cultural capital: argument and critique.** The pro case (Hirsch, Dixons, Ofsted) is that shared "powerful knowledge" is a lever for equity — disadvantaged children depend on school for what advantaged homes provide by default. The academic critique is serious: Ofsted's usage inverts Bourdieu (whose concept explains how schools *reproduce* privilege), risks a deficit view of working-class and minority cultures, and can collapse into an elitist canon checklist ([Nightingale 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1757743820961417); [British Journal of Sociology of Education](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01425692.2024.2325542)). The design resolution: keep the entitlement to powerful knowledge, but curate it to include students' own communities and treat trips/arts/enrichment as curriculum, not decoration — which is exactly where national policy has landed (see below).

**The 2025 Curriculum & Assessment Review.** Professor Becky Francis's [final report (5 Nov 2025)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report) recommends, and the [government response](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report-government-response) broadly accepts: scrapping the EBacc performance measures (with a reformed Progress 8/Attainment 8); cutting KS4 exam time by ~10%; a national **oracy framework** alongside reading and writing; a statutory diagnostic **Year 8 reading test** (fluency + comprehension; school-level results unpublished — the response declined statutory Year 8 maths/English tests, but "expects" schools to assess writing and maths in Year 8 using high-quality products; see research/08); a **core enrichment entitlement** for all pupils; stepping-stone Level 1 maths/English qualifications; better assessment accessibility for SEND; stronger diversity across programmes of study; and a "V-level" third 16–19 pathway ([summary](https://www.riseedumag.com/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report-summary/)). The new national curriculum is due **spring 2027, first teaching September 2028**, and — via s.54 of the [Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/contents) — **academies must teach it too** ([Ward Hadaway analysis](https://www.wardhadaway.com/insights/education-law-digest/childrens-wellbeing-and-schools-act-2026-changes-to-academies/)). Ofsted's [renewed framework (from 10 Nov 2025)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/education-inspection-framework/education-inspection-framework-for-use-from-november-2025) replaces single-word judgements with report cards graded on a 5-point scale across six areas including *inclusion* and *curriculum and teaching*, with explicit emphasis on a broad, rich curriculum for SEND and disadvantaged pupils, and no more subject deep dives.

## What the best operators actually do

- **Michaela Community School (Brent)**: highly disadvantaged intake; knowledge-rich curriculum, whole-class explicit instruction, daily retrieval ("recap starters"), silent corridors. Progress 8 of **+2.55 in 2024 — highest in England for the third consecutive year** ([school results page](https://www.michaela.education/about-us/exam-results); [DfE performance data](https://www.compare-school-performance.service.gov.uk/school/140862/michaela-community-school/secondary)). Contested culturally, but the results are real.
- **Star Academies**: tops MAT performance tables; Eden schools posted disadvantaged-pupil P8 of **+0.89 to +1.62 in 2024** ([Star Academies](https://staracademies.org/news-story/star-academies-tops-mat-performance-tables/); [disadvantaged P8 rankings](https://curriculum.substack.com/p/top-93-schools-for-disadvantaged)). Model: high-expectations culture, strong personal development/character offer rooted in community identity — a counterexample to the claim that knowledge-rich must mean culturally alienating.
- **Dixons Academies Trust** (Bradford/Leeds/Liverpool/Manchester): trust-wide [curriculum principles](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/curriculumprinciples) explicitly built on "powerful knowledge" to "narrow the gap between demographic groups", combined with mastery-style assessment and cultural enrichment for disadvantaged and SEND pupils.
- **Ark / United Learning / Oak**: centrally-built, fully-resourced curricula (Ark Curriculum Plus's Mathematics Mastery and English Mastery) that reduce teacher workload and variance — the trial effects are modest (~+1 month), but the delivery model (shared booklets, centrally-written retrieval quizzes) is what makes cognitive-science routines survive contact with real staffing.
- **Voice 21 / School 21**: oracy as a planned curriculum strand (talk protocols, debate, presentational talk) in [1,000+ schools](https://voice21.org/oracy-education-commission-report/), disproportionately serving disadvantaged communities.

Common operator playbook: one coherent curriculum spine, booklets/knowledge organisers, daily review routines, whole-class reading of demanding texts, centralised assessment with reteach loops, and enrichment treated as entitlement.

## Numbers that matter

- Disadvantaged pupils were **19.0 months behind** peers at GCSE in 2025 — the joint-widest gap since 2013 ([EPI Annual Report 2025](https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2025-disadvantage/)); **44% of that gap is locked in by age 11** ([EEF/EPI](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/disadvantage-gap-locked-in-new-research-shows)); equalising absence alone would cut ~4 months of the KS4 gap ([EPI](https://epi.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Breaking-down-the-gap.pdf)). Curriculum cannot fix what attendance destroys — design them together.
- 2025 GCSE: **26% of disadvantaged pupils vs 53% of others** achieved grade 5+ in English and maths ([EPI](https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2025/)).
- EEF Toolkit months-progress (cheap, whole-school levers): metacognition +7/+8; oral language +6; reading comprehension strategies +6; mastery learning +5; retrieval/spacing directionally positive but classroom evidence thin ([EEF Toolkit](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit); [EEF cog-sci review](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/evidence-reviews/cognitive-science-approaches-in-the-classroom)).
- Core Knowledge lottery study: **+0.24 SD reading, ≈16 percentile points** over grades 3–6 ([Grissmer et al. 2023](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai23-755)).
- Policy clock: new national curriculum **spring 2027 → first teaching Sept 2028**; oracy framework and reformed accountability incoming; academy NC freedom ends under the 2026 Act.

## Design implications for our school

1. **Build one knowledge-rich, cumulative curriculum spine 2–16**, specified at the level of core knowledge and vocabulary per unit, and audit it against the incoming 2028 national curriculum from day one (we will be statutorily bound to it as an academy).
2. **Make mastery the assessment architecture, not a slogan**: low-stakes checks every lesson, weekly cumulative quizzes, defined mastery thresholds with built-in reteach time in the timetable — prioritised in maths and early reading where low attainers gain most.
3. **Institutionalise cognitive science through materials, not exhortation**: centrally-written retrieval starters, spaced revisit maps in schemes of work, worked-example-then-fade task design (this also serves the large SEND cohort — explicit instruction and scaffolding are the EEF's core SEND strategies).
4. **Literacy and oracy in every subject**: disciplinary literacy per EEF guidance, whole-class reading of challenging texts, explicit tier-2/3 vocabulary teaching, and a planned oracy curriculum (Voice 21-style protocols) ahead of the national oracy framework.
5. **Enrichment as entitlement**: a guaranteed pledge of trips, instruments, performance, debate and clubs — framed as broadening cultural capital *and* valuing the community's own culture, pre-empting both the CAR enrichment entitlement and the Bourdieu critique.
6. **Plan for the statutory Year 8 reading test (and expected non-statutory Year 8 maths/writing assessment) and Ofsted report cards**: KS3 must be rigorous, not a holding pen; inclusion and curriculum breadth are now separately graded inspection areas.
7. **Be honest about limits**: curriculum design is necessary but not sufficient — attendance, early language, and SEND identification move the gap at least as much as pedagogy.
