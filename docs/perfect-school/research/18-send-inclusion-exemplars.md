# SEND inclusion exemplars: English mainstream schools and trusts combining high disadvantage with exceptional inclusion

*Research brief 18 — for the design of a UK secondary/all-through school serving a deprived, high-SEND catchment. Compiled July 2026.*

## What the evidence says

**High-quality, adaptive whole-class teaching is the foundation.** The EEF's *Special Educational Needs in Mainstream Schools* guidance report concludes that "to a great extent, good teaching for pupils with SEND is good teaching for all", and distils the evidence into the "Five-a-day" repertoire every teacher should use daily: explicit instruction, cognitive and metacognitive strategies, scaffolding, flexible grouping, and technology ([EEF guidance report](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/send); [Five-a-day poster](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/eef-guidance-reports/send/Five-a-day-poster_1.1.pdf)). The same guidance stresses building an inclusive culture led by senior leaders, the graduated assess–plan–do–review cycle, and ensuring teaching assistants **supplement, not replace,** the teacher. TAs deployed to deliver structured, time-limited interventions add about **+4 months** progress, whereas informal "velcro" TA support can depress outcomes ([EEF toolkit: TA interventions](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/teaching-assistant-interventions)).

**Resourced provision works when it is a bridge, not a silo.** Peer-reviewed work on resourced provisions (RPs) in mainstream schools finds they can operate either as an "inclusive service" or a segregating "safe space", depending on whether pupils genuinely access mainstream lessons and whether expertise flows out into classrooms ([Strogilos et al., *JORSEN* 2024](https://nasenjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1471-3802.12622)). Recent research on specialist resource centres for autistic pupils similarly reports improved experience of mainstream school where centres are well integrated ([Cook & Boddy, *Autism* 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13623613261426099)).

**Inclusion is unevenly distributed — and inclusive schools in poor areas carry the load.** NFER's Nuffield-funded analysis shows schools with the highest EHCP concentrations have **up to six times** as many EHCP pupils as the lowest, that **56% of EHCP pupils were in mainstream in 2024/25** (up from 49% in 2015/16), and that high-SEND schools disproportionately serve disadvantaged communities ([NFER 2025](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/high-send-schools-understanding-the-uneven-distribution-of-pupils-with-send-across-england-s-mainstream-schools/)). IPPR/The Difference's *Who is Losing Learning?* work links this to the exclusions crisis: 11.5 million days of learning were lost to suspension and absence in autumn 2023/24 (+67% on pre-pandemic), concentrated among poor and SEND pupils ([IPPR solutions report, March 2025](https://the-difference.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/FINAL-Who-is-Losing-Learning-Solutions-Report.pdf)).

**The 2025–26 policy landscape now pushes hard toward mainstream inclusion.** The Schools White Paper *Every child achieving and thriving* (Feb 2026) proposes a tiered support model (universal / targeted / targeted-plus / specialist), digital Individual Support Plans, and an expectation that **every secondary school will in time host an "inclusion base"**, backed by **£1.6bn over three years from 2026–27**, including the new Inclusive Mainstream Fund ([Commons Library briefing](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10550/); [Schools Week summary](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/schools-white-paper-the-key-send-reform-policies/); [IMF guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-mainstream-fund-2026-to-2027/inclusive-mainstream-fund-best-practice-for-schools--2)). Ofsted's renewed framework and report cards include a stronger inclusion focus ([IQM explainer](https://iqmaward.com/blogs/ofsted-2025-what-the-new-framework-means-for-inclusion-and-send/)). A school designed now should assume it will be inspected on inclusion and funded to build internal specialist capacity.

*Honesty flag:* the exemplar evidence below is largely self-reported case-study material (DfE's Inclusion in Practice project, The Difference's own evaluations) rather than controlled trials; treat effect claims as promising, not proven.

## What the best operators actually do

**1. Carr Manor Community School, Leeds (all-through, 4–19).** Graded **Outstanding in all five areas in 2025**, with inspectors highlighting its relational "coaching" model — small, mixed-age coaching groups that check in and out with the same trusted adult so every child is known ([school report page](https://carrmanor.org.uk/2025/03/10/ofsted-inspection-2025/); [Ofsted report](https://files.ofsted.gov.uk/v1/file/50271457)). Its **Mosaic resourced provision (12 places)** serves autistic pupils with complex communication needs: personalised timetables with **~50% of lessons in mainstream classes**, low-arousal environments, and autism-specific intervention alongside age-related classes ([Leeds Local Offer](https://leedslocaloffer.org.uk/education/specialist-send-places-and-settings/resourced-provision/carr-manor-community-school-4-19)). This is the strongest single template for an all-through inclusive design: relational universal tier + small RP with high mainstream access.

**2. Lister Community School, Newham (secondary, very high deprivation, The Difference partner).** Hosts Newham's **24-place resourced provision for deaf pupils, all with EHCPs**, who follow the full mainstream curriculum, supported by a specialist Deaf Support Department and integrated socially — praised in its October 2024 Ofsted monitoring ([school inclusion page](https://lister.ncltrust.net/curriculum/inclusion/)). Layered above universal provision are a **Student Support Centre** — a time-limited **six-week** placement triggered by live behaviour-points, internal-exclusion and attendance data, with reintegration hinging on communicating what staff learned about the child to all their teachers — and a full-time **internal alternative provision** on site (case study 3 in [Who is Losing Learning? Solutions](https://the-difference.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/FINAL-Who-is-Losing-Learning-Solutions-Report.pdf)).

**3. The Academy at Shotton Hall, Peterlee, Co. Durham (secondary, ex-mining town, North East Learning Trust).** Ofsted Outstanding; a DfE **Inclusion in Practice** case study on adaptive teaching and teacher-led interventions ([case-study list](https://inclusioninpractice.org.uk/casestudies/)). Cited in DfE inclusive-mainstream best-practice material for a distinctive staffing choice: **increasing subject-teacher capacity in core subjects so targeted intervention is delivered by qualified subject specialists rather than TAs**, embedded in a graduated assess–plan–do–review cycle; **Progress 8 of 0.58 in 2024, with disadvantaged pupils progressing faster than local and national peers** ([GOV.UK IMF best practice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-mainstream-fund-2026-to-2027/inclusive-mainstream-fund-best-practice-for-schools--2)).

**4. Ormiston Academies Trust (35,000+ pupils; CEO Tom Rees chairs the DfE inclusion expert group).** Co-created **Specialist Internal Provisions (SIPs)** across six primaries (Cliff Park, Edward Worlledge, Herman, Meadows, Packmoor, South Parade): each SIP has a **dedicated teacher lead**, explicit **entry and exit criteria**, personalised pathways, and a design principle that SIPs are "a stepping stone, not a final destination", with mainstream classrooms restructured to receive pupils back; early data shows improved behaviour, attendance and engagement ([OAT case study PDF](https://inclusioninpractice.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Ormiston_Case_Study-vF.pdf)). This is the closest existing operational blueprint for the white paper's "inclusion bases".

**5. Two Counties Trust / Heritage High School (Derbyshire/Notts) — inclusion led from the top.** All senior leaders completed The Difference's Inclusive Leadership Course; within a year Heritage High **halved suspensions**, cut persistent absence by 4 points and raised attendance 1.75 points, with trust-wide gains largest for Pupil Premium students ([Who is Losing Learning? Solutions, case study 4](https://the-difference.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/FINAL-Who-is-Losing-Learning-Solutions-Report.pdf)). Across The Difference's programmes, **58% of school leaders report reduced formal exclusions within the first year** ([The Difference](https://the-difference.com/programmes/for-schools/)). Related trust practice worth copying: Ted Wragg Trust's weekly **lost-learning data dashboards** (same report), and **Manchester Communication Academy** (Harpurhey; ~**68% disadvantaged**) which pairs evidence-informed teaching with a dedicated **family partnership and social investment team** ([MCA](https://www.manchestercommunicationacademy.com/about-us); [Parliamentary Review](https://www.theparliamentaryreview.co.uk/organisations/manchester-communication-academy)). For autism-specific external QA, the NAS **Cullum Centre** model (five resource bases in mainstream secondaries, each working to NAS Autism Accreditation, majority of lessons in mainstream) is the benchmark ([NAS Cullum Centres](https://www.autism.org.uk/what-we-do/education-and-children-services/cullum-centres); [Autism Accreditation](https://www.autism.org.uk/what-we-do/autism-know-how/autism-accreditation)).

## Numbers that matter

- **+4 months**: average impact of TA-delivered structured interventions; near zero/negative for unstructured TA deployment ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/teaching-assistant-interventions)).
- **RP scale**: 12 places (Carr Manor Mosaic) to 24 places (Lister deaf provision); Carr Manor pupils spend **~50% of time in mainstream lessons**.
- **Funding**: RP/SEN-unit places attract **£10,000 per place** plus top-up under high-needs funding ([2025–26 operational guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/high-needs-funding-arrangements-2025-to-2026/high-needs-funding-2025-to-2026-operational-guide)); high-needs spending is now **~£12bn, up 66% since 2016** ([IFS](https://ifs.org.uk/publications/support-children-disabilities-and-special-educational-needs)); **£1.6bn** new inclusion funding over three years from 2026–27.
- **Need**: **638,700 EHCPs (Jan 2025, +10.8% y-o-y)**; **19.6% of pupils have identified SEN** ([Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/sn07020/); [DfE statistics](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/education-health-and-care-plans/2025)). Expect materially higher shares in a deprived catchment — high-SEND schools skew disadvantaged and can hold 6x the EHCP load of neighbours ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/high-send-schools-understanding-the-uneven-distribution-of-pupils-with-send-across-england-s-mainstream-schools/)).
- **Outcomes attainable**: Shotton Hall P8 **+0.58** with above-average disadvantage; Heritage High **suspensions −50%** in one year; Haringey Learning Partnership (AP comparator) gets **40% English & maths GCSE passes vs 4% AP national average** and has reintegrated **348 pupils to mainstream since 2020** using an on-site multidisciplinary team (SaLT, EP, counsellor, social worker, mentors) ([Who is Losing Learning? Solutions](https://the-difference.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/FINAL-Who-is-Losing-Learning-Solutions-Report.pdf)).
- *Caveat*: school-level figures above are single-cohort, non-experimental; the EEF effect sizes are the only causally robust numbers here.

## Design implications for our school

1. **Build the universal tier first**: train every teacher in the EEF Five-a-day and adaptive teaching; make inclusion a classroom-teaching issue, not a SEND-department issue.
2. **Prefer qualified-teacher-led intervention** (Shotton Hall model: extra core-subject staffing) over large TA teams; reserve TAs for structured, evidence-based programmes.
3. **Design in 2–3 resourced provisions/inclusion bases from day one** (12–26 places each, e.g. autism/communication + SEMH), with written entry/exit criteria, a dedicated teacher lead each, and a guarantee of ≥50% mainstream lesson time (Carr Manor/Ormiston SIP model) — this also matches the white paper's funded direction of travel.
4. **Create a time-limited (≈6-week) internal support centre** with data-triggered referral and a formal mechanism for feeding what is learned back to every classroom teacher (Lister model), plus small on-site internal AP as the step above.
5. **Relational architecture at scale**: mixed-age coaching groups/daily check-ins so every child has a known trusted adult (Carr Manor), and universal belonging routines (family lunch, Heritage High).
6. **Put multidisciplinary specialists on site** — speech and language therapy, educational psychology, counselling, mentoring — to remove waiting-time cliff edges (HLP model), and a family partnership team for a deprived catchment (MCA model).
7. **Lead inclusion from the top**: SLT-wide inclusion training (The Difference Inclusive Leadership) and weekly lost-learning dashboards spanning attendance, removals, suspensions and SEND cohorts (Ted Wragg model).
8. **Seek external accreditation** (NAS Autism Accreditation / IQM) as ongoing QA, and plan finances for being a "magnet" high-SEND school in an unevenly distributed local system.
