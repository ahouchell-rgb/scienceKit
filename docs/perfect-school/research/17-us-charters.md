# Case study: High-performing US charter networks serving poverty — KIPP, Success Academy, Uncommon Schools

*Research brief 17 — US charter evidence and its lessons for an English school in a high-deprivation, high-SEND catchment. Drafted July 2026.*

## What the evidence says

The strongest causal evidence on "No Excuses"-style urban charters comes from **admissions-lottery studies**, which compare lottery winners and losers and therefore control for family motivation and prior attainment — the classic selection critique.

- **KIPP (Knowledge Is Power Program)** — the largest US network (~280 schools). Mathematica's multi-site lottery evaluation of 43 KIPP middle schools found impacts of **+0.36 SD in maths and +0.21 SD in reading after three years** — roughly 90% of an extra year of learning in maths and two-thirds of a year in reading ([Mathematica KIPP middle schools report](https://www.mathematica.org/publications/kipp-middle-schools-impacts-on-achievement-and-other-outcomes-full-report); [scale-up study PDF](https://www.mathematica.org/-/media/publications/pdfs/education/kipp_scale-up_vol1.pdf)).
- **Boston "No Excuses" charters** — Abdulkadiroğlu, Angrist, Dynarski, Kane & Pathak's lottery studies found each year in a charter middle school added roughly **0.2 SD in ELA and 0.3–0.4 SD in maths**, with high-school gains as large; the authors describe effects big enough to close Black–white attainment gaps within a school career, plus improvements in longer-run outcomes such as four-year college attendance ([NBER w15549](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w15549/w15549.pdf); [Blueprint Labs summary](https://seii.mit.edu/research/study/how-effective-are-boston-charter-and-pilot-schools); [CEPR/VoxEU on replication](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/replicating-successful-charter-schools-boston)).
- **Success Academy (New York, ~50 schools)** — MDRC's lottery study of 4,710 kindergarten/Y1 applicants found winners gained **about 1–1.5 years of additional learning in maths by grades 3–4**; reading effects positive but imprecise ([MDRC](https://www.mdrc.org/work/publications/early-look-effects-success-academy-charter-schools); [Chalkbeat summary](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2017/8/29/21101021/winning-the-lottery-enrolling-in-success-academy-produces-math-gains-for-students-new-study-shows/)). MDRC called these effects "towards the top of what's been estimated for charter schools."
- **Uncommon Schools (~50 schools, NY/NJ/MA)** — Mathematica's 2022 federal evaluation found large, persistent maths and ELA gains in **Newark** (propensity-score design; cumulative maths impact after three years ≈ the entire poverty test-score gap) but **no discernible effects in the NYC lottery sample** — a useful reminder that network effects are heterogeneous and depend on the counterfactual schools ([Mathematica/ERIC ED628817](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED628817.pdf)).
- **Long-run outcomes are more mixed.** Mathematica's 15-year follow-up (2023) found KIPP **middle school alone had ~zero effect on degree completion** (22% for both lottery winners and losers), but attending **KIPP middle + high school** was associated with four-year degree completion of **39% vs 20% (+19pp)** — though that combined estimate is exploratory, not lottery-randomised, so treat it as promising rather than proven ([Mathematica](https://www.mathematica.org/publications/long-term-impacts-of-kipp-middle-and-high-schools-on-college-enrollment-persistence-and-attainment); [Chalkbeat analysis](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2023/10/12/23914799/kipp-research-study-middle-high-school-graduation-college/)).
- **What explains effectiveness:** Dobbie & Fryer's landmark NYC study found traditional inputs (class size, per-pupil spend, teacher certification) were **uncorrelated** with charter effectiveness, while an index of five practices — frequent teacher feedback, data-driven instruction, high-dosage tutoring, increased instructional time, high expectations — explained **~45% of the variation** in school effectiveness ([AEJ: Applied 2013](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.5.4.28); [working paper PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/fryer/files/dobbie_fryer_revision_final.pdf)).
- **The practices transfer.** Fryer's Houston "Apollo 20" experiment injected the five-practice bundle into 20 failing district schools: **+0.15–0.18 SD/year in maths** (little effect on reading) — comparable to attending a high-performing charter — at ~**$2,000 per student per year** ([QJE 2014](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/129/3/1355/1817328); [NBER w17494](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17494); [PBS Frontline on costs](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/frontline/article/apollo-20-one-mans-plan-to-fix-failing-schools/)).

**Honest caveats.** Lottery studies only generalise to applicants of oversubscribed schools; effects are concentrated in maths and in urban, high-poverty settings; reading effects are consistently smaller; and CREDO-style matched studies show wide within-sector variation ([CREDO NCSS3 2023](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/credo-at-stanford-university-finds-that-students-in-charter-schools-on-average-perform-better-academically-than-those-attending-traditional-public-schools-301843486.html); [Hechinger summary](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-charter-schools-have-improved-in-the-past-15-years-but-many-still-fail-students-researchers-say/)).

## What the best operators actually do

1. **More time.** KIPP's classic model runs ~9-hour days plus Saturday/summer sessions; Apollo 20 added ~10 school days and an extra hour, four days a week ([Mathematica scale-up](https://www.mathematica.org/-/media/publications/pdfs/education/kipp_scale-up_vol1.pdf); [EdWeek on Apollo](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/houston-schools-take-a-page-from-best-charters/2012/03)).
2. **High-dosage tutoring built into the timetable.** Apollo gave every Y7-equivalent and Y10-equivalent student **70 minutes/day of 1:2 maths tutoring as a scheduled period**, not an after-school add-on ([NBER w17494](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17494/w17494.pdf)).
3. **Data-driven instruction.** Uncommon (Bambrick-Santoyo's "Driven by Data" model) runs 4–6-weekly interim assessments with mandatory analysis meetings and scheduled reteach weeks; this is a core element of the Dobbie–Fryer index ([Uncommon evaluation](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED628817.pdf); [Dobbie & Fryer](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/fryer/files/dobbie_fryer_revision_final.pdf)).
4. **Frequent teacher observation and feedback.** Effective NYC charters gave teachers ~10+ informal observations with feedback per term; Uncommon/Success run weekly coaching cycles and heavy in-house teacher development (Teach Like a Champion originates at Uncommon).
5. **Culture of high expectations** — explicit college-for-all norms, sweat-the-small-stuff behaviour systems, uniform routines, and (at Success Academy) tightly scripted curriculum and daily reading blocks.

### The attrition and discipline critiques

- **Discipline:** Success Academy suspends at far above NYC norms (one school at 25% vs ~3% state average) and a principal's 2015 **"Got to Go" list** of disruptive pupils became a national scandal — 9 of 16 listed children left ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/n-y-c-charter-network-under-scrutiny-for-got-to-go-list-ceo-fights-back/2015/10); [Yale Education Studies review](https://campuspress.yale.edu/edstudiescourses/new-yorks-success-academy-charter-schools-a-tentative-success/)).
- **Attrition/backfill:** Miron (2011) found **40% of Black male pupils left KIPP middle schools between grades 6–8** and KIPP backfilled departing pupils at lower rates than districts; Success Academy stops backfilling after grade 4, with attrition estimated ~17%/yr thereafter ([Miron/NCSPE](https://ncspe.tc.columbia.edu/working-papers/files/OP195_3.pdf); [EdWeek on attrition](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/study-stings-kipp-on-attrition-rates/2011/04)). Mathematica counters that its intention-to-treat lottery estimates keep leavers in the treatment group, so headline effects are not simply attrition artefacts ([EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/leadership/kipp-outpacing-regular-public-schools-study-finds/2013/03)) — but peer-composition benefits from selective attrition and non-backfill remain a live, partly unresolved critique, and SEND/EAL pupils are typically under-represented relative to host districts.

## Design implications for our school

The English 2025–26 context sharpens both the lessons and the cautions: Ofsted's new report cards (from 10 Nov 2025) include a **standalone "inclusion" evaluation area** scrutinising provision for disadvantaged and SEND pupils ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ofsted-confirms-changes-to-education-inspection-and-unveils-new-look-report-cards)); England's suspensions hit a record **955,000 in 2023/24**, falling disproportionately on FSM pupils ([DfE statistics](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions-in-england/2023-24)); the **Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act (Royal Assent 29 April 2026)** aligns academies more closely with maintained schools ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/childrens-wellbeing-and-schools-act-the-policies-signed-into-law/)); and the free-schools pipeline has been largely cut, with ~£600m redirected to SEND places in mainstream schools ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/nightmare-before-christmas-46-free-school-projects-scrapped-and-58-special-schools-in-limbo/)).

1. **Import the five tenets, not the discipline brand.** The replicable, causally supported core is time + tutoring + data + coaching + expectations (Fryer's bundle worked in ordinary district schools). A US-style suspension-heavy regime is legally and reputationally untenable in England — and now directly graded against us via the inclusion judgment.
2. **Timetable high-dosage tutoring** (1:2 or 1:3, 3–5 short sessions/week, tied to the curriculum) as a scheduled period for Y7 and KS4 catch-up cohorts, prioritising maths. Budget honestly: Apollo's ~$2,000/pupil ≈ £1,500–1,700/pupil — plan Pupil Premium and recovery funding around it.
3. **Extend time strategically, not maximally**: a 30–60-minute longer core day plus holiday/Saturday academies for target groups gets most of the KIPP time dividend without unaffordable staffing costs; expect maths to respond more than reading.
4. **Build the Uncommon-style data engine**: 6-weekly standardised interim assessments, structured reteach weeks, and department data meetings with named actions per pupil.
5. **Invest in weekly instructional coaching** (frequent low-stakes observation + feedback), which Dobbie–Fryer rank alongside tutoring and beats spending on smaller classes.
6. **Commit to backfill and inclusion by design** — take in-year admissions, publish attrition and suspension data, and set internal alarms on FSM/SEND/ethnicity gaps in exits, so we get the gains without replicating the "got to go" failure mode (off-rolling is the direct English analogue and an Ofsted red flag).
7. **Plan all-through or guaranteed 11–18 continuity**: KIPP's long-run degree effects appeared only for pupils who stayed through high school; a middle-phase boost alone faded by college completion.
