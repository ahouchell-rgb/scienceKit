# Early Reading & Numeracy Catch-up at Secondary Transfer (Year 7)

Research brief 08 — designing for a high-deprivation, high-SEND catchment. Evidence reviewed July 2026; policy status verified against the post-2024 landscape (Curriculum & Assessment Review, end of the National Tutoring Programme).

## What the evidence says

**The scale of the problem.** In 2025 KS2 SATs, 75% of pupils met the expected standard in reading and 74% in maths — so roughly a quarter of each Year 7 cohort nationally arrives below age expectation, and the proportion will be far higher in a deprived catchment ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/key-stage-2-sats-2025-62-meet-expected-standard-as-results-inch-up/); [DfE statistics](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/key-stage-2-attainment/2024-25-revised)). Ofsted's 2022 research report on struggling readers notes that fewer than one in five pupils who miss the expected standard at KS2 go on to a grade 4 in GCSE English ([Ofsted, "Now the whole school is reading"](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/now-the-whole-school-is-reading-supporting-struggling-readers-in-secondary-school/now-the-whole-school-is-reading-supporting-struggling-readers-in-secondary-school)). Doing nothing is a decision to fail these pupils.

**Diagnose before you intervene.** The consistent message across EEF and DfE guidance is that "poor reader" is not a diagnosis. The EEF's *Improving Literacy in Secondary Schools* guidance (Recommendation 7) calls for standardised group screening on entry, followed by individual diagnostic assessment to distinguish decoding, fluency, vocabulary and comprehension problems, then tiered intervention matched to the deficit ([EEF guidance report](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/literacy-ks3-ks4)). The DfE Reading Framework (updated 2023 to cover up to Year 9) says every KS3 teacher must know which pupils have poor decoding, that standardised tests are only a "first step", and that pupils with reading ages of ~9 and below need individual diagnostic assessment of grapheme-phoneme knowledge and words-per-minute fluency (~90 wpm is the fluency threshold at which comprehension becomes possible) ([DfE Reading Framework](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/664f600c05e5fe28788fc437/The_reading_framework_.pdf)).

**Phonics still works at secondary — for the right pupils.** The EEF Toolkit rates phonics at +5 months on average, high security, low cost, but notes fewer studies with older readers and a weaker effect when used as a blanket intervention beyond primary; it remains appropriate specifically where diagnosis shows a decoding deficit ([EEF Toolkit: Phonics](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/phonics)).

**Fluency is the highest-leverage middle tier.** HFL Education's KS3 Reading Fluency Project (modelled repeated reading, echo reading, performance reading, twice weekly for 8 weeks) reports an average of ~18 months' comprehension-age gain in 8 weeks ([HFL impact data](https://www.hfleducation.org/reading-fluency/key-stage-3-ks3-reading-fluency-project/impact-and-outcomes)). Caveat: these are pre-post project data, not an RCT — treat the headline number as inflated but the mechanism as sound; the EEF has co-produced fluency guidance with HFL and backs repeated-reading approaches ([EEF fluency resources](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/reading-house/fluency)).

**Tutoring is the best-evidenced catch-up mechanism in education.** The EEF Toolkit: one-to-one tuition +5 months ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/one-to-one-tuition)); small-group tuition +4 months and more cost-effective ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/small-group-tuition)). The Nickow/Oreopoulos/Quan meta-analysis of 96 RCTs found a pooled effect of ~0.37 SD (revised to ~0.29 SD in the 2024 journal version), with teacher/paraprofessional tutors beating volunteers and in-school beating after-school ([NBER w27476](https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476)). Optimal dosage: ~30 minutes, 3–5×/week, sustained up to ~10 weeks. Note the National Tutoring Programme subsidy ended in August 2024, so tutoring must now be funded from core/pupil premium budgets ([NFER Year 4 evaluation](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/national-tutoring-programme-year-4-evaluation-and-reflections/)) — NFER's evaluations found only small effects at national scale, driven largely by variable dosage and fidelity.

**Programme-level evidence is genuinely mixed — plan for fade-out at scale.** EEF efficacy trials of Year 7 interventions found ~+3 months for Fresh Start (RWI phonics-based catch-up, 2015 trial), Switch-on Reading, and Accelerated Reader (with the largest AR gains for FSM pupils). But the later effectiveness trials at scale found no effect for Switch-on and *minus* two months for Fresh Start (with poor implementation fidelity flagged) ([EEF Fresh Start/RWI evaluation](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/read-write-inc-and-fresh-start); [Switch-on effectiveness trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/switch-on-effectiveness-trial); [Accelerated Reader first trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/accelerated-reader)). Across seven EEF efficacy-to-effectiveness scale-ups, mean effect fell from 0.25 to 0.01 ([EEF scale-up review](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED625479.pdf)). The lesson is that implementation fidelity, not brand choice, is the active ingredient.

**Software: promising but weaker than vendor claims.** Lexia Core5's EEF RCT (57 schools, high security) found +1 month overall but +2 months for FSM pupils and +2 months on decoding — an EEF "Promising Programme" ([EEF Lexia trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/lexia)). Lexia PowerUp (the adolescent product) has a US RCT meeting ESSA Tier 1 ([Lexia](https://www.lexialearning.com/blog/study-finds-lexia-powerup-literacy-5x-more-effective-average-reading-intervention-program)) but no UK EEF result yet. Reading Plus (fluency-focused, now DreamBox) has mainly small, developer-linked UK studies (Derby Research School quasi-experiment showed large relative gains for PP and SEN pupils); an EEF RCT started in 2024–25 ([EEF Reading Plus trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/reading-plus-2024-25-trial); [Daisy Education study](https://www.daisyeducationuk.com/impact/research/article/reading-plus-efficacy-study-in-partnership-with-derby-research-school-full-report)). Treat all adaptive-software claims as Tier-2 supplements, never the core offer.

**Maths catch-up has a thinner evidence base than reading.** Mathematics Mastery (Ark) secondary RCT: +0.06 SD (~1 month) in Year 7, not statistically significant ([EEF evaluation](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/mathematics-mastery-secondary)) — mastery is cheap and cumulative but not a catch-up intervention. Catch Up Numeracy went from +0.21 at efficacy to null at effectiveness, with FSM pupils actually two months *worse* off; the EEF dropped it ([EEF second trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/catch-up-numeracy-2015)). The strongest maths bets are the EEF *Improving Mathematics in KS2 and 3* guidance (diagnostic teaching, manipulatives and representations, explicit teaching of strategies) ([EEF guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/maths-ks-2-3)) plus small-group tutoring, where Nickow et al. found maths tutoring effects are *stronger* in later grades.

**Policy context (verified 2025–26).** The Curriculum & Assessment Review final report and government response (Nov 2025) introduce a statutory diagnostic Year 8 reading test (fluency + comprehension; school-level results unpublished) and national secondary reading/fluency training from January 2026, targeting 75% of secondary literacy leads by end of 2025–26 ([GOV.UK response](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report-government-response); [NFER commentary](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/blogs/year-8-diagnostic-tests-lets-make-them-worthwhile/)). Our design should exceed, not wait for, this floor.

## What the best operators actually do

- **Ofsted's six case-study schools** (all with strong outcomes for weak readers): senior-leader ownership of reading, universal screening on entry followed by individual diagnostics, trained specialist staff delivering discrete phonics/fluency/comprehension pathways, and re-testing to exit pupils from intervention ([Ofsted report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/now-the-whole-school-is-reading-supporting-struggling-readers-in-secondary-school/now-the-whole-school-is-reading-supporting-struggling-readers-in-secondary-school)).
- **Dixons Academies** (high-deprivation, consistently strong P8): NGRT adaptive testing twice yearly to track reading ages; daily 20-minute whole-school "Robust Reading" (tutor reads a challenging text aloud, Y7–Y10) at Dixons City; tiered intervention with Fresh Start for pupils with KS2 scaled scores 80–90 and intensive strand-one support below reading age 8; extra explicit literacy/grammar lessons in Y7–8 at Croxteth ([Dixons City literacy](https://www.dixonsca.com/why/literacy); [Dixons Croxteth](https://www.dixonscr.com/why/literacy-at-dixons-croxteth)).
- **HFL project schools** run the KS3 fluency protocol as a fixed 8-week, twice-weekly cycle with pre/post standardised assessment and trained TAs/teachers ([HFL](https://www.hfleducation.org/reading-fluency/key-stage-3-ks3-reading-fluency-project)).
- Post-NTP, schools sustaining tutoring embed it in the timetable and staff it with their own trained TAs/HLTAs or fixed-price online providers rather than ad-hoc agency tutors ([Third Space Learning survey of practice](https://thirdspacelearning.com/blog/funding-tutoring-after-ntp/)).

## Design implications for our school

1. **Universal screening in the first fortnight of Year 7** (NGRT or equivalent standardised adaptive reading test plus a standardised maths test), repeated at least twice yearly; individual diagnostic follow-up (GPC gaps, wpm fluency, comprehension) for anyone below ~reading age 9–10. This also pre-empts the statutory Year 8 reading test.
2. **A three-tier reading pathway with exit criteria**: Tier 3 daily SSP-based decoding intervention (Fresh Start-type) for non-decoders, delivered by a small number of *specialist-trained* staff with fidelity monitoring — the effectiveness-trial failures were implementation failures; Tier 2 8-week repeated-reading fluency cycles (HFL protocol) for decoders below ~90 wpm; Tier 1 whole-school disciplinary literacy, daily tutor-led reading of challenging texts (Dixons model), and vocabulary teaching.
3. **Timetable the intervention in, don't bolt it on**: in-school tutoring beats after-school in the meta-analytic evidence; protect a daily 20–30 minute slot.
4. **Staff it with permanent, trained TAs/HLTAs as tutors** (1:1–1:3, 3×30 min/week, 10–12-week blocks) rather than agency tutors — cheaper, better fidelity, and the NTP is gone so this must be a core budget line (~£15–25k/year per FTE tutor covering ~40–60 intervention places/year).
5. **Maths: don't buy a branded catch-up scheme.** Run a Y7 mastery curriculum with diagnostic pre-testing of every unit, plus small-group teacher/HLTA tutoring for the bottom quintile targeting specific gaps (place value, fractions, multiplicative reasoning) per EEF KS2–3 maths guidance. Catch Up Numeracy is explicitly not recommended.
6. **Use software (Lexia PowerUp/Core5-type) only as a supervised supplement** — 3×20 min/week minimum dosage, with an adult monitoring — and re-evaluate when the EEF Reading Plus RCT reports.
7. **Measure everything with standardised pre/post tests and exit pupils when they reach threshold** — intervention places are the scarcest resource in a high-SEND school.
8. **SEND interface**: diagnostic assessment doubles as the evidence trail for EHC needs assessment; the reading team should sit inside the SEND/inclusion directorate.
