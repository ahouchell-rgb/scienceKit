# Case study 13: Dixons Academies Trust (Bradford / Leeds / Liverpool / Manchester)

*Researched July 2026. Focus: morning meeting/advisory, culture, instructional coaching at scale, Dixons OpenSource, outcomes in deprived catchments, SEND and inclusion.*

## What the evidence says

**Who they are.** Dixons Academies Trust runs 17 settings — 11 secondaries, 3 primaries, 2 all-through academies and a sixth-form college — across Bradford, Leeds, Liverpool and Manchester, with an explicit mission "to challenge educational and social disadvantage in the North" ([dixonsat.com](https://www.dixonsat.com/); [Inclusion in Practice case study, July 2025](https://inclusioninpractice.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Dixons_Case_Study.pdf)). The trust deliberately opens or takes over schools in the hardest circumstances: its secondary intake is **45% disadvantaged, 20% SEN, 26% EAL** (primary: 34% disadvantaged, 21% SEN, 48% EAL) — roughly double national disadvantage rates.

**Flagship proof point.** Dixons Trinity Academy (Bradford) was the **first secondary free school judged Outstanding by Ofsted (January 2014)** ([Ofsted report](https://files.ofsted.gov.uk/v1/file/2354585)). Its model — daily Morning Meeting, self-determination-theory drivers (mastery, autonomy, purpose), values of hard work/trust/fairness, "climbing the mountain to university" — has since been replicated across the trust and shared free via Dixons OpenSource.

**The underpinning research base is genuinely strong in places.** The trust's twin engines — instructional coaching and culture built on Deci & Ryan's self-determination theory — align with the best causal evidence we have on teacher development. Kraft, Blazar & Hogan's meta-analysis of 60 causal studies found teacher coaching improves instruction by **0.49 SD** and pupil achievement by **0.18 SD** ([Kraft et al. 2018, Review of Educational Research](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/mkraft/files/kraft_blazar_hogan_2018_teacher_coaching.pdf)) — though effects shrink markedly in at-scale programmes (larger programmes ~0.10 SD on achievement) and most studies are US elementary literacy. The EEF's Effective Professional Development guidance (built on the Sims, Fletcher-Wood et al. systematic review) finds PD works best when it combines "mechanisms" for insight, goal-setting, technique and deliberate practice — exactly the recipe Dixons runs ([EEF guidance report](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/effective-professional-development/EEF-Effective-Professional-Development-Guidance-Report.pdf); [Sims et al. 2025, RER](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543231217480)).

**Policy fit, 2025–26.** Ofsted's new report cards (from November 2025) grade **inclusion** as a standalone core area on a five-point scale ([Tes explainer](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/ofsted-report-cards-school-inspection-everything-you-need-know); [IQM analysis](https://iqmaward.com/blogs/ofsted-2025-what-the-new-framework-means-for-inclusion-and-send/)). Dixons' mainstream-first inclusion model is unusually well positioned for this framework — which is why the DfE-adjacent Inclusion in Practice programme chose Dixons as a national case study in July 2025.

## What the best operators actually do

**1. Morning Meeting (their version of advisory).** Every student, Years 7–11, starts every day in Morning Meeting: retrieval practice ("brain dumps" and quizzing of powerful knowledge), literacy and numeracy, an explicit reset of cultural expectations, and **appreciations/recognition** ([Dixons Brooklands](https://www.dixonsbk.com/why/morning-meeting); [Dixons City](https://www.dixonsca.com/why/morning-meeting); [Dixons Unity](https://www.dixonsua.com/why/recognition)). At Dixons Unity every staff member gives one student appreciation weekly, shared in Morning Meeting and pushed to families via app; students arrive by 7:55am; at Trinity, houses lead Friday meetings. It is simultaneously a retrieval engine, a culture ritual and the daily relational touchpoint — replacing woolly "form time."

**2. Culture codified to "an almost impossibly granular degree."** Trust-wide mission and values sit above school-level values (Trinity: hard work, trust, fairness; Unity: integrity, resilience, respect), operationalised through artifacts: induction, briefings, shared language, bulletins, walk-throughs ([dixonsat.com/why/culture](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/culture)). Founding principal (now CEO) Luke Sparkes summarises it as "Clarity, simplicity and rigour. Repeat. Repeat." Motivation is deliberately built on mastery–autonomy–purpose rather than compliance alone. The trust also flattened status hierarchies — it abolished executive job titles ([Tes](https://www.tes.com/magazine/leadership/strategy/why-dixons-academies-trust-has-no-executive-job-titles)).

**3. Coaching instead of appraisal.** Dixons **jettisoned performance appraisal entirely** and gives every member of staff personalised instructional coaching, plus whole-staff deliberate practice of key techniques **twice weekly in staff morning meetings** ([dixonsat.com/why/coaching-and-practice](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/coaching-and-practice)). Coaching runs on Steplab (incremental, low-stakes, technique-level steps), and the trust employs Evidence Leads to keep the model research-honest ([Bradford Research School](https://researchschool.org.uk/bradford/news/making-the-most-of-instructional-coaching)).

**4. SEND: the "Mountain Rescue" model (Nicole Dempsey).** The most distinctive element. Principles ([Ambition Institute blog](https://www.ambition.org.uk/blog/a-mountain-rescue-approach-to-send-inclusion/); [Inclusion in Practice case study](https://inclusioninpractice.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Dixons_Case_Study.pdf)):

- "There's no such thing as children and SEND children — only children." No SEND department (seen as internal segregation); instead one **multidisciplinary pastoral department ("Mountain Rescue")** combining SEND, safeguarding and behaviour, plus counsellors, key workers and mentors. Motto: "whatever it takes, for as long as it takes."
- **No teaching assistants** at Trinity ("or TAs by any other name"): teaching is the exclusive domain of qualified teachers. Specialists do only what they are trained for; mentors use a "Jiminy Cricket" model (brief pep talk, then withdraw) rather than being "velcroed" to a child.
- **Double staffing / flexible grouping**: originally an extra teacher per year group every lesson in English and maths; under budget pressure this evolved into flexible grouping — e.g. the strongest teacher takes 49 pupils while a second teacher runs bespoke small-group teaching for 11 (framed as non-deficit: the small group may be strugglers, poor attenders or stretch mathematicians).
- **No withdrawal from lessons** except to facilitate return; no separate SEND column on schemes of work — "either your lessons are for all the children or they aren't."
- Assess–plan–do–review cycles are hard-wired into the trust calendar with obligatory face-to-face review meetings; SENCo is reframed as **INCo** (individual needs coordinator); Dixons will only train external SENCos if the **principal attends alongside them** — "game-changing" for shifting whole-school mindset.

**5. Dixons OpenSource.** The trust open-sources its entire playbook — culture, coaching, assessment, systems — free on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/dixonsopensource) and [dixonsat.com](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/dixons-opensource), with paid tiers (Insight: virtual events/exec Q&A; Onsite: bespoke visits) added in 2022. For a design team, this is the rare case where a top-performing trust's operating manual is publicly inspectable.

**Honest caveats.** (1) Trust-wide P8 of +0.11 shows replication is real but much weaker than the Trinity flagship (+0.76); turnaround schools (Croxteth −0.94) remain well below zero years into the model. (2) The disadvantaged gap persists even at Trinity (+0.34 vs +0.76). (3) Ofsted found suspensions at Dixons Allerton were **high**, mostly for persistent disruption, with re-suspension of the same pupils ([Ofsted, Dixons Allerton](https://files.ofsted.gov.uk/v1/file/50251009)) — the strict-culture model carries an exclusions risk that Mountain Rescue mitigates but does not eliminate. (4) Cottingley's leaders attribute the 2024 jump "solely" to Mountain Rescue — a causal claim from a single uncontrolled school-year; treat as promising, not proven. (5) Pure double staffing proved financially unsustainable ("we have to operate within the realms of reality") and has already been diluted to flexible grouping.

## Design implications for our school

1. **Adopt a daily Morning Meeting** (20–30 min, whole cohort or advisory-sized) combining retrieval practice, literacy/numeracy, culture reset and staff-given appreciations pushed to families — one ritual doing four jobs.
2. **Build the SEND model in from day one, not as a bolt-on**: one multidisciplinary inclusion/pastoral/safeguarding department (our "Mountain Rescue"), mainstream-first, no-withdrawal default, with specialist roles (counsellor, key worker, mentor) strictly separated from teaching.
3. **Convert most TA budget into qualified-teacher capacity** for flexible grouping in English and maths (e.g. 49/11 splits with the strongest teacher on the large group); design timetable and rooming to make regrouping cheap.
4. **Replace appraisal with universal incremental coaching** plus twice-weekly whole-staff deliberate practice; resource ~1 trained coach per 6–8 teachers and protect the time in the calendar.
5. **Codify culture granularly** (values → observable behaviours → scripts/artifacts) and push decision-making power down — flexible grouping only works if heads of faculty can re-timetable without escalation.
6. **Hard-wire assess–plan–do–review into the whole-school calendar** with obligatory review meetings, and train the head alongside the SENCo/INCo, never the SENCo alone.
7. **Plan for the new Ofsted inclusion grade**: Dixons-style mainstream inclusion is now inspection-aligned; segregated units and TA-dependency are increasingly exposed.
8. **Monitor suspension rates as a first-class KPI** — high-expectations cultures in deprived catchments drift toward repeat suspensions without deliberate counter-pressure.
