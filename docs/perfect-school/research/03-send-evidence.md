# SEND Provision That Works in Mainstream Settings

Research brief 03 · Compiled 2026-07-16 · For the design of a UK all-through school in a high-deprivation, high-SEND catchment

## What the evidence says

**The policy ground has just shifted.** The February 2026 Schools White Paper *Every Child Achieving and Thriving* and its companion consultation *SEND reform: putting children and young people first* propose the biggest redesign of SEND since 2014: a four-level model (universal → targeted → targeted plus → specialist), with digital **Individual Support Plans (ISPs)** for all pupils above universal, and **EHCPs reserved only for the specialist tier** — issued *after* a specialist placement decision, reversing today's sequence. Implementation begins ~2029; no EHCP changes before September 2030, current Year 3+ pupils keep plans to age 16, and DfE expects EHCP volumes back at current levels by 2035. Schools must publish annual "inclusion strategies" (replacing SEN information reports), join local SEND clusters with pooled inclusion funding, and be inspected on inclusion — already a standalone evaluation area in Ofsted's report cards introduced from November 2025 ([Commons Library CBP-10550](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10550/); [Schools Week policy summary](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/schools-white-paper-the-key-send-reform-policies/); [DfE consultation](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/send-reform-putting-children-and-young-people-first/send-reform-putting-children-and-young-people-first-html-version)). A school designed now should be architected for this tiered system, not the 2014 one.

**Why reform is happening: the current system is failing financially and educationally.** The NAO (Oct 2024) judged the system "financially unsustainable": high-needs funding rose 58% in a decade to £10.7bn while outcomes did not improve; EHCP demand is up ~140% since 2015; local authority DSG deficits were projected to hit £4.6bn by March 2026, with a £2.9–3.9bn annual funding mismatch by 2027-28 ([NAO report](https://www.nao.org.uk/reports/support-for-children-and-young-people-with-special-educational-needs/)). Scale in 2025: 638,700 EHCPs (+10.8% year-on-year) and SEN support at 14.8% of pupils — roughly 1.7m pupils with identified SEND ([DfE EHCP statistics 2025](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/education-health-and-care-plans/2025); [SEN in England 2025/26](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/special-educational-needs-in-england/2025-26)).

**Adaptive teaching, not differentiation-by-task.** The Early Career Framework deliberately dropped "differentiation" in favour of "adaptive teaching": diagnose understanding continuously and vary *support* (scaffolds, questioning, flexible grouping, pre-teaching), rather than producing separate tasks or capped "must/should/could" objectives, which the EEF warns can lower expectations and fragment the curriculum ([EEF blog on the ECF evidence](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/eef-blog-ecf-exploring-the-evidence-part-1); [EEF: moving from differentiation to adaptive teaching](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/moving-from-differentiation-to-adaptive-teaching)). Honest caveat: "adaptive teaching" is a professional consensus construct built on correlational and theoretical work (e.g., Corno), not a trialled intervention with its own effect size.

**The EEF's *SEN in Mainstream Schools* guidance (2020) is the anchor document.** Its five recommendations: (1) create a positive, supportive environment for all; (2) build a holistic understanding of each pupil (with families and other agencies); (3) ensure high-quality teaching for all — the "Five-a-day": explicit instruction, cognitive/metacognitive strategies, scaffolding, flexible grouping, and technology; (4) complement this with carefully selected, well-implemented small-group and 1:1 interventions; (5) deploy teaching assistants effectively. Its headline: pupils with SEND need high-quality teaching *first*; specialness lies mostly in intensity and precision, not different pedagogy ([EEF guidance report](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/send)).

**Resourced provision / SEN units vs special schools.** England had 449 schools with SEN units and 1,217 with resourced provision in January 2025, and the White Paper folds both into a new "inclusion bases" concept, with DfE guidance urging models where children spend the *majority* of time in mainstream classes ([Tes on inclusion bases guidance](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/dfe-guidance-for-schools-inclusion-bases-send-support)). Older Ofsted evidence found mainstream schools with additionally resourced provision proportionately the *most* effective placement type ([Ofsted 2006, *Inclusion: does it matter where pupils are taught?*](https://cyps.northyorks.gov.uk/sites/default/files/SEND/Autism/Appendix_L_-_Ofsted_report_2006_-_Inclusion_-_Does_it_matter_pupils_are_taught.doc)). But the modern evidence base is thin and mixed: Strogilos et al. (2024) found RPs sit on a continuum from "inclusive service" to segregated "safe space", with weak integration a common failure ([JORSEN](https://nasenjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1471-3802.12622)); in DfE-commissioned survey work over a third of unit schools reported weak joint working between unit and mainstream staff, and under half believed they had sufficient specialist expertise ([Tes](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/is-send-provision-in-mainstream-schools-working-inclusion)). On placement effects overall, recent analysis suggests heavy special-school use risks depressing attainment for pupils who could be in mainstream, while heavy mainstream use risks wellbeing costs for a minority — there is no clean winner; design quality matters more than placement label ([Educational Review 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00131911.2025.2520993)). Treat "RPs outperform" claims as promising but under-evidenced.

**Speech, language and communication is the deprivation-facing need.** Speech and Language UK estimates 1.9m UK children (~1 in 5) are behind with talking and understanding — the highest ever recorded — concentrated in areas of social deprivation ([Speech and Language UK](https://speechandlanguage.org.uk/the-issue/)). SLCN is the second most common EHCP primary need (22.1%), behind autism (33.5%) ([2025/26 data via Schools Week/DfE](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/special-educational-needs-in-england/2025-26)). The White Paper adds speech/language/communication as a named development area in the updated Code of Practice.

**Autism at scale.** Autism is the largest EHCP category; 60% of teachers told the APPG on Autism they lacked training to teach autistic pupils ([NAS](https://www.autism.org.uk/advice-and-guidance/professional-practice/supporting-autistic-children-to-stay-in-mainstream)). Whole-workforce training (Autism Education Trust tiers, including office/catering/transport staff) plus environmental design (low-arousal spaces, predictable routines) is the consensus model, though AET training itself has limited RCT evidence.

## What the best operators actually do

- **Integration, not co-location.** The National Autistic Society's **Cullum Centres** (units within mainstream secondaries) have pupils spend the majority of time in mainstream lessons, while centre specialists coach mainstream colleagues — the base is a whole-school service, not a destination ([NAS](https://www.autism.org.uk/advice-and-guidance/professional-practice/supporting-autistic-children-to-stay-in-mainstream)). Strogilos's "inclusive service vs safe space" framing is the design test.
- **TAs redeployed on MITA/EEF lines.** Effective schools stop "velcro" 1:1 TA support (which can *reduce* progress by substituting TA talk for teacher teaching) and instead use TAs to deliver brief, structured, trained interventions (~30 min, several times weekly) and to enable the teacher to work with the SEND pupil ([EEF TA deployment guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/teaching-assistants)).
- **A disciplined graduated response.** Assess–Plan–Do–Review cycles owned by class teachers, coordinated by a SENCO who since 1 Sept 2024 must hold or be working towards the **NPQ for SENCOs** within 3 years of appointment ([DfE mandatory qualification guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mandatory-qualification-for-sencos/transition-to-national-professional-qualification-for-special-educational-needs-co-ordinators)).
- **Universal screening + in-house therapy.** High-SEND schools screen language on entry (Reception and Y7), employ or commission speech and language therapists directly rather than waiting on NHS lists, and run communication-friendly classroom standards for every teacher ([Speech and Language UK on specialist resourced provision](https://speechandlanguage.org.uk/about-us/news-and-blogs/new-guidance-on-specialist-resourced-provision-in-mainstream-schools-transforms-outcomes-for-pupils-with-speech-and-language-challenges/)).
- **SEND as a whole-school teaching issue, not a department.** EEF-aligned operators put the "Five-a-day" into their CPD spine and instructional coaching for every teacher, making tier-1 adaptation the SENCO's biggest lever.

**Evidence-quality flags.** Effect sizes for whole-school inclusion models barely exist; RP/unit outcome research is described in the literature as "very little known". The 1.9m SLCN figure is a teacher-survey estimate, not clinical prevalence. Adaptive teaching lacks direct trial evidence. Ofsted's 2006 finding predates the current cohort. Plan on the EEF's trialled ingredients (explicit instruction, oral language, TA-led structured interventions, small-group tuition), not on placement-model claims.

## Design implications for our school

1. **Build for the 2029 tiered system now**: an in-house continuum of universal → targeted → targeted plus (inclusion base) → specialist partnership, with digital, family-visible support plans that will map onto ISPs.
2. **Tier 1 is the SEND strategy**: embed the EEF Five-a-day in the CPD/coaching entitlement for every teacher; ban differentiation-by-task; train all staff (including non-teaching) via AET.
3. **Two inclusion bases from day one — autism/regulation and SLCN** — funded at £10k/place plus top-ups, run as "inclusive services": pupils majority-time in mainstream, base specialists coaching all staff. This keeps children out of £61.5k independent placements and is where the White Paper is heading.
4. **Employ speech and language therapy in-house**; screen every child's language at entry (Reception and Y7); whole-school oracy and vocabulary curriculum as the deprivation-facing universal offer.
5. **Redesign the TA workforce**: fewer, better-paid, properly trained TAs delivering structured, time-limited interventions with entry/exit data — never default 1:1 velcro support.
6. **SENCO (NPQ-qualified) on SLT**, leading a distributed inclusion team; graduated APDR cycles owned by classroom teachers.
7. **Target the SEND suspension gap** (~4×) with relational behaviour policy, regulation spaces and internal alternative-to-suspension pathways — exclusion is the pipeline out of mainstream.
8. **Financial realism**: model the £6k notional-SEN threshold honestly, join/lead the local SEND cluster early, and treat pooled inclusion funding as a strategic opportunity.
