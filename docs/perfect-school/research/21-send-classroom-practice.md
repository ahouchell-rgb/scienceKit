# SEND at the classroom and department level: what a teacher, TA and HoD actually do

*Research brief 21 — operational layer beneath Sections 3 and 18. Compiled 2026-07-17. Worked example: the science department (KS3 curriculum lead, TLR3).*

## What the evidence says

**"Five-a-day" is a repertoire, not a checklist to tick once a lesson.** The EEF's *Special Educational Needs in Mainstream Schools* guidance names five adaptive-teaching strands every teacher should draw on continuously: **explicit instruction** (model → guided practice → independent practice, not "discover it yourself"); **cognitive and metacognitive strategies** (making pupils plan, monitor and self-evaluate their approach, not just execute it); **scaffolding** (temporary verbal/visual/written supports, explicitly withdrawn as competence grows — not permanent crutches); **flexible grouping** (formed around a current shared need, disbanded once that need is met — the opposite of a fixed "bottom set" a child never leaves); and **use of technology** (assistive tools — text-to-speech, word prediction, visualisers — targeted at a specific access barrier) ([EEF Five-a-day guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/supporting-high-quality-teaching-for-pupils-with-send); [Five-a-day poster](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/eef-guidance-reports/send/Five-a-day-poster_1.1.pdf)). The evidence base for each *component* is solid (explicit instruction and scaffolding sit among the best-evidenced teaching strategies in the toolkit); the claim that bundling them as "five-a-day" *for SEND specifically* improves outcomes is a professional-consensus synthesis, not itself a trialled intervention — flag accordingly.

**Adaptive teaching replaced "differentiation" for a reason grounded in evidence of harm.** The Early Career Framework dropped differentiation-by-task because pre-set "must/should/could" objectives and permanently separate worksheets tend to lower expectations, cap ceiling, and calcify a pupil's identity as "the one who gets the easy sheet" ([EEF: moving from differentiation to adaptive teaching](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/moving-from-differentiation-to-adaptive-teaching)). Adaptive teaching means the *same* lesson objective for (almost) everyone, reached via varied support, questioning and scaffolding that is diagnosed and adjusted in real time — the difference is in the how, not the what.

**TA deployment is where good intentions do the most damage.** The EEF's *Making Best Use of Teaching Assistants* guidance report (7 recommendations across three domains — everyday classroom use; structured out-of-class interventions; linking the two) found that pupils allocated a permanent, dedicated 1:1 TA (the "**Velcro TA**" pattern) made *less* progress than similar pupils without one, driven by three mechanisms: TA talk substituting for teacher talk, TAs prioritising task completion over understanding, and TAs inadvertently fostering learned helplessness and separation from the class/teacher ([EEF TA guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/teaching-assistants); [DERA PDF of the full report](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/29360/1/TA_Guidance_Report_MakingBestUseOfTeachingAssisstants-Printable.pdf)). The seven recommendations, condensed: (1) TAs must not be an informal teaching substitute for low attainers; (2) use TAs to add value to the teacher, not replace them; (3) use TAs to help pupils build independent learning skills, not dependency; (4) fully prepare TAs for their classroom role (they need the lesson plan and subject content *before* the lesson, not during it); (5) use TAs to deliver structured 1:1/small-group interventions with a validated protocol; (6) ensure interventions are timetabled to allow genuine implementation fidelity; (7) explicitly connect what happens in interventions to classroom teaching so gains transfer. Well-implemented structured TA-led intervention adds **~4 months' progress**; unstructured, unplanned deployment is close to zero or negative ([EEF Toolkit: TA interventions](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/teaching-assistant-interventions)).

**The graduated approach (assess–plan–do–review, APDR) is a legal duty, not a good idea** — it is the operationalisation of the SEND Code of Practice 2015's four-stage cycle, still the current statutory reference pending the White Paper's 2029 legislative changes. Inside a single lesson, APDR compresses to: **assess** (retrieval starter/hinge question surfaces who doesn't yet have the prerequisite), **plan** (which of the five-a-day strand + which scaffold, decided in seconds, ideally pre-planned at the point of lesson design not improvised), **do** (deliver it, live, without pulling the pupil out of the lesson), **review** (exit ticket/live marking shows whether the scaffold worked; feeds the next lesson's plan). The unit of analysis that most guidance omits is: **this cycle has to run in the 45–60 minutes of a single lesson**, dozens of times a week, for one teacher covering 28+ pupils with 6–8 different needs — which is why it must be pre-planned into the lesson resource, not invented live.

**The 2025–26 policy shift changes what "SEND at classroom level" will formally require.** The Schools White Paper (Feb 2026) introduces the **National Inclusion Standards** (early rollout from 2025/26, full introduction 2028) and a statutory duty for schools to maintain digital **Individual Support Plans (ISPs)** for pupils at Targeted and Targeted Plus tiers, setting out day-to-day educational provision and reviewed at least annually ([Commons Library CBP-10550](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10550/); [structural-learning.com summary](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/send-white-paper-2026-every-teacher-needs)). Practically, this means every subject teacher — not just the SENCO — will need to read and act on an ISP for a much larger cohort than currently hold an EHCP, because ISPs sit below EHCP threshold. A department-level system that assumes only EHCP pupils need documented adjustments will be out of compliance by the time the standards land.

**Behaviour and SEND: the evidence does not support "different rules for different children," but it equally does not support "identical response regardless of need."** Tom Bennett — now the DfE's national behaviour and attendance ambassador — argues strict, consistent routines and inclusion "dovetail perfectly" and that reasonable adjustments (e.g. for autism) are a legal duty schools must meet *without* creating a two-tier system of visibly different consequences ([Tes, "strict rules dovetail perfectly with inclusion"](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/tom-bennett-strict-rules-dovetail-perfectly-inclusion); [original Bennett review](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7506e4ed915d3c7d529cec/Tom_Bennett_Independent_Review_of_Behaviour_in_Schools.pdf)). SEND sector voices have pushed back that a blanket "no exceptions" reading of consistency risks penalising behaviours that are disability-driven (dysregulation, sensory overload, demand avoidance) rather than choice-driven — precisely the tension this brief has to resolve operationally, not rhetorically. The legal floor is the Equality Act 2010: schools must anticipate and make reasonable adjustments for disabled pupils (including ADHD/ASC where it meets the definition) — this is a duty on the *system* (seating, instructions, movement breaks, sensory access, adjusted response to dysregulation), not a licence for individual staff discretion, and not a suspension of the behaviour code itself.

**Exclusion data shows this reconciliation is currently failing.** In autumn 2024/25, the permanent exclusion rate for pupils with SEN support was **0.14** and for EHCP pupils **0.11**, against a whole-cohort rate of **0.04** — SEN-support pupils are excluded at roughly **3.5×** the overall rate, and suspension rates show an even starker gap (SEND brief 03 in this series cites ~4× for suspensions) ([DfE, Suspensions and permanent exclusions in England, autumn 2024/25](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions-in-england/2023-24)). This is the sharpest evidence that "consistency" as currently practised in many schools is not, in fact, adjusting for disability at the point of behaviour response — the adjustment is happening (if at all) only after the exclusion, not before it.

## What the best operators actually do

- **Michaela** runs a maximally consistent, scripted behaviour system (silent corridors, tracked demerits) but pairs it with heavy **pre-emptive scaffolding at the point of instruction** (knowledge organisers, "My Turn/Your Turn," cold-call with think time) so that the adjustment happens in the *teaching*, reducing the number of moments where a SEND pupil is set up to fail a behaviour expectation in the first place — the reconciliation is upstream, not a carve-out downstream (Section 11 of this research set covers Michaela's whole-school model in depth).
- **Dixons Academies Trust** builds "reasonable adjustments" into its behaviour policy as a named, written **pre-authorised list per pupil** (e.g. permitted movement break, a fidget tool, a modified response to a specific trigger) attached to the pupil's plan and shared with every teacher who teaches them — so the adjustment is applied *consistently by every adult*, which is the opposite of a two-tier system: it is one system, individually calibrated and transparently documented, not an ad hoc exception granted by whichever teacher is in the room that day (Section 13).
- **Star Academies** trains all staff (not just SENCOs/TAs) in de-escalation and "regulate-relate-reason" sequencing before any consequence is issued to a dysregulated pupil, and logs each such incident against the pupil's plan for pattern review at the termly APDR review (Section 14).
- **Ark** deploys TAs on the MITA model described above: TAs are subject/phase-attached rather than pupil-attached where possible, trained on the week's content in advance, and explicitly briefed to prompt-not-tell and to withdraw support incrementally within the lesson — matching EEF recommendation 3 (build independence) (Section 15).
- **The Difference / Heritage High School** (cited in brief 18) halved suspensions within a year by training the *whole SLT* — not just pastoral staff — in inclusive leadership, so that behaviour decisions involving SEND pupils are made with the same person applying the same threshold-and-adjustment logic school-wide, closing the inconsistency gap between departments that a purely departmental approach cannot fix.
- **Common departmental practice worth copying at HoD level**: a **department SEND crib sheet**, one side of A4 per class, updated termly, listing every pupil's primary need, their pre-agreed scaffold(s) and pre-agreed behaviour adjustment(s) side by side — so a cover teacher or new starter can run the reconciliation correctly on day one without waiting for a briefing.

**Evidence-quality flags.** The "five-a-day-as-a-cluster" framing and the "reasonable adjustments must be pre-authorised and written" practice above are professional/operator consensus, not RCT-evidenced — treat as promising, not proven. The EEF TA effect size is the strongest causal number in this brief; everything about *behaviour-SEND reconciliation specifically* is drawn from case studies, DfE ambassador commentary and sector debate, not controlled trials — there is essentially no experimental literature on "how to run a consistent behaviour system with disability adjustments," which is itself worth stating plainly rather than dressing up borrowed confidence.

## Design implications for our department layer

1. **Pre-plan the five-a-day into the lesson resource, not the teacher's head.** Every department (science first, TLR3-led) builds scaffold ladders into the shared lesson slide/booklet template — the choice of strand happens at planning time, so APDR can run in real time during the lesson without inventing support live.
2. **Kill the Velcro TA by job design, not just guidance.** TAs are timetabled to a subject/year group, not a named pupil, wherever legally possible (EHCP-specified 1:1 support is the documented exception); TAs receive the week's lesson content and glossary **48 hours ahead**; every TA intervention has a named structured protocol, an entry/exit assessment, and a written link back to what happens in the following lesson.
3. **Build the department SEND crib sheet now**, one per class, reviewed each half-term at department APDR review — need, agreed scaffold, agreed behaviour adjustment, in one place, so cover staff and new joiners apply it identically.
4. **Write behaviour adjustments down and pre-authorise them per pupil**, on the Dixons model, signed off by SENCO + HoD/pastoral lead, shared with every teacher of that pupil — this is the mechanism that keeps one consistent behaviour system while making individualised, disability-driven adjustments, because the adjustment is transparent, documented and applied by every adult identically rather than granted ad hoc.
5. **Separate the response to dysregulation from the sanction for defiance** in the behaviour policy text itself: a written de-escalation/regulate-first sequence for a documented dysregulation trigger, distinct from (and not a substitute for) the standard consequence ladder for behaviour that is not disability-linked — train all staff, not just pastoral leads, in this distinction (Star Academies model).
6. **Build ISPs before they're statutory.** Start digital, teacher-visible support plans now for every Targeted-tier pupil (not just EHCP), reviewed termly, so the school is ahead of the 2028 National Inclusion Standards rather than retrofitting under inspection pressure.
7. **Track the exclusion/suspension SEND gap as a live department and whole-school metric**, reviewed at the same cadence as academic data — a widening or static gap is the signal that "consistency" has drifted into "no adjustment," and a narrowing gap with stable overall behaviour is the signal the reconciliation is working.
8. **Make the SENCO (NPQ-qualified) the co-owner of the behaviour policy's SEND clauses**, not just its academic provision — the reconciliation this brief is built around fails whenever behaviour and SEND are run as two separate systems that only meet at exclusion.
