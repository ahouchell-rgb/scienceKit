# Behaviour and School Culture in High-Deprivation Contexts

*Research brief 04 — Perfect School project. Drafted 17 July 2026. Covers: warm-strict vs relational approaches, routines and centralised detentions, exclusion/suspension data for disadvantaged and SEND pupils, internal inclusion units, restorative practice, and calm corridors without off-rolling.*

## What the evidence says

**Behaviour interventions work on average, but the "what" matters less than the "how".** The EEF Toolkit rates behaviour interventions as moderate impact (~+3–4 months' additional progress, similar for primary and secondary), with wide variation by intervention quality ([EEF Toolkit](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/behaviour-interventions)). The underpinning evidence review (Moore et al., 2019) found that *both* improving teachers' classroom-management skill *and* building pupils' social-emotional/cognitive skills are effective on average — i.e. the warm-strict vs relational framing is a false binary at the level of the research ([EEF/Moore review](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED612210.pdf)).

**Consistency, routines and leadership are the strongest common threads.** The EEF's *Improving Behaviour in Schools* guidance (2019) makes six recommendations: know and understand pupils and their influences; teach learning behaviours alongside managing misbehaviour; use simple classroom-management routines (e.g. greeting at the door); use targeted, personalised approaches (daily report cards have "good evidence") for the minority who need more; and prioritise consistency and implementation over any specific policy choice ([EEF guidance report](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/behaviour)). Tom Bennett's DfE review *Creating a Culture* (2017) reached the same conclusion from case studies: successful schools show relentless attention to detail on whole-school routines, and leadership — not individual teacher heroics — is the decisive lever ([Creating a Culture](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7506e4ed915d3c7d529cec/Tom_Bennett_Independent_Review_of_Behaviour_in_Schools.pdf)). The DfE's Behaviour Hubs final evaluation (programme ran 2021–March 2025) found the most improved schools increased their focus on **rewarding positive behaviour** and on staff responsibilities — not on harsher sanctions ([interim report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/673e1768b3f0df6d2ebaef69/Evaluation_of_the_behaviour_hubs_programme_interim_report.pdf); [Tes summary of final evaluation](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/rewarding-good-behaviour-key-school-success-dfe-hubs-evaluation-finds)).

**Relationships have real but mostly correlational evidence.** Roorda et al.'s meta-analysis (99 studies) found medium-to-large associations between teacher–pupil relationship quality and engagement, small-to-medium with achievement — and **larger effects for academically at-risk students**, directly relevant to our catchment ([Roorda et al. 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654311421793)). Caveat: this is largely correlational; causal evidence for whole-school "relational policies" (e.g. Paul Dix-style no-sanction models, attachment-aware programmes) is thin — evaluations are mostly small, uncontrolled and perception-based ([Attachment Aware Schools evaluation](https://www.education.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/Bath-and-NE-Somerset-Attachment-Aware-Schools-Programme-Evaluation-report.pdf)).

**Restorative practice: genuinely mixed RCT evidence.** The strongest UK trial, INCLUSIVE/Learning Together (40 schools, Lancet 2018), found a small but significant reduction in bullying (effect size −0.08), no effect on aggression, but meaningful secondary benefits (wellbeing, police contact, smoking/alcohol) ([Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(18)31782-3/fulltext)). The US Pittsburgh RCT (44 schools) cut days lost to suspension by 36%, but gains were concentrated in elementary schools and maths achievement dipped ([RAND/Augustine RCT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9972315/)). The London LEIP cluster-RCT (36 schools) found **null-to-negative** effects — treatment pupils self-reported *more* exclusion ([LEIP](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5306147/)). Honest read: restorative practice works as a *language and repair layer on top of* a clear, consistently enforced system; as a *replacement* for consequences it has no reliable evidence base, and implementation quality is decisive.

**Tiered positive-behaviour systems have decent causal evidence (US).** The SWPBIS randomised effectiveness trial (37 elementary schools) showed significant reductions in suspensions and office referrals and improved perceived safety ([Bradshaw et al. 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1098300709334798)). Transferability to English secondaries is untested at scale — flag as promising, not proven.

**Warm-strict itself has no trial evidence.** Michaela (Progress 8 ≈ +2.3 in recent years, top nationally, in a deprived Brent catchment) and Dixons' trust-wide results are striking but are single-operator outcomes with possible selection, intake and replication caveats ([Michaela Ofsted](https://files.ofsted.gov.uk/v1/file/50222824)). The honest claim: warm-strict is *consistent with* the routines/consistency/high-expectations evidence, not independently proven by RCT. Likewise **centralised same-day detentions**: no direct causal studies exist; the rationale rests on deterrence theory (certainty beats severity) and on removing enforcement burden from individual teachers ([TeacherToolkit pros/cons](https://www.teachertoolkit.co.uk/2022/10/12/centralised-detentions-the-pros-and-cons/)). Evidence-thin but operationally coherent.

**Internal exclusion is the hidden risk zone.** Thornton (BERJ, 2026; 34,000+ pupils, 121 schools) found 8.3% of pupils report isolation in a typical week, averaging 8.44 hours; SEND and FSM pupils are roughly **twice as likely** to be isolated *even after controlling for behaviour*, with measurable harm to belonging and wellbeing ([Thornton 2026](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/berj.70049)). The literature distinguishes punitive isolation from **staffed "internal inclusion units"** offering planned academic/SEMH support — used by >50% of 143 secondaries studied by Mills & Thomson ([DfE 2018 via BeeWell summary](https://beewellprogramme.org/understanding-internal-exclusion-in-english-secondary-schools-what-the-evidence-tells-us/)).

**Policy context (verified 2025–26).** Ofsted's new report cards (from 10 November 2025) make **inclusion a standalone graded evaluation area** with case-sampling of individual vulnerable pupils; behaviour and attendance is also separately graded ([The Key](https://thekeysupport.com/blog/inclusion-under-the-new-ofsted-framework-what-inspectors-will-look-for/)). The Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (Royal Assent 29 April 2026) tightens oversight: revised statutory suspension/exclusion guidance applies from 26 July 2026, new governing-board review duties for off-site direction from 1 August 2026, and a local-authority register of children not in school ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/contents/enacted); [Hill Dickinson summary](https://www.hilldickinson.com/our-view/articles/department-for-education-releases-new-statutory-suspension-and-exclusions-guidance/)). Off-rolling — removal from roll in the school's interest rather than the pupil's — is explicitly hunted by inspectors and disproportionately hits SEND/low-attaining pupils ([Ofsted blog](https://educationinspection.blog.gov.uk/2019/05/10/what-is-off-rolling-and-how-does-ofsted-look-at-it-on-inspection/)).

## What the best operators actually do

- **Dixons Academies** (deprived northern catchments): explicitly "warm-strict" — values-driven, tightly scripted routines ("do the simple things well every day"), *plus* deliberate relationship-building at all levels; disadvantage-conscious support is built into daily routines rather than bolted on ([Dixons culture](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/culture)).
- **Michaela**: uncompromising expectations with no differentiation of standards by background; family lunch, gratitude rituals and high-frequency merits supply the "warm" side ([Ofsted report](https://files.ofsted.gov.uk/v1/file/50222824)).
- **Common operational pattern across high performers**: a taught behaviour curriculum (routines explicitly rehearsed, especially Year 7 induction "bootcamps"); centralised same-day detentions run by SLT so certainty is high, severity low, and teacher workload/inconsistency is removed ([Parents & Teachers for Excellence](https://parentsandteachers.org.uk/a-centralised-detention-system/)); heavy reward-to-sanction ratios (the Behaviour Hubs finding); and senior leaders visibly on corridors at every transition (Bennett's "flood the corridors" leadership finding).
- **Schools graded 'exceptional' for inclusion** under the new framework share: fast, accurate identification of need, pupil premium used to remove specific barriers, and strong external/AP partnerships ([The Key](https://thekeysupport.com/blog/what-schools-can-learn-from-ofsted-report-cards/)) — i.e. calm corridors *and* inclusion are achieved together, not traded off.

## Numbers that matter

- **2024/25 (full year, England)**: 913,000 suspensions (rate 10.88 per 100 pupils, down from 11.31); 9,900 permanent exclusions (0.12). FSM pupils: suspension rate **26.66 vs 5.41** for non-FSM (~5×); permanent exclusion **0.32 vs 0.05** (~6×). SEN support pupils: suspension rate **28.86**; EHCP **26.45**; no-SEN **6.78** ([DfE, released 9 July 2026](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions-in-england/2024-25)).
- **Persistent disruptive behaviour** = ~51% of suspension reasons; **1.83 million learning days** lost to suspension in 2023/24 ([DfE 2023/24](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions-in-england-2023-to-2024)).
- **Timpson (2019)**: 78% of permanent exclusions go to pupils with SEN, in-need status or FSM; 11% have all three ([Timpson Review](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5cfe7d8de5274a0906be72c8/Timpson_review.pdf)).
- **Cost of exclusion**: £170k–£370k lifetime cost per permanently excluded child (IPPR; methodology contested — treat as order-of-magnitude); AP places average ~£17k/year, private AP up to £111k ([IPPR *Who is Losing Learning?*](https://www.ippr.org/articles/who-is-losing-learning); [critique](https://teachingbattleground.wordpress.com/2022/05/13/do-permanent-exclusions-cost-370k-per-excluded-pupil/)).
- **Classroom reality**: teachers report ~7 minutes lost per 30 minutes of teaching to misbehaviour — unchanged since 2022 ([National Behaviour Survey 2024/25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691dd17a513046b952c50093/National_behaviour_suvery_report_2024_to_2025.pdf)).
- **Internal exclusion**: 8.3% of pupils isolated in a typical week, ~8.4 hours each; SEND/FSM ~2× more likely after behaviour controls ([Thornton 2026](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/berj.70049)).

## Design implications for our school

1. **Build a warm-strict spine with a relational layer — not either/or.** Tightly rehearsed whole-school routines, a taught behaviour curriculum (Year 7 induction bootcamp, re-induction each term), and visible SLT presence at transitions; alongside a structure that guarantees every child is known (small tutor "families", EEF-recommended daily report cards for the 5–10% who need personalisation).
2. **Certainty over severity.** Centralised, same-day, SLT-run detentions with a mandatory 2-minute restorative conversation before release — capturing the certainty logic while embedding repair. Track reward:sanction ratio weekly; target ≥4:1 (Behaviour Hubs finding).
3. **Replace punitive isolation with a staffed internal inclusion unit.** Time-limited (≤2 days), curriculum-continuous, staffed by qualified teachers plus SEMH support, with automatic SEND screening on second referral. Publish an internal-exclusion dashboard split by FSM/SEND/ethnicity/sex to governors half-termly — Thornton's 2× disparity is the metric to beat.
4. **Make SEND identification precede sanction escalation.** Given SEN-support pupils are suspended at 4× the no-SEN rate and most of our excluded pupils will be FSM/SEND, hard-wire a graduated-response check (communication/SEMH screening) before any second suspension.
5. **Zero off-rolling by design.** No managed moves without a documented best-interests case and destination-school confirmation; governor sign-off on every roll removal in Years 9–11. Ofsted's inclusion case-sampling and the 2026 Act's off-site direction review duties make this both ethically and inspectorially non-negotiable.
6. **Use restorative practice as universal language, not the accountability system.** Train all staff in restorative conversations (INCLUSIVE-style), but keep clear consequences — the LEIP null result warns against RP-as-replacement.
7. **Honest evidence flags**: warm-strict, silent corridors and centralised detentions have no RCT evidence — we adopt them on coherence with routines/consistency evidence and operator track records, and commit to measuring suspension-equity gaps, minutes-lost surveys and belonging measures from day one.
