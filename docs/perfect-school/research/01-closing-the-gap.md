# 01 — What Actually Closes the Disadvantage Attainment Gap

*Research brief for the perfect-school design project. Drafted 2026-07-17. Focus: a high-deprivation, high-SEND English catchment. Policy status verified against the post-2024 landscape (Curriculum & Assessment Review final report Nov 2025; Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act, Royal Assent 29 April 2026).*

## What the evidence says

**1. The gap is mostly built before secondary school, and schools explain a minority of it.** EPI/EEF analysis (March 2025) shows **44% of the GCSE disadvantage gap is already "locked in" by age 11** ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/disadvantage-gap-locked-in-new-research-shows); [EPI, Breaking Down the Gap](https://epi.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Breaking-down-the-gap.pdf)). Multilevel studies consistently find schools account for roughly **10–15% of variance in pupil attainment** — Wilkinson & Bryson estimate schools explain ~14% of Progress 8 variance ([IZA DP 11372](https://docs.iza.org/dp11372.pdf)), and Rasbash et al. show much "pupil-level" variance is really family variance ([JRSS-A](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-985X.2010.00642.x)). Honest design implication: an excellent school can move a disadvantaged cohort by months, not erase a 19-month gap alone. This is an argument for schools that also act on out-of-school factors (attendance, food, parents, early years), not an excuse.

**2. Teaching quality and structured, cheap classroom strategies are the best bets.** The [EEF Teaching and Learning Toolkit](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit) headline estimates (updated through 2025): **metacognition and self-regulation +7 months** (raised to ~+8 in the [2025 strand updates](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/updates-ten-strands-toolkit); very low cost, extensive evidence — and studies with more disadvantaged pupils showed *higher* impact); **feedback +6** ([updated guide](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/updated-eef-guide-to-metacognition-and-self-regulation)); **reading comprehension strategies +6**; **oral language/oracy interventions ~+6**, strengthened by 34 new studies; **phonics +5** with very secure evidence ([EEF phonics](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/phonics)); **mastery learning +5**; **collaborative learning and peer tutoring +5**. Caveat, flagged honestly: "months of progress" is a translation of pooled effect sizes from heterogeneous studies; treat rankings as ordinal best bets, not guarantees ([toolkit critique summary](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/eef-teaching-learning-toolkit-guide)).

**3. Tutoring works when it is small, frequent, curriculum-linked — and actually reaches poor pupils.** [One-to-one tuition](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/evidence-summaries/teaching-learning-toolkit/one-to-one-tuition/) averages **+5 months (+6 primary, +4 secondary)**; [small-group tuition](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/small-group-tuition) (1:2–1:5) **+4 months**. The National Tutoring Programme (2020–24) is the cautionary tale: NFER's [Year 2](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/independent-evaluation-of-the-national-tutoring-programme-year-2-impact-evaluation/) and [Year 3 impact evaluations](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/national-tutoring-programme-year-3-impact-evaluation/) found only ~**1 month's additional progress** at scale, and **only ~35–47% of tutored pupils were disadvantaged** — schools spent the subsidy on whoever was convenient ([DfE final reflections, 2024](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/66e2c1f46cc3c902a6e6fbed/National_Tutoring_Programme_-_evaluation_and_reflection.pdf)). Effect sizes from tight efficacy trials do not survive loose implementation.

**4. Attendance is the single biggest tractable lever right now.** EPI (2025) finds that if disadvantaged pupils had their peers' absence rates, the **age-16 gap (18.6–19 months) would be over 4 months smaller** ([EPI Annual Report 2025](https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2025-disadvantage/)). FSM pupils are **2.5x more likely to be persistently absent (34.8% vs 14.1%)** ([Health Foundation evidence hub](https://www.health.org.uk/evidence-hub/education/relationship-between-school-absences-and-attainment)); 83.7% of KS4 pupils with no absence pass English and maths at 9–4 vs 35.6% of the persistently absent ([Children's Commissioner](https://assets.childrenscommissioner.gov.uk/wpuploads/2023/11/CC-REPORT-_-Attendance-and-Attainment-_-Oct-23.pdf)); missing ~10 extra days in Y11 roughly halves the odds of grade 5 in English and maths ([FFT Datalab, Dec 2025](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2025/12/exploring-the-relationship-between-year-11-absence-and-gcse-results/)). Weak-evidence flag: causal evidence on *which attendance interventions work* is thin — a 2025 systematic review of secondary-school interventions for persistent absence found mostly low-quality studies ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12832960/)). Relationship-based, family-level approaches look most promising; fines do not.

**5. National-scale record: money alone didn't do it; sustained, place-plus-practice efforts partly did.**
- **Pupil premium** (~£2.9bn/yr since 2011): a sensible funding mechanism ([IFS](https://ifs.org.uk/news/pupil-premium-more-cash-poor-pupils-it-worth-cost)), but there is **little evidence it narrowed the gap by itself** — the gap plateaued in the 2010s and is now the joint-widest on record ([Gorard et al.](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02671522.2019.1677759)). Untied money without implementation quality buys little.
- **London Challenge / the "London effect"**: London's disadvantaged pupils dramatically outperform (Newham's GCSE gap is **3.9 months vs 25.7 in West Berkshire** — [EPI 2026 LA data](https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2026/annual-report-local-authority-gaps-2026/)). Cause is contested: Burgess attributes most of it to ethnic composition and aspiration; Blanden et al. find demographics explain the *level* but not the *improvement*, crediting 1990s primary reforms and London Challenge-era practices — data-driven school-to-school support, keys-to-success brokering, leadership development ([DfE LSYPE analysis](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5fb7cc538fa8f559dbb1ad4b/London_effect_report_-_final_20112020.pdf); [FFT Datalab](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2015/12/the-quest-to-find-london-effect-why-are-some-groups-of-pupils-making-more-progress-than-they-used-to/); [UCL IOE](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/ioe/2014/11/17/the-london-effect-did-not-just-happen-without-hard-work/)). Honest read: partly demography, partly a decade of coherent, well-led school improvement.
- **Opportunity Areas** (2017–22, £72m, 12 cold spots): process evaluations found slow progress and no demonstrated attainment impact ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/implementation-of-opportunity-areas-an-independent-evaluation/); [NatCen](https://natcen.ac.uk/publications/opportunity-areas-process-evaluation); [Commons evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/101194/html/)). Five-year place-based programmes are too short and too thinly spread.

## What the best operators actually do

- **Teaching first, tiered spending.** High-performing trusts serving deprived catchments (Star Academies — whose founding school is among the top nationally for disadvantaged pupils' progress; Dixons; Ark; Harris) follow the EEF tiered model: most pupil-premium money on teacher development and curriculum, then targeted small-group/1:1 academic support, then wider strategies (attendance, behaviour, enrichment) ([Star performance](https://staracademies.org/education/our-performance/); [Dixons PP strategy example](https://www.dixonsat.com/uploads/files/dixonsmn/DMN-PP-Strategy-Statement-Sept-2023_2023-10-23-104118_nfeo.pdf)).
- **Codified routines and a knowledge-rich curriculum** with explicit instruction, daily retrieval, and mastery checkpoints — the approaches that dominate the toolkit's high-impact/low-cost quadrant.
- **Cradle-to-career, all-through provision.** Reach Academy Feltham (Progress 8 ≈ +0.94, non-selective, deprived catchment) pairs a 2–18 all-through school with a Children's Hub offering family support from pregnancy onward; the model is now replicated across ~70 place-based clusters ([Reach](https://reachacademyfeltham.com/cradle-to-career/)) — a direct answer to the "44% locked in by 11" problem.
- **Forensic attendance work**: named staff per family, first-day response, home visits, breakfast provision — treating attendance as a relationship problem, not a compliance problem.
- **Deliberate targeting.** The NTP failure shows the discipline the best operators keep: name the pupils, track dosage, protect the ratio (≤1:3, 3+ sessions/week, linked to classwork).

## Design implications for our school

1. **Start before Reception and go all-through (2–18)** with an attached family hub, Reach-style — the single strongest response to gap "lock-in" by 11.
2. **Attendance as a core function**: dedicated family-relationship team, first-day-response, breakfast club (now statutory for primaries under the [Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/contents)), and use of expanded FSM auto-eligibility (all Universal Credit households from Sept 2026, ~500k more children — [DfE](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/families-to-save-up-to-1000-as-childrens-reforms-become-law)) to register every entitled family.
3. **Spend pupil premium on the tiered model**, weighted to teacher development; every intervention has named pupils, dosage tracking, and a kill criterion — the anti-NTP discipline.
4. **Bake best-bet strategies into the universal offer**: explicit metacognitive teaching, whole-school oracy (the Curriculum & Assessment Review's first recommendation, with a national oracy framework coming — [final report, Nov 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report)), systematic phonics with rapid catch-up, and daily embedded small-group tuition on the timetable rather than as a bolt-on. For our high-SEND cohort these same strategies are the core of the EEF's ["five-a-day" SEND guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/send) — explicit instruction, scaffolding, cognitive/metacognitive strategies, flexible grouping, technology — so the SEND and disadvantage strategies are one strategy.
5. **Be honest in our theory of change**: target moving the in-school gap by 4–8 months (attendance + best-bet teaching + tutoring), and address the remaining, larger share through family/place work rather than claiming schooling alone will close a 19-month gap.
6. **Plan for a decade, measure from year one** — London Challenge worked over ~10 years; Opportunity Areas failed in 5. Governance and funding commitments should be written for the long haul, with interim leading indicators (attendance, reading age, oracy) rather than GCSE-only accountability.
