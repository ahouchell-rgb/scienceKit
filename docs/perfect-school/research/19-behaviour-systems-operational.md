# Behaviour Systems, Operationally: Minute-to-Minute in the Department and Classroom

*Research brief 19 — Perfect School project. Drafted 17 July 2026. This is the level BELOW brief 04 (Behaviour and School Culture): not what the evidence says about behaviour in general, but what a Head of Department, class teacher, TA and pupil actually do, second by second — entry routines, the warning ladder, who runs detentions, on-call/removal mechanics, the words staff say, and the throughput numbers. Science (Adam's TLR3) is the worked example; the model generalises to any department.*

## What the evidence says

**The mechanics of consequence ladders and on-call systems are evidenced far more thinly than the constructs behind them.** EEF, Bennett and Behaviour Hubs (brief 04) support *consistency, routines, reward-weighting* as causal-ish constructs, but almost no research measures the actual mechanics — warning counts, detention length, on-call response time, reset-room dwell time — against outcomes. What follows is triangulated from named-operator published policies and DfE statutory guidance, not RCTs of the mechanics themselves.

**The near-universal structure is a 3–5 stage "consequence ladder"** ending in an out-of-classroom step, typically C1–C4: reminder, formal warning, short teacher-issued same-day detention, then removal/longer centrally-run detention. This exact structure recurs, near-verbatim, across dozens of published UK secondary policies (Caldew, Queens', Wordsley, Ripley, Bournside), with only minor label variation ([Caldew](https://www.caldew.cumbria.sch.uk/uploads/1090/Behaviour_Ladder_-_Sep_24..pdf/); [Queens'](https://www.queens.herts.sch.uk/attachments/download.asp?file=233&type=pdf); [Wordsley](https://wordsleyschool.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Wordsley-Consequence-Ladder_V8.pdf); [Bournside 2025-26](https://www.bournside.com/assets/Policies-/Attendance-and-Behaviour/Ladder-of-consequences-2025-26.pdf)). The stated rationale everywhere is deterrence theory — certainty beats severity — consistent with, but not proven by, brief 04's causal literature.

**Centralised detentions have no direct trial evidence, but a coherent rationale**: removing enforcement discretion from individual teachers cuts both perceived inconsistency and the workload of chasing/running detentions ([Parents & Teachers for Excellence](https://parentsandteachers.org.uk/a-centralised-detention-system/)) — the most-cited lever among high performers, evidence-thin but mechanistically sound.

**On-call/removal-to-reset-room systems are near-universal but under-measured.** DfE's statutory guidance (issued May 2026, in force 26 July 2026) requires schools, for the first time, to record and monitor "removals from the classroom" as a category distinct from suspensions ([HCR Law](https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/dfe-issues-new-statutory-guidance-on-suspensions-and-school-exclusions/); [VWV](https://www.vwv.co.uk/insights/articles/dfe-publishes-revised-guidance-on-suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions/)) — until now no national dataset existed, so brief 04's Thornton (2026) internal-exclusion study (8.3% of pupils isolated in a typical week; SEND/FSM ~2x more likely even controlling for behaviour) is the best available proxy.

**Scripted, low-affect de-escalation language has strong practitioner consensus but weak trial evidence.** Paul Dix's "30-second intervention" — non-judgemental, fixed-pace ("I've noticed... I need you to... I know you'll do it because... I'll come back in two minutes") — dominates English secondary CPD and is echoed across published policies, but the evidence is testimonial, not RCT ([Teachwire](https://www.teachwire.net/news/paul-dix-how-to-be-an-emotionally-consistent-teacher/); [Pivotal Education](https://pivotaleducation.com/scripted-behaviour-interventions-pp1/)). Separately, **behaviour-specific praise at 4:1–5:1 praise:reprimand ratios (~6 statements/15 min) has a genuine applied-behaviour-analysis evidence base** ([synthesis](https://www.skillbuildersaba.com/blog/how-to-use-behavior-specific-praise-effectively); [narration-of-positive practice](https://blog.irisconnect.com/uk/managing-behaviour-in-schools-behaviour-management-strategies)).

**National scale**: suspensions and removals are not an edge case. Autumn term 2024 alone recorded 313,008 suspensions and 3,715 permanent exclusions (down from 4,168 the prior autumn) ([SecEd](https://www.sec-ed.co.uk/content/news/school-suspensions-autumn-2024-dfe-data); [FFT Education Datalab](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2025/09/exclusions-and-suspensions-in-2024-25/)); FSM rates run roughly 4–5x non-FSM, consistent with brief 04's fuller 2024/25 figures (26.66 vs 5.41 per 100).

## What the best operators actually do

**Entry/Do-Now routine (Michaela, Dixons, Star, Ark, XP Doncaster — briefs 11–17).** Pupils line up silently, are greeted by name at the door, enter in silence, and start a retrieval-practice "Do Now" within 60 seconds, rehearsed to automaticity. Michaela's published policy formalises entry, seating and equipment checks as scripted, non-negotiable steps, not teacher discretion ([Michaela Behaviour Policy, Sept 2025](https://michaela.education/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Behaviour-Policy-September-2025.pdf)).

**The science-department ladder** (composited from the C1–C4 pattern above, adapted to a lab context):
- **C1 — verbal reminder**, silent and non-judgemental, no written record ("Sam, C1", or a scripted 30-second intervention for a pupil struggling to start).
- **C2 — formal warning**, logged (ClassCharts/Bromcom/Arbor), seat moved if needed — still teacher-managed.
- **C3 — 10-minute same-day teacher detention** at break/lunch, logged centrally so HoD/SLT see patterns.
- **C4 — on-call trigger**: teacher radios/app-flags; a non-teaching senior/pastoral staff member attends (practitioner target: under 5 minutes — unverified) and removes the pupil to a centrally staffed reset room for the rest of the lesson, resulting in a longer centrally-run detention (30–60 min) and a mandatory brief reset conversation before return.
- **Safety override**: any lab-safety breach (naked flame, chemical/glassware misuse) skips straight to removal regardless of warning count — the harm profile differs categorically from talking-in-class; this is a departmental addition, not a replacement for the whole-school ladder.

**Centralised vs teacher-owned — the operating split.** High performers centralise the serious end (C3/C4, run by SLT/pastoral staff, same-day, parents notified same day) while leaving C1/C2 teacher-owned — certainty at the serious end without stripping teachers of everyday judgement. Dixons frames this as "warm-strict": scripted, uncompromising routines at the serious end plus deliberate relationship-building and disadvantage-conscious support built into daily practice ([Dixons culture](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/culture)). Michaela centralises further still, with minimal teacher discretion at any stage ([Michaela policy](https://michaela.education/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Behaviour-Policy-September-2025.pdf)).

**Scripts staff actually say**: "I've noticed you're finding it hard to get going. I need you to start question one. I know you can do it because [specific past success]. I'll come back and check in two minutes" — fixed pace, no raised voice, positioned at the pupil's level, not an invitation to argue in the moment ([Teachwire](https://www.teachwire.net/news/paul-dix-how-to-be-an-emotionally-consistent-teacher/); [restorative-conversation transcript](https://coachtestprep.s3.amazonaws.com/direct-uploads/user-176781/65b79918-8980-4a27-a612-e5ea1a659a30/Restorative%20Conversations%20Transcript.pdf)). Reset conversations after removal use a short 4-question script (What happened? Who was affected? What needs to happen? How do we put it right?), kept to 2–5 minutes so it repairs *on top of* the consequence rather than replacing it — echoing brief 04's finding that restorative-as-replacement has null/negative RCT evidence (LEIP) while restorative-as-add-on has small positive effects (Lancet/INCLUSIVE).

**Praise/reward economy.** Behaviour Hubs' final evaluation found the most-improved schools increased focus on rewarding positive behaviour, not harsher sanctions (brief 04). Operationally: a visible merit system tied to named behaviours ("resilience on that problem," not generic praise); a ≥4:1 positive:corrective target tracked informally; department-level layers (science "star of the week," postcards home) sitting under, not competing with, the whole-school system.

**On-call staffing and repeat-offence handling.** A rota of non- or partially-timetabled senior/pastoral staff covers each period so a C4 is never unanswered mid-lesson — a direct headcount cost that scales with C4 volume. A second C3/C4 in a rolling 2–4 week window triggers a pastoral/SENCO screen rather than automatic harsher sanction, echoing Timpson's finding (brief 04) that 78% of permanent exclusions go to pupils with SEN, in-need status or FSM.

## Numbers that matter

- **313,008** suspensions, **3,715** permanent exclusions in autumn term 2024 alone (down from 4,168 the prior autumn) — the base rate any removal infrastructure is sized against ([SecEd](https://www.sec-ed.co.uk/content/news/school-suspensions-autumn-2024-dfe-data); [FFT Datalab](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2025/09/exclusions-and-suspensions-in-2024-25/)).
- **FSM suspension rates run 4–5x non-FSM** (9.73 vs 2.11 per 100 in one snapshot; 26.66 vs 5.41 in brief 04's fuller 2024/25 DfE release) — the same gradient is highly likely to reproduce inside any internal removal system unless actively monitored (Thornton 2026: SEND/FSM ~2x more likely to be isolated, controlling for behaviour).
- **~7 minutes lost per 30 minutes of teaching** to low-level misbehaviour, unchanged since 2022 — the C1/C2 volume the ladder exists to suppress before any on-call trigger (National Behaviour Survey 2024/25, brief 04).
- **On-call response target of <5 minutes** is a widely cited practitioner norm with **no independent verification** — an operational aspiration to measure locally, not an evidenced benchmark.
- **26 July 2026**: DfE's new statutory guidance makes classroom removals a distinct, monitored category for the first time — a previously informal department log now feeds a formal, inspectable data trail ([HCR Law](https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/dfe-issues-new-statutory-guidance-on-suspensions-and-school-exclusions/)).
- **4:1–5:1 praise:reprimand ratio (~6 statements/15 min)** — a genuinely measurable, ABA-evidenced learning-walk metric ([synthesis](https://www.skillbuildersaba.com/blog/how-to-use-behavior-specific-praise-effectively)).
- **Restorative practice ceiling**: Lancet/INCLUSIVE found only a small bullying effect (−0.08), none on aggression — a ceiling-setting number against overselling a reset-room conversation alone (brief 04).

## Design implications for our department layer

1. **Adopt a 4-stage C1–C4 ladder**, teacher-owned at C1/C2, centrally-triggered from C3/C4 — the dominant national pattern; novelty raises training burden with no evidence upside.
2. **Science-specific safety override**: any lab-safety breach bypasses the ladder straight to removal regardless of warning count; document explicitly so it reads as principled, not inconsistent, to pupils and to Ofsted case-sampling.
3. **Staff the on-call rota to measured C4 volume, not intuition** — log every C3/C4 from day one (a statutory requirement from 26 July 2026 regardless) and size the rota/reset-room capacity off the first half-term's data.
4. **Keep the split deliberate**: teacher-owned certainty at the low end, centralised same-day at the high end — mirrors Dixons/Michaela and protects teacher time without diluting certainty where it matters.
5. **Publish an internal-removal equity dashboard** (C3/C4/on-call/reset-room data split by FSM/SEND/ethnicity/sex, half-termly to HoD and governors) — the national FSM/SEND removal disparity is highly likely to reproduce internally unless actively monitored.
6. **Script the language department-wide**: a fixed 30-second intervention script and a fixed 4-question reset script as standard text, reducing the inconsistency EEF and Bennett flag as the main driver of perceived unfairness.
7. **Layer department rewards under, not instead of, the whole-school system** — science-specific recognition should reinforce the whole-school merit economy.
8. **Hard-wire a SENCO/pastoral screen on second C3/C4 in any 2–4 week window** before further escalation — given Timpson, this needs department-level tracking, not a lagging whole-school process.
9. **Flag honestly**: the exact ladder shape, the 5-minute on-call target and the scripted intervention have no RCT evidence behind their precise configuration — adopted because they cohere with the causal evidence on consistency/routines/praise-weighting (brief 04) and reflect converged practice among the strongest named operators in comparable catchments, not because a trial has proven this exact design.
