# The School as More Than Lessons: Extended Services, Food, Enrichment, Attendance and Family Support

*Research brief 06 — for the design of a UK secondary/all-through school serving a high-deprivation, high-SEND catchment. Verified against the 2025–26 policy landscape (post-Curriculum & Assessment Review, Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, Ofsted report cards).*

## What the evidence says

**Extended school day.** The EEF Toolkit rates extending school time at **+2 months average progress** (moderate cost, moderate evidence security), with **disadvantaged pupils benefiting more — closer to +3 months** — and the strongest effects coming from *targeted* before- and after-school programmes rather than blanket longer hours ([EEF, Extending school time](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/extending-school-time)). The EEF's honest caveat: without additional funding these programmes are expensive and may not be cost-effective. The design implication is that *what fills the extra time* matters more than the extra time itself — structured academic support, enrichment with an intentional teaching component, and food.

**Breakfast.** The EEF/IFS Magic Breakfast evaluation (106 high-deprivation primaries, 8,600 pupils) found free universal before-school breakfast produced **~+2 months progress in KS1 reading, writing and maths (0.14–0.16 SD)**, plus improved behaviour and concentration — with the benefit appearing to come from *attending the club* (calm, supervised, social start to the day), not just the food ([EEF Magic Breakfast trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/magic-breakfast); [evaluation report](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/projects/Magic_Breakfast_report.pdf)). Honesty flag: a 2019 randomisation-code error meant the study was reclassified from RCT to control-group study, but IFS robustness checks upheld the KS1 conclusion ([EEF statement](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/eef-statement-republication-of-the-evaluation-of-school-breakfast-clubs)). Secondary-phase attainment evidence is thinner; the National School Breakfast Programme (primary and secondary) reported a **28% reduction in late marks** and widespread school-reported attendance and behaviour gains ([Family Action NSBP impact report](https://family-action.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/NSBP-final-impact-report.pdf)).

**Enrichment.** EEF Toolkit: **arts participation +3 months (low cost)**; **physical activity +2 months**, with academic benefit strongest when sport is combined with short structured literacy/numeracy teaching; **summer schools +3 months**, but expensive and best with an intensive small-group teaching component ([arts](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/arts-participation); [physical activity](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/physical-activity); [summer schools](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/summer-schools)). EEF frames enrichment as valuable in its own right for low-income pupils who could not otherwise afford it — a "guarantee" logic rather than purely an attainment one.

**Attendance.** This is the weakest evidence area relative to the scale of the problem. The EEF's rapid evidence assessment concluded most attendance interventions have **limited or inconsistent evidence and few high-quality studies** ([EEF evidence to Education Committee](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/118069/pdf/); [EEF blog on tailored approaches](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/taking-a-tailored-approach-to-improving-attendance)). A 2025 systematic review of secondary-phase interventions for persistent absence reached similar conclusions ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/child-and-adolescent-psychiatry/articles/10.3389/frcha.2025.1603680/full)). What has *practice-level* evidence: relational one-to-one mentoring (DfE/Barnardo's attendance mentors pilot supported pupils with anxiety, SEND and complex family circumstances, though Year 1 delivery was inconsistent — [Year 1 evaluation](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65fb20d3703c423c5158ef03/Evaluation_of_the_attendance_mentors_pilot_-_Year_1_findings.pdf)) and whole-family keyworkers (below). Punitive-only approaches lack support; the statutory framework itself is now "support first."

**Family support workers.** EEF rates parental engagement at **+4 months average**, but with lower impact for disadvantaged families using generic approaches — intensive, personalised liaison work is what shifts the dial ([EEF parental engagement](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/early-years/toolkit/parental-engagement)). School-Home Support's practitioner model (whole-family casework embedded in school) reports **58% of supported children improved attendance in 2024/25, by an average of 13 percentage points (~26 extra days), with the largest gains for severely absent children (~46 extra days)** ([SHS impact reports](https://www.schoolhomesupport.org.uk/our-impact-improving-persistent-absence/our-impact-reports)). Charity-reported, not RCT — treat as promising practice evidence.

**Trauma-informed/pastoral caution.** Whole-school trauma-informed approaches are popular but the empirical base is weak: a 2025 critical review found limited empirical support, and the YEF's UK trial of Trauma Informed Schools UK suffered attrition so severe no recommendation could be made ([Watson et al. 2025](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rev3.70025); [YEF evaluation](https://youthendowmentfund.org.uk/funding/evaluations/trauma-informed-schools-uk/)). Adopt the relational principles; do not buy the branded packages expecting measurable effects.

## What the best operators actually do

- **Reach Academy Feltham (all-through, cradle-to-career).** Runs the Reach Children's Hub alongside the school: antenatal/perinatal support, parenting programmes, early-years workforce training, careers support and community organising, co-locating services around one school community. Founding cohort achieved top-20 national GCSE results; 70% to university ([Reach cradle-to-career](https://reachacademyfeltham.com/cradle-to-career/); [Reach Foundation](https://www.reachfoundation.uk/organisation)). This is the strongest UK model of the school as neighbourhood infrastructure.
- **Oasis Community Learning.** Every academy paired with a Community Hub delivering food, youth work, adult education and family support, with safeguarding policy contextualised locally and hub staff feeding concerns into the academy pastoral/DSL structure ([Oasis Community Hubs](https://www.oasiscommunitylearning.org/community/community-hubs); [Oasis safeguarding](https://www.oasisuk.org/safeguarding/)). Oasis co-authored the 2025 model enrichment benchmarks with DofE and others ([Enrichment for All](https://oasisuk.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Enrichment-benchmarks_Final-DofE-Oasis-RA-BE-1.pdf)).
- **Dixons Academies Trust.** Extended compulsory day (~33 hours/week at some academies) with the extra time spent on co-curricular *electives* framed as cultural-capital entitlement for disadvantaged and SEND pupils, not just more lessons ([Dixons Broadgreen academy day](https://www.dixonsba.com/about/our-academy-day); [Dixons Trinity curriculum](https://www.dixonsta.com/about/curriculum)).
- **Barnardo's attendance mentors** (Middlesbrough, then Doncaster, Knowsley, Salford, Stoke): intensive 1:1 mentoring for persistently/severely absent pupils, working with the family on root causes; DfE expanded the approach with £15m into further areas ([DfE press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/major-national-drive-to-improve-school-attendance)).
- **School-Home Support**: embeds a full-time family support practitioner in high-need schools, doing home visits, benefits/housing casework and multi-agency coordination — the operational template for a family team ([SHS practitioner service](https://www.schoolhomesupport.org.uk/our-services/practitioner-service)).

**Current policy scaffolding (verify dates in any plan):** the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act received Royal Assent on 29 April 2026, creating a breakfast-club duty for schools with primary-aged pupils (relevant to an all-through school), uniform-cost limits, and children-not-in-school registers ([Bill page](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/3909)). *Working together to improve school attendance* became statutory on 19 August 2024, requiring an attendance policy, a Senior Attendance Champion at SLT level, and a support-first approach considering SEND/medical barriers before enforcement ([DfE](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/working-together-to-improve-school-attendance)). The DfE's non-statutory **Enrichment Framework** (eight benchmarks, five categories) feeds Ofsted's personal-development area from September 2026 and school profiles will publish enrichment offers ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/new-enrichment-benchmarks-what-schools-need-to-know/); [GovernorHub summary](https://schoolgovernors.thekeysupport.com/curriculum-and-pupils/curriculum/school-curriculum/dfe-publishes-new-enrichment-framework-and-benchmarks-summary/)). RISE attendance-and-behaviour hubs (90 lead schools supporting ~5,000 schools) exist but are thinly funded (£1.5m) — useful CPD, not a strategy ([DfE RISE hubs](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rise-attendance-and-behaviour-hubs-programme)).

## Design implications for our school

1. **Universal free breakfast for all phases, run as a club, not a canteen.** Draw down the national grant for primary-aged pupils and self-fund the secondary phase (~£1/pupil/day marginal); the mechanism is the calm supervised start, so design it with games/reading, staffed by pastoral staff who use it as a daily attendance touchpoint.
2. **Extended day (roughly 8am–5pm available, ~33h compulsory) with the extra time programmed, not padded**: Dixons-style electives satisfying the DfE enrichment benchmarks (arts, sport, outdoors, civic, life skills), plus targeted small-group academic support — because targeted before/after-school programmes are where the +2–3 months sits.
3. **An enrichment guarantee**: every pupil, every year, gets a funded instrument/performance opportunity, a competitive or physical activity, a residential/outdoor experience, and a civic project — tracked by pupil-level participation data (Ofsted will look for exactly this from September 2026, and it must reach FSM/SEND pupils first, not last).
4. **Be the HAF delivery site for the catchment**: bid into the LA's ~£200m/year programme so holiday provision (food + activities, 4 days/week across major holidays) runs on our site with our pastoral staff, keeping sight of high-risk children across the summer dip.
5. **A family team, not a lone worker**: embed School-Home-Support-style family practitioners (~1 FTE per 300–400 disadvantaged pupils) doing home visits, benefits/housing casework and early help coordination, line-managed within the safeguarding structure.
6. **Attendance built on relationships plus data**: Senior Attendance Champion at deputy-head level; daily first-response calls/home visits; 1:1 attendance mentors for the persistent/severe cohort (Barnardo's model); support-first plans that address SEND and anxiety barriers before any enforcement. Be honest internally that the causal evidence here is weak — so instrument everything and evaluate.
7. **Safeguarding-led pastoral architecture**: a DSL-led team integrating family practitioners, attendance mentors, counselling and the (NHS) Mental Health Support Team — push hard for MHST coverage given need-based prioritisation. Adopt relational/trauma-aware principles but avoid expensive branded trauma-informed packages with no outcome evidence.
8. **Plan for the FSM expansion**: from September 2026 all Universal Credit households qualify — auto-enrol aggressively, since FSM registration also drives Pupil Premium funding that pays for much of the above.

**Contested/weak evidence flagged:** months-of-progress conversions are disputed as a metric ([Jones critique](https://www.garyrjones.com/blog/2019/5/31/its-time-we-changed-converting-effect-sizes-to-months-of-learning-is-seriously-flawed)); the Magic Breakfast trial lost RCT status; attendance-intervention evidence is thin across the board; SHS and HAF figures are self-/programme-reported; trauma-informed whole-school evidence is weak; and secondary-phase breakfast attainment effects are unproven (behaviour/punctuality effects are better evidenced).
