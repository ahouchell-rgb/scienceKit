# Case study: King Solomon Academy & Ark Schools — all-through mastery, tutoring, and network design

## What the evidence says

**Ark is one of the few MATs with consistent, externally verified evidence of closing the disadvantage gap.** The Sutton Trust's *Chain Effects* series (2014–2018), which tracked disadvantaged pupils' attainment across 58 academy chains, found two-thirds of chains performed *below* the national average for disadvantaged pupils — but named Ark (with Harris) among the small group that "do very well across the board" year after year ([Sutton Trust, Chain Effects 2018](https://www.suttontrust.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/Chain-Effects-2018.pdf); [summary](https://www.suttontrust.com/news-opinion/all-news-opinion/best-academy-chains-outperform-mainstream-average-poorer-pupils-weakest-ones-fall-behind-new-sutton-trust-research/)). Ark's own network data claims pupil-premium pupils average Progress 8 of −0.09 vs −0.57 for disadvantaged pupils nationally — roughly half a grade more progress per subject ([Ark](https://arkonline.org/news/Progress8-Ark-schools-in-top-1percent)). Trust-published figures should be treated as promotional, but they are directionally consistent with the independent Sutton Trust analysis and DfE performance tables.

**The mastery curriculum evidence is real but modest.** Ark's Mathematics Mastery (Singapore/Shanghai-informed: concrete–pictorial–abstract, depth before breadth, whole-class progression) was tested in two large EEF randomised controlled trials: the secondary trial (7,712 pupils, 40 schools) found **+1 month** progress; the primary trial (5,108 pupils, 90 schools) found **+2 months**; pooled across 127 schools the effect is **~+1 month, statistically significant, 3–4 padlock security** ([EEF secondary trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/mathematics-mastery-secondary); [EEF primary trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/mathematics-mastery-primary); [EEF overview](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/mathematics-mastery)). Honest read: after one year the effect is small — mastery is not a silver bullet; its advocates argue effects compound over a full key stage, which the trials did not test. Ark's **English Mastery** did better in a later trial: after three years, **~+4 months** additional reading progress in the EEF/Accelerator Fund RCT of ~120 schools, evaluated by NatCen ([EEF English Mastery second trial](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/english-mastery-accelerator-fund); [Ark Curriculum Plus impact](https://www.arkcurriculumplus.org.uk/impact/)). The first English Mastery trial (2019) was disrupted by COVID and produced no robust impact estimate — Ark's marketing tends to cite the favourable trial.

**Small-group tutoring — a KSA/Ark staple — has strong toolkit evidence.** EEF: small-group tuition averages **+4 months** (reading +4, maths +3; biggest effects from ~3 sessions/week, up to an hour, over ~10 weeks, groups of 2–3, targeted at low attainers; FSM pupils benefit disproportionately) ([EEF small group tuition](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/small-group-tuition)). One-to-one tuition averages **+5 months** ([EEF one to one](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/one-to-one-tuition)). Note the National Tutoring Programme subsidy ended in August 2024, so any tutoring offer must now be funded from pupil premium / core budget.

**Caveats on KSA specifically.** There is no RCT of the KSA model; its results are observational. Plausible confounds: a small, single-site secondary intake (60/year) with highly committed leadership; London effect (Westminster schools generally outperform); possible intake and attrition effects common to oversubscribed academies (Sutton Trust has shown top-performing schools are often socially selective relative to catchment — though KSA's ~62–67% FSM intake substantially blunts that critique) ([Sutton Trust admissions research](https://www.suttontrust.com/school-admissions-dashboard/); [FSM figure](https://snobe.co.uk/schools/ark-king-solomon-academy)). Replication of KSA-level results across the rest of the Ark network has been partial, not universal.

## What the best operators actually do

**King Solomon Academy (all-through, Westminster; one of London's most deprived wards; ~62–67% FSM):**
- **All-through 3–18 on one site**, secondary intake deliberately small: **60 pupils per year (two classes of 30)** vs 90 in primary; every child known by name, families known for a decade ([KSA admissions](https://kingsolomonacademy.org/admissions/secondary); [Ark](https://arkonline.org/news-item/ark-king-solomon-academy-ranked-in-top-five-schools-in-the-country-for-progress/)).
- **University-facing identity engineering**: classes named after university cities; each cohort named for its Year 13 graduation year ("Class of 2031"); Year 6 spends a week in Oxford attending lectures ([KSA](https://kingsolomonacademy.org/about/welcome); [Good Schools Guide](https://www.goodschoolsguide.co.uk/uk-schools/review/ark-king-solomon-academy)).
- **Depth before breadth**: heavily weighted English and maths time in KS3 (Ark schools have run 5–6 hours of each per week), a slightly narrower GCSE diet done well, extended day (Ark norm 8:30–16:30 at secondary), longer contact time repaid by paying staff ~2.5% above scale ([DfE sponsor profile, 2014](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75647fe5274a1baf95e36a/ARK_Schools_Case_Study_FINAL_APPROVED.pdf)).
- **First-cohort proof point (2014)**: 93% 5 A*–C including English & maths, 75% EBacc, in "the poorest ward in London" — top-10 nationally ([Tes](https://www.tes.com/magazine/archive/how-we-achieved-dramatically-good-gcse-results-poorest-ward-london)). Sustained since: Progress 8 **+1.06 to +1.18** in recent years, top five schools nationally for progress, Ofsted Outstanding in every category twice (most recently Dec 2023, pre-report-card framework) ([Ark](https://arkonline.org/news-item/ark-king-solomon-academy-ranked-in-top-five-schools-in-the-country-for-progress/); [Ofsted](https://reports.ofsted.gov.uk/provider/28/135242); [Ark, Outstanding again](https://arkonline.org/news-item/ofsted-rates-arks-king-solomon-academy-outstanding-a-second-time/)).
- **Alumni/destinations**: KSA defines success as a "top-third university or high-quality apprenticeship" at 18, publishes destination data by cohort, runs an Oxbridge programme and Realising Opportunities partnerships (Warwick, Nottingham, KCL, Manchester), and consistently places 5–10% of each sixth-form cohort in competitive apprenticeships ([KSA destinations](https://kingsolomonacademy.org/curriculum/careers-destinations/destinations); [KSA university prep](https://kingsolomonacademy.org/curriculum/careers-destinations/preparing-for-university)). 2025 GCSEs: 69% grade 5+ in English & maths ([KSA news](https://www.kingsolomonacademy.org/news/class-2025-celebrate-gcse-success)).

**Ark network (39 schools, ~30,000 pupils in London, Birmingham, Portsmouth, Hastings; expanding north to ~48 schools via the Pontefract merger with a new northern hub):** ([Ark impact](https://arkonline.org/impact/); [Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/ark-schools-to-set-up-northern-hub-after-mat-merger/))

*What Ark centralises:*
- **Mission and the "six pillars"** every school must sign up to: high expectations; excellent teaching; exemplary behaviour; depth before breadth; more time for learning; knowing every child ([DfE sponsor profile](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75647fe5274a1baf95e36a/ARK_Schools_Case_Study_FINAL_APPROVED.pdf)).
- **Governance**: the Ark Schools board is the legal governing body of every school; LGBs are advisory sub-committees with no statutory powers, an Ark-appointed chair, and a central-team representative on each (same source).
- **Curriculum**: Ark Curriculum Plus supplies full curriculum sequences, lesson resources, CPD and assessments for maths, English, science, geography, history — created explicitly because teachers were wasting hours making resources and the trust couldn't compare performance without a shared sequence ([Ark Curriculum Plus](https://www.arkcurriculumplus.org.uk/about-us/); [programmes](https://www.arkcurriculumplus.org.uk/programmes/)).
- **Data and assessment**: common definitions, common assessments on a common calendar, common measures, common dashboards; a single data warehouse ("enter once, use many times"); standardised KS1/KS2 tests and curriculum-aligned network tests at KS3, giving ~2,000-pupil year-group benchmarks. Crucially, Ark *cut* summative assessment from six to two–three windows a year and automated marks processing to reduce workload ([Ark data blog](https://arkonline.org/blog/how-make-data-work-network-schools)).
- **Quality assurance**: half-termly low-notice monitoring visits led by the Director of Education with peer principals and ex-HMI; termly attainment and finance reports; principals line-managed by education directors ([DfE sponsor profile](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a75647fe5274a1baf95e36a/ARK_Schools_Case_Study_FINAL_APPROVED.pdf)).
- **People pipeline**: own ITT provider, 10 days CPD/teacher/year, and leadership pipelines (Future Leaders, Teaching Leaders — later merged into Ambition Institute) (same source).
- **Back office**: finance, HR, IT, admissions/exclusions policy, internal audit.

*What Ark devolves:* school identity, ethos and "distinct character" (Lucy Heller: no "cookie cutter"); day-to-day pedagogy within the shared curriculum; staffing decisions; customisable vs mandatory policies are explicitly labelled in the ARKepedia leaders' handbook; and **earned autonomy** — heads gain freedom as results are sustained, with the centre dropping to a monitoring role and re-intervening on decline (same source). Heller has argued publicly that tighter trusts outperform, *given central capacity* ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/top-chief-executives-reveal-what-makes-a-successful-academy-trust/)).

## Design implications for our school

1. **Go all-through (3/4–18) on one site or a tight cluster.** A decade of relationship with each family is KSA's core mechanism for a deprived, high-SEND intake: early identification, no Y6→Y7 transition cliff (a known risk point for SEND pupils), and cumulative culture.
2. **Keep secondary year groups small — 60–120, not 240.** "Knowing every child" is structural at 60/year. For SEND pupils this matters more than almost anything: fewer adults, deeper knowledge, stable relationships.
3. **Depth before breadth, protected core time.** 5–6 hours/week each of English and maths in KS3, a slightly narrower KS4 offer done to a high standard. This is the pillar most protective of pupils arriving below age-related expectations.
4. **Adopt, don't write, the core curriculum** — buy in a mastery-sequenced curriculum (Ark Curriculum Plus or equivalent) and spend the saved teacher hours on coaching and adaptation for SEND. Expect modest test-score effects (+1–4 months) but large workload and consistency gains.
5. **Build small-group tutoring into the timetable and budget** (post-NTP, self-funded): groups of ≤3, 3×/week, ~10-week blocks, tied to the curriculum, targeted by data at low attainers — the highest-confidence intervention in the EEF toolkit for our demographic.
6. **Extend the day — and pay for it.** 8:30–16:30 with enrichment (music for all, residentials, university visits) narrows the cultural-capital gap; budget ~2.5% salary uplift as Ark does rather than relying on goodwill.
7. **Centralise data the Ark way, not the caricature way**: common assessments, 2–3 windows a year, automated marking analytics, network benchmarks — and *ban* additional local data collections. Data volume is not rigour.
8. **Identity engineering from Reception**: name cohorts by university/destination graduation year; define an explicit destination measure (top-third university *or* high-quality apprenticeship) and publish it annually.
9. **Earned autonomy governance**: if part of a trust, insist on labelled mandatory-vs-customisable policies, an advisory LGB with genuine local/SEND parent voice, and low-notice peer review visits rather than data-only oversight.
10. **Verify against the 2025–26 landscape**: KSA's "Outstanding" predates the November 2025 Ofsted report cards; single-word grades are gone, so design for the new multi-domain evaluation (inclusion and attendance now more visible), and note the free-schools route for opening a KSA-style new school is currently paused — expansion now runs through trust mergers, as Ark's own Pontefract deal shows.
