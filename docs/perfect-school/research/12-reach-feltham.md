# Case study 12: Reach Academy Feltham and the Reach Foundation cradle-to-career model

Reach Academy Feltham (Hounslow, west London) is the most complete UK attempt at a "cradle-to-career" school: a deliberately small, all-through 2–18 free school wrapped in a children's/family hub, a teacher-training arm, and a post-18 support pipeline, all run by a school trust (Reach Schools) plus a sister charity (The Reach Foundation). It opened in 2012 with just Reception and Year 7; in September 2024 it was judged Outstanding in all six Ofsted categories — under the post-2024 framework in which Ofsted no longer gives an overall effectiveness grade — making it one of the strongest-performing non-selective schools in a disadvantaged catchment in England ([Ofsted 2024 summary](https://reachacademyfeltham.com/ofsted-2024/); [Ofsted provider page](https://reports.ofsted.gov.uk/provider/28/138266)).

## What the evidence says

- **The underlying theory: schools are "necessary but not sufficient."** The Reach Foundation's stated premise is that great schools alone cannot close the opportunity gap; children need a connected system of support from pregnancy to employment ([Reach Foundation](https://www.reachfoundation.uk/)). This is explicitly modelled on the Harlem Children's Zone (HCZ). The best causal evidence for that model is Dobbie & Fryer's lottery-based study of HCZ's Promise Academy: middle-school entrants gained ~0.23 SD/year in maths (reading ~0.05 SD/year), enough to close the Black–white maths gap in NYC ([Dobbie & Fryer paper](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/5-dobbie-fryer-paper.pdf); [GiveWell analysis](https://blog.givewell.org/2009/05/08/fryer-and-dobbie-on-harlem-childrens-zone-what-they-found/)). **Important caveat:** Dobbie & Fryer found the school itself drove most of the measured gains; they could not isolate additional impact from the wrap-around "zone" services. The causal case for the wrap-around layer, as distinct from a high-quality school, remains weaker than advocates imply — flag this honestly.
- **Family/parental engagement has a solid evidence base.** The EEF Early Years Toolkit rates parental engagement at roughly **+5 months** additional progress, low cost, extensive evidence (94 studies) ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/early-years/toolkit/parental-engagement)). Reach's perinatal, parenting (Family Links, PEEP, Families Connect) and early-learning programmes sit squarely on this evidence.
- **All-through 2–18 structure per se is under-evidenced.** There is no robust UK effect-size literature showing all-through schools outperform; the argument is mechanistic (no KS2–KS3 transition dip, 11+ years of relationship data on each child, coherent curriculum). Reach's results are consistent with the mechanism but cannot prove it — the school also benefits from exceptional teaching quality and oversubscription.
- **Policy tailwind (2025–26).** The Labour government's *Best Start Family Hubs* programme funds a hub in **every local authority by April 2026**, up to 1,000 hubs by end-2028, backed by ~£500m (within a £900m package including £200m for SEND practitioners in every hub), with 70% of hubs targeted at the 30% most disadvantaged communities ([DfE, gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-revives-family-services-supporting-500000-more-kids); [SEND support in hubs](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/access-to-early-send-support-through-best-start-family-hubs)). A new school can now plan to *host or co-locate* a state-funded family hub rather than fundraise the whole thing philanthropically, as Reach had to.

## What the best operators actually do

- **Deliberately small, all-through design.** Reach was founded (Ed Vainker, Rebecca Cramer, Jon McGoh) as a **one-form-plus school: ~60 pupils per year group, Nursery (age 2) to Year 13**, on a single site — a constraint chosen so every child is known for 11+ years and personalisation survives the transition points where most systems drop it ([Our story](https://reachacademyfeltham.com/our-story/); [Scholato interview](https://www.scholato.com/what-did-ed-vainker-do/)). Total roll ~900 (898 pupils; 456 boys/442 girls) ([snobe school data](https://snobe.co.uk/schools/reach-academy-feltham)).
- **Relationships engineered in, not hoped for:** home visits for all new pupils, family support services, and a phased careers curriculum from early years to sixth form benchmarked against all eight Gatsby Benchmarks with a named careers lead and Unifrog for every KS3/4 pupil ([Cradle to career page](https://reachacademyfeltham.com/cradle-to-career/)).
- **Reach Children's Hub (2017)** — the wrap-around layer, run by the Reach Foundation, not the school budget: perinatal support (0–4, incl. NCT Birth & Beyond peer support for new mothers), early-years workforce training, parenting programmes (Family Links, PEEP, Families Connect), the Feltham Early Learning Community with Save the Children, community organising, a Girls' Group for vulnerable pupils, and **Feltham Futures** (careers/HE progression into early adulthood) ([Hub programmes](https://children.reach.lets-go.live/our-work); [Hounslow Connect listing](https://hounslowconnect.com/services/reach-childrens-hub)).
- **Post-16 innovation:** Feltham College, a sixth form opened in 2022 at the academy **in partnership with two leading independent schools (Hampton School and Lady Eleanor Holles)**, graded Outstanding in 2024 ([Feltham College](https://felthamcollege.com/2024/10/16/ofsted-2024/)).
- **Teacher pipeline:** Reach Teacher Training runs classroom-based primary and secondary ITT (QTS via South West Teacher Training SCITT, PGCE with the University of Exeter) — trainees are in school daily ([Reach Teacher Training](https://reachteachertraining.com); [school ITT page](https://reachacademyfeltham.com/teacher-training/)). Reach won the 2026 Global Schools Prize for Teacher Development ([Reach Schools](https://reachschools.uk/teacher-development-award-winner/)).
- **Replication, carefully:** a second small all-through school, Reach Academy Hanworth Park, opened September 2024 (funding agreement June 2024, just before the free-school pipeline review) ([RAHP](https://reachacademyhanworthpark.com/rahp-our-story/)). Nationally, the Foundation runs a **Cradle-to-Career Partnership with ~30 school trusts** designing place-based versions of the model ([Reach Foundation C2C](https://www.reach-c2c.org/cradle-career-school-partnerships-0)); some secondary sources claim ~70 place-based clusters touching 500+ schools — treat the larger figures as unverified marketing-adjacent numbers.
- **Genuine SEND inclusion at intake.** In one recent Year 7, **9 of 60 entrants (15%) had EHCPs** — roughly 3–5x the mainstream secondary norm — including children moving from specialist provision; the school has also, with parents, "handed back" two EHCPs when no longer needed, and has spent up to double the funding attached to an EHCP on a child's provision ([Schools Week interview with Ed Vainker](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/should-you-hand-back-an-ehcp-if-you-think-the-child-no-longer-needs-it/)).

## Numbers that matter (effect sizes, costs, ratios, outcomes)

- **School size:** ~60 pupils/year group × 16 year groups (age 2–18); ~900 on roll. Contrast: typical secondary ~180–240/year.
- **Disadvantage profile:** 35.6% FSM; 58% EAL; 14.2% SEN support ([snobe/DfE-derived data](https://snobe.co.uk/schools/reach-academy-feltham)); ~15% EHCP in a recent Y7 intake (Schools Week, above).
- **Outcomes:** Progress 8 **+1.11 in 2017** (16th nationally, first cohort); **0.50 in 2023** (a notable dip year worth remembering — even flagship schools fluctuate); **~+0.85–0.97 in 2024**, with 83% grade 4+ and 71% grade 5+ in English & maths ([2024 results](https://reachschools.uk/reach-academy-felthams-first-cohort-completes-their-gcses/)); **2025: best-ever results, 44.8% of grades at 7+** ([2025 results](https://reachacademyfeltham.com/gcses-2025/)). 2023 A-level: average grade B, 30% AAB+.
- **Ofsted:** Outstanding in all six judgement areas, Sept 2024 — first all-through free school rated Outstanding (2014) and repeated it a decade later.
- **Wrap-around cost:** the Mohn Westlake Foundation grant for the Reach Children's Hub is **£3.36m over 8 years (2020–2027)** — roughly **£420k/year** ([Mohn Westlake Foundation](https://www.themohnwestlakefoundation.co.uk/current-grants/creating-a-new-support-hub-for-children-young-people-and-families-in-feltham/)). The Reach Foundation's total income FY2025 was **£4.24m** (expenditure £3.08m; 30 staff; £403k from government contracts) ([Charity Commission, charity 1129683](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/charity-details/?regid=1129683&subid=0)). So the full national-plus-local operation costs ~£3–4m/year on top of normal school funding — but the *local hub layer alone* is nearer £0.4–1m/year, of an order a school could now partly cover via Best Start Family Hub co-location.
- **Benchmark effect sizes:** HCZ Promise Academy ~0.23 SD/yr maths (middle school); EEF parental engagement ~+5 months (early years, low cost).

## Design implications for our school

1. **Adopt the "necessary but not sufficient" architecture but fund it separately:** a linked charity/foundation carrying the hub, so wrap-around services never raid the core school budget. Reach's split (trust = school; foundation = hub, ~£3–4m/yr philanthropy + contracts) is the template.
2. **Bid to host a Best Start Family Hub on site.** The 2025–26 rollout (every LA by April 2026; SEND practitioners funded in every hub) means our deprived catchment is exactly where DfE wants hubs; co-location converts Reach's philanthropic model into a substantially state-funded one.
3. **Go all-through 2–18 and small (1–2 forms of entry per phase).** The honest rationale is relational continuity and transition-proofing, not proven effect sizes — say so in the business case, but Reach shows it is compatible with top-1% outcomes at 35%+ FSM.
4. **Start from birth, not from Reception:** perinatal peer support, 2-year-old nursery places, parenting programmes (PEEP/Family Links) — the strongest-evidenced, cheapest layer (+5 months, EEF).
5. **Design SEND inclusion into admissions**, Reach-style: plan for ~15% EHCP intake, accept that provision may cost more than the EHCP funding attached, and build the confidence mechanism (agreed review/transition routes) that lets you say yes to children leaving specialist provision.
6. **Own the teacher pipeline** (school-based ITT with a SCITT/university partner) — small schools in tough catchments cannot rely on the open labour market.
7. **Extend the pipeline past 18** (Feltham Futures equivalent) and consider a sixth form in partnership with strong local independent or selective institutions.
8. **Hold the humility:** the wrap-around layer's *independent* causal impact is unproven (Dobbie & Fryer), Reach's P8 has swung from 1.11 to 0.50 and back, and Reach itself is only now replicating (one second school, opened 2024). Build measurement in from day one.
