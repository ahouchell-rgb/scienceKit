# Assessment & Accountability: Ofsted Report Cards, Progress 8 and Deprivation, and Internal Assessment Design (2025–26)

Research brief for a UK secondary/all-through school serving a high-deprivation, high-SEND catchment. Verified against the post-2024 policy landscape (Labour government, Curriculum & Assessment Review, Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act, Ofsted renewed framework).

## What the evidence says

**Ofsted has been transformed, but the deprivation penalty has not disappeared.** Single headline grades were scrapped in September 2024; from 10 November 2025 routine inspections use the renewed framework with **report cards**: a 5-point scale ("urgent improvement", "needs attention", "expected standard", "strong standard", "exceptional") across six core evaluation areas — inclusion; curriculum and teaching; achievement; attendance and behaviour; personal development and wellbeing; leadership and governance — plus a separate met/not-met safeguarding judgement ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ofsted-confirms-changes-to-education-inspection-and-unveils-new-look-report-cards)). Early outcomes replicate the old pattern: of 1,803 full inspections by 30 April 2026 (921 published report cards), schools with above-average FSM were almost three times as likely to be graded below "expected standard" for achievement, and Ofsted itself has conceded "a relationship between the level of disadvantage in a school and the school's grade for the 'achievement' evaluation area" ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/ofsted-admits-relationship-between-disadvantage-and-achievement-judgment/); [Ofsted statistical commentary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/school-inspections-statistical-commentaries-2025-to-2026/the-achievement-grade-attainment-data-and-schools-in-challenging-circumstances)). NAHT analysis found high-SEND schools twice as likely to get "needs attention" for attendance and behaviour (20% vs 9%) — a warning that the new **inclusion** judgement can become a "poisoned chalice" that penalises genuinely inclusive schools ([Schools Week op-ed](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/ofsteds-ill-considered-inclusion-judgment-is-a-poisoned-chalice/)). In response, from **September 2026** Ofsted introduces a "similar schools" contextual comparison model (built with DfE), judges progress "from starting points", and requires schools to publish an **inclusion strategy** ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/ofsted-the-key-changes-to-school-inspection-from-september-2026/); [Ofsted blog](https://educationinspection.blog.gov.uk/2026/06/12/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-september-2026-updates-to-education-inspections/)).

**Progress 8 is fairer than raw attainment but still biased against deprived intakes — and it is temporarily dead.** P8 controls only for KS2 prior attainment, so schools with more disadvantaged pupils systematically score lower: the 2024 disadvantage gap was ~0.7 P8 points, and FFT Datalab has long shown that contextualising P8 (FSM, EAL, SEND, mobility, month of birth) materially reranks schools — the number below the −0.5 floor falls from ~300 to ~240 ([FFT: Contextualising Progress 8](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2024/05/contextualising-progress-8/); [FFT: taking account of context](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2019/10/solutions-to-problems-with-progress-8-part-two-taking-account-of-context/)). Pupil absence — much higher in deprived cohorts — also drags P8 down mechanically ([FFT](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2023/07/the-impact-of-absence-on-progress-8/)). Critically, **no P8 can be calculated for the 2025 and 2026 GCSE cohorts** (KS2 tests were cancelled in 2020–21); it returns for 2026/27, and Bristol researchers warn any proxy is a poor substitute ([PolicyBristol](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/policybristol/policy-briefings/progress-8-2025-6/); [FFT: So. Farewell then Progress 8](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2024/12/so-farewell-then-progress-8/)). Following the Curriculum & Assessment Review (5 Nov 2025), government **scrapped the EBacc** and proposed a reformed P8/A8: double-weighted English and maths, two science slots, and four "breadth" slots (vocational qualifications confined to two of them), with consultation response due summer 2026 for 2027-28 option choices ([DfE explainer](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690a8e5388a98da87e2922cb/Progress_8_and_Attainment_8_-_an_explanation_of_the_proposed_improved_model.pdf); [government response](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report-government-response); [FFT analysis](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2025/11/what-difference-will-the-proposed-changes-to-attainment-8-and-progress-8-make/)). The Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act ends automatic academy orders for struggling maintained schools; RISE teams now lead intervention ([NGA](https://www.nga.org.uk/news-views/directory/from-the-commons-to-the-classroom-what-you-need-to-know-about-the-children-s-wellbeing-and-schools-act/)).

**High-stakes assessment corrupts teaching; formative and low-stakes assessment improves it.** UK evidence links high-stakes accountability to curriculum narrowing, drill-and-practice, and lower sets being denied untested content — harms that fall hardest on SEND and low-attaining pupils ([Commons Education Committee](https://committees.parliament.uk/committee/203/education-committee/news/102666/highstakes-testing-harming-teaching-and-learning-in-primary-schools/); [Chartered College](https://my.chartered.college/impact_article/english-and-mathematics-teachers-perceptions-of-how-high-stakes-gcse-examinations-are-shaping-relationships-with-their-learners-in-one-secondary-school-in-england/)). Conversely, the EEF's *Embedding Formative Assessment* RCT (140 schools, 25,000 pupils) delivered **+2 months on Attainment 8 (ES ≈ 0.10)** at very low cost, with the biggest gains for the lowest prior-attainment third ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/embedding-formative-assessment); [UCL IoE](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/ioe/news/2022/mar/embedding-formative-assessment-leads-higher-attainment)). The EEF Toolkit rates feedback as high-impact/very-low-cost (~+6 months), with guidance stressing formatively useful feedback and avoiding workload-heavy marking ([EEF feedback guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/feedback)).

## What the best operators actually do

- **Dixons Academies** (high-deprivation, top-decile P8 trust): daily formative assessment — targeted questioning, low-stakes quizzing, Show Call, whole-class "messy marking" with green-pen response. Crucially, in Y7–Y10 they report **raw scores plus academy averages, never GCSE grade predictions**, only introducing grades once students sit full past papers — explicitly to avoid spurious flightpaths and teaching-to-the-test ([Dixons Trinity Chapeltown assessment](https://www.dixonstc.com/about/assessment); [powerful feedback](https://www.dixonstc.com/why/powerful-feedback)).
- **United Learning** runs trust-wide **twice-yearly standardised assessments in Y7–8 across six subjects** (English, maths, science, history, geography, RS), nationally benchmarked with percentile ranks and question-level analysis via Smartgrade — now offered free to any secondary ([United Curriculum assessment](https://unitedcurriculum.org.uk/assessment); [Smartgrade](https://www.smartgrade.co.uk/blog/bringing-powerful-assessment-to-every-school)).
- Strong trusts standardise the *instrument*, not the teaching: NFER and GL Assessment trust packages give comparable standardised scores across schools to spot gaps and target intervention without per-lesson data drops ([NFER MAT tests](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/for-schools/nfer-assessments/nfer-tests/nfer-tests-for-multi-academy-trusts/); [GL for trusts](https://www.gl-assessment.co.uk/academy-trusts)). CAT4-style baselines are also the leading candidate replacement baseline while KS2-based P8 is suspended ([FFT](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2025/08/calculating-a-key-stage-4-progress-measure-using-cat4-in-place-of-key-stage-2/)).
- **Comparative judgement** (No More Marking) for writing: ~1m scripts judged; scale reliability up to 0.96 with anchoring that prevents schools inflating their own pupils' scores — a workload-light, standardised alternative to rubric marking ([Assessment in Education study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/0969594X.2019.1700212); [No More Marking](https://www.nomoremarking.com/products/aaw)).

**Evidence caveats.** Contextualised P8 remains contested: DfE has repeatedly declined to publish it, arguing it entrenches low expectations, and effects are correlational. The EFA effect (0.10) was significant only at the 10% level in the primary model. Early report-card patterns are based on part-year data (first inspections skewed towards volunteer schools). Destinations data lag ~2 years and "sustained destination" says nothing about destination *quality*; LEO earnings data are even slower.

## Design implications for our school

1. **Build the Ofsted case around inclusion and context from day one.** Publish a strong inclusion strategy (mandatory from Sept 2026), track SEND/PP/social-care cohorts' attendance, suspensions and progress separately, and pre-empt the "similar schools" model by benchmarking ourselves against high-FSM comparators — our achievement narrative must be "progress from starting points", evidenced internally, because raw national comparisons will read as "needs attention".
2. **Expect to operate without Progress 8 until 2027 and under a redesigned P8 thereafter.** Curriculum planning for the 2027-28 KS4 options must anticipate double English/maths, two science slots, four breadth slots and no EBacc constraint — this frees space for arts/vocational courses our cohort values, but only two vocational slots will count.
3. **Run one standardised, nationally benchmarked low-stakes assessment window per term (max twice-yearly per subject at KS3)**, using instruments like United Learning/Smartgrade, NFER or GL, giving standardised scores and question-level analysis — never teacher-predicted GCSE grades or flightpaths at KS3 (Dixons model: raw scores + cohort averages only until full past papers in Y10/11).
4. **Invest in formative assessment as the core assessment system**: daily low-stakes retrieval quizzing, whole-class feedback, EFA-style teacher learning communities — cheap, evidence-backed (+2 months, strongest for low attainers), and workload-reducing versus deep-marking policies.
5. **Use comparative judgement for extended writing** across the school to get reliable, standardised writing data without rubric-driven teaching-to-the-mark-scheme.
6. **Adopt destinations as a headline internal KPI**: target closing the 11.4pp sustained-destination gap, with named tracking of every Y11/Y13 leaver for 12+ months, because destinations are the outcome measure least distorted by intake and most meaningful for a deprived catchment.
7. **Institute an internal "no teaching-to-the-test" guardrail**: full-breadth curriculum entitlement for all sets, no early entry to game measures, and governor-level review of any assessment practice that narrows the taught curriculum for lower attainers.
