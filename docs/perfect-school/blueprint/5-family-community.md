# Section 5 — Family, Community and Cradle-to-Career

A great school is necessary but not sufficient to close the opportunity gap in a high-deprivation, high-SEND catchment (see research/12-reach-feltham.md). The quantitative case is now sharper: multilevel studies find schools explain only ~10–15% of variance in pupil attainment (~14% of Progress 8), and EPI/EEF analysis shows **44% of the GCSE disadvantage gap is already locked in by age 11** (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md; [EEF/EPI](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/disadvantage-gap-locked-in-new-research-shows)). Reach Academy Feltham — Outstanding in all six areas in 2024 at 35.6% FSM — proves a school can act on that out-of-school share: it wraps the school in a cradle-to-career system running from a 2-year-old nursery to post-18 careers support. We adopt that architecture, but with a discipline the evidence demands: **fund the wrap-around layer separately from the core school budget, and be honest that its independent causal impact is unproven.** Dobbie & Fryer found the school itself, not the surrounding "zone", drove most of the Harlem Children's Zone gains (see research/12-reach-feltham.md). So we build the family and community layer where the evidence is genuinely strong — parental engagement, early years, food, attendance — and instrument the rest.

## Governance and money: a linked foundation plus a state-funded hub

We split the architecture exactly as Reach does: the trust runs the school; a **linked charity ("[School] Foundation") runs the community hub** on separate philanthropic and contract income — **£0.4–1.0m/year off-GAG, ring-fenced** (Section 0 canon) — so wrap-around services never raid teaching budgets. Reach's local hub layer costs ~£0.4–1m/year (its Mohn Westlake grant is £3.36m over 8 years, ~£420k/yr; total Foundation income £4.24m in FY2025) (see research/12-reach-feltham.md).

Crucially, the 2025–26 policy window lets us convert most of that philanthropy into state funding. We **bid to host a Best Start Family Hub on site**: the DfE programme puts a hub in every LA by April 2026, up to 1,000 hubs by end-2028, ~£500m within a £900m package that funds a SEND practitioner in every hub, with 70% of hubs targeted at the most deprived 30% of areas — which is precisely our catchment ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-revives-family-services-supporting-500000-more-kids); [SEND in hubs](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/access-to-early-send-support-through-best-start-family-hubs)). Co-location means the state pays for the perinatal and early-help spine; the Foundation adds the marginal, catchment-specific layer on top. One timescale lesson is written into the governance documents: London Challenge worked over ~10 years while the five-year Opportunity Areas showed no attainment impact, so the Foundation's funding commitments and KPIs are framed for a decade, with leading indicators (attendance, reading age, oracy) from year one (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md).

## Start from birth: early years and pre-school links

We go **all-through 2–18**: a ~50-place funded 2-year-old nursery, a 2FE primary (60/year, 420 pupils), a 6FE secondary (180/year, 900 pupils) and a 150-place sixth form — ~1,520 on roll at statutory age, ~1,570 including nursery (Section 0 canon). The honest rationale is relational continuity and transition-proofing — earlier SEND identification and up to 16 years of data on every child — not a proven effect size, and the business case should say so (see research/12-reach-feltham.md). Two honesty clauses attach. First, because a 2FE primary feeds a 6FE secondary, **only the internal third of each Year 7 escapes the Year 6→7 cliff; two-thirds (120 of 180) arrive from external primaries**, and the answer for them is the enhanced transition programme (summer visits, transition passports, SEND data handover, induction bootcamp — Section 0 canon), not all-through structure. Second, **nursery attendance confers no Reception place**: families apply through the normal admissions round, which blocks backdoor selection. Reach shows the model is compatible with top-1% outcomes at 35%+ FSM, and the "44% locked in by 11" finding is the single strongest argument for starting at 2 rather than 4 (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md).

The early-years layer is the **cheapest, best-evidenced investment in the whole blueprint**: EEF rates parental engagement at ~+5 months, low cost, 94 studies (see research/12-reach-feltham.md; [EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/early-years/toolkit/parental-engagement)). We commit to:

- **~50 funded 2-year-old nursery places** and perinatal peer support (NCT Birth & Beyond model), run through the Best Start hub.
- **Structured parenting programmes** — PEEP, Family Links, Families Connect — the specific named programmes Reach uses, not generic "workshops".
- **A universal language screen at Reception and at Y7 entry**, feeding the in-house speech-and-language therapy commissioned in the SEND design (detail in research/03-send-evidence.md) — because ~1 in 5 UK children arrive behind on language, concentrated in poverty. This is the family-facing front end of the school's oracy and vocabulary strategy — and it now runs ahead of national policy, since the Curriculum & Assessment Review has made oracy its first recommendation with a national framework coming (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md, research/02-curriculum-design.md).

## Parental engagement that actually works in deprived areas

EEF rates parental engagement at +4 months on average, **but with lower impact for disadvantaged families using generic approaches — only intensive, personalised liaison shifts the dial** (see research/06-extended-services.md; [EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/early-years/toolkit/parental-engagement)). So we reject newsletters-and-parents'-evenings as the strategy and commit to:

- **A home visit for every new family** at every phase entry (Reach practice) — relationships engineered in, not hoped for (protocols in the safeguarding subsection below).
- **One named adult per child across phases**, and daily positive contact home. We import Dixons' Morning Meeting mechanic of staff-given "appreciations" pushed to families the same day (see research/13-dixons.md) — cheap, high-frequency positive parental contact, the opposite of the deficit call-home pattern poor families usually experience. The Behaviour Hubs evaluation backs the direction: the most-improved schools increased their focus on rewarding positive behaviour, not harsher sanctions (see research/04-behaviour-culture.md).
- **Parental text-messaging on homework and tests** — the single cheapest evidenced win available, ~+1 month in maths at ≤£6/pupil/year (see research/09-edtech-ai.md), deployed before any five-figure platform.
- **Aggressive FSM auto-enrolment — with honest accounting.** From September 2026 all Universal Credit households qualify for FSM, worth ~£500/child/year to families, and each registration adds the NFF FSM factor (~£295–£505/pupil) (see research/06-extended-services.md). But **Pupil Premium is not paid on the expanded eligibility**: DfE has confirmed PP stays tied to the pre-2026 targeted (≤£7,400/Ever-6) cohort, worth **~£0.48m across phases** (~126 primary pupils × ~£1,480; ~270 secondary × £1,075 — Section 0 canon), not the ~£580k a 45%-FSM6 calculation would imply. We still treat auto-enrolment as core business — for family income and the FSM factor — but the family offer is budgeted against the targeted-PP and Foundation lines. And because untied PP money shows little evidence of narrowing the gap by itself, while the National Tutoring Programme decayed to ~+1 month once targeting slipped, **every PP-funded intervention here carries named pupils, dosage tracking and a kill criterion** (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md).

## A family support team, not a lone worker

The dominant family-facing need in this catchment is casework — housing, benefits, early help, and the barriers behind absence. We build a **team, not a token worker: ~1 FTE School-Home-Support-style family practitioner per 300–400 disadvantaged pupils** (see research/06-extended-services.md; [SHS](https://www.schoolhomesupport.org.uk/our-services/practitioner-service)) — at our roll (~1,520, ~45% FSM6, so ~680 disadvantaged pupils) that is **~2 FTE practitioners plus attendance mentors**, doing home visits, benefits/housing casework and multi-agency coordination. These are trained specialist roles, not repurposed general support staff — the same EEF logic that says structured, trained deployment works where informal deployment fails (see research/05-staffing-workforce.md). SHS reports 58% of supported children improved attendance (avg +26 days; +46 for the severely absent) — charity-reported, not RCT, so promising rather than proven.

The team is **line-managed inside the safeguarding structure under the DSL**, integrating family practitioners, attendance mentors, counselling and — pushing hard for coverage given need-based prioritisation — an NHS Mental Health Support Team (52% pupil coverage spring 2025, rising to 60% by March 2026) (see research/06-extended-services.md). We adopt **relational and trauma-aware principles but do not buy branded trauma-informed packages**: the empirical base is weak and the YEF's UK trial of Trauma Informed Schools UK could reach no recommendation (see research/06-extended-services.md).

## Attendance: relationships plus data, support before enforcement

Attendance is the biggest presenting problem and, per EPI, the **single biggest tractable lever on the gap: if disadvantaged pupils had their peers' absence rates, the age-16 gap would shrink by over 4 months** — against a 19-month gap that is the joint-widest on record (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md; [EPI](https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2025-disadvantage/)). FSM pupils are ~2.5x more likely to be persistently absent (34.8% vs 14.1%); in autumn 2024/25, ~33.3% of EHCP pupils were persistently absent, and severe absence is still rising ([DfE](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/pupil-absence-in-schools-in-england/2024-25-autumn-term)). It is also the weakest evidence base: a 2025 systematic review of interventions for persistent absence found mostly low-quality studies, with relationship-based, family-level approaches the most promising and **fines not working** (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md). Our system:

- **A Senior Attendance Champion at deputy-head level** (statutory requirement under *Working together to improve school attendance*, statutory since August 2024).
- **Same-day first-response calls and home visits**, with the breakfast club (below) as the daily pastoral touchpoint.
- **1:1 attendance mentors for the persistent/severe cohort** on the Barnardo's/DfE model — relational mentoring is the strongest practice-level bet, working on family root causes.
- **Support-first plans** that address SEND and anxiety barriers before any enforcement — non-negotiable given SEND pupils are suspended and absent at 3–4x the rate of peers, and Ofsted's report cards (from November 2025) grade attendance and inclusion as standalone areas with case-sampling of individual vulnerable pupils (see research/03-send-evidence.md, research/04-behaviour-culture.md, research/10-assessment-accountability.md).
- Following Star Academies, we make **attendance a rewarded virtue, not a compliance metric** — wired into an earnable character award (below) — because Star's England-leading attendance sits on exactly that reframing (see research/14-star-academies.md).

We will be honest internally that the causal evidence is thin, so **every intervention is instrumented and evaluated** against a lost-learning dashboard.

## Food: breakfast as a club, and holiday provision on site

**Universal free breakfast for all phases, run as a club (07:45–08:20, inside the 07:45 site opening — Section 0 canon), not a canteen.** Magic Breakfast found ~+2 months at KS1 (0.14–0.16 SD), with the mechanism being the calm, supervised, social start — not the food alone (see research/06-extended-services.md; [EEF/IFS](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/magic-breakfast)). We draw the national grant for primary-aged pupils (£25/day fixed + £1/pupil/day; a duty for schools with primary pupils under the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act, Royal Assent 29 April 2026) and self-fund the ~£1/pupil/day marginal secondary cost. Design it with reading and games, staffed by pastoral staff, doubling as the daily attendance touchpoint (the National School Breakfast Programme reported 28% fewer late marks).

**We become the HAF delivery site for the catchment**, bidding into the LA's ~£200m/year Holiday Activities and Food programme (secured to 2028–29) so food + activities run 4 days/week across major holidays on our site with our pastoral staff (see research/06-extended-services.md) — keeping eyes on high-risk children through the summer dip.

## Safeguarding across two employers and one site

This design creates unusual safeguarding surface — staff conducting home visits at scale, HAF holiday provision, a 2-to-18 age span on one site, adult education co-located with children, and a legally separate Foundation whose staff work with the same children under a different employer. That surface is designed for, not discovered later:

- **A joint trust–Foundation safeguarding agreement**: shared KCSIE-compliant policies, safer-recruitment standards and DBS/vetting parity across both employers, a **single central record covering every adult who works with children on site regardless of employer**, and a formal information-sharing agreement so family-practitioner casework, hub data and school safeguarding records operate as one picture with lawful bases documented.
- **Named DSL cover for every hub and HAF session**, including holidays and twilight hours — Foundation activity never runs without a trained designated lead on call and a live handover route into the school's safeguarding record.
- **Home-visit and lone-working protocols** for family practitioners and attendance mentors: paired visits for first contacts and any flagged household, a check-in/check-out system with a named duty contact, dynamic risk assessment, and recording on the safeguarding system same-day.
- **Site segregation for adult services**: adult education and community provision use a separate entrance and defined zones/hours; adults attending Foundation services are never unsupervised in pupil areas.
- **DSL capacity that is not swallowed by the Inclusion Directorate.** The DSL role sits with a dedicated senior leader with protected time, supported by **trained deputy DSLs per phase (EYFS/primary, secondary, post-16) plus a Foundation-employed deputy for hub/HAF activity** — so the leader who also oversees behaviour, SEND, attendance and family support is never the single point of safeguarding failure. Deputies get timetabled release, not goodwill time (the known failure mode of under-resourced statutory roles — see research/05-staffing-workforce.md).

## Community partnerships and enrichment guarantee

We treat the school as neighbourhood infrastructure (Reach, Oasis Community Hubs). The community layer runs through the Foundation and the Best Start hub: adult education, community organising, and a co-located multidisciplinary team (speech therapist, EP, counsellor, mentors) eliminating waiting-time cliff-edges (see research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md).

Curriculum-side enrichment detail sits in the extended-day design — the **15:30–16:30 directed-but-tiered block**, where enrichment is a guaranteed rotating entitlement for pupils not in directed targeted support (Section 0 canon). The **family-facing commitment is an enrichment guarantee tracked by pupil-level participation, prioritising FSM/SEND uptake** — which now anticipates national policy twice over: Ofsted's report-card framework (from November 2025) inspects personal development and inclusion as standalone graded areas, and the Curriculum & Assessment Review's accepted recommendations include a **core enrichment entitlement for all pupils** (see research/02-curriculum-design.md, research/04-behaviour-culture.md, research/06-extended-services.md). We fold this into a **codified, earnable character award on the Star "ABC" model** — Bronze→Platinum for Attendance, Behaviour and Community service (see research/14-star-academies.md) — which broadens success criteria beyond GCSE grades, disproportionately valuable for SEND pupils, and positions pupils as contributors (Star pupils raised £350,000 for charity in 2024–25).

## Post-16: our own sixth form, designed for our own leavers

Destinations are the outcome measure least distorted by intake and most meaningful for a deprived catchment: disadvantaged pupils' sustained KS4 destination rate is 83.2% vs 94.6% (an 11.4pp gap) (see research/10-assessment-accountability.md; [DfE](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/key-stage-4-destination-measures/2023-24)). We make **closing that 11.4pp gap a headline KPI**, with named tracking of every Y11/Y13 leaver for 12+ months.

The post-16 phase is settled as a commitment — and costed honestly: **the school runs its own 150-place sixth form** (Section 0 canon), funded on the 16–19 formula at ~£4,000–4,800/student plus disadvantage/retention uplifts (~£0.66m in the Section 6 budget). We do not pretend that income sustains a comprehensive prospectus: ~75 students per year is below the ~200-student scale at which DfE and EPI analyses consistently find sixth forms break even, and ~£0.66m buys roughly 8–10 teaching FTE. So two consequences are designed in upfront rather than discovered in year one: **a capped pre-16 cross-subsidy, carried as an explicit named line in the Section 6 budget with an annual ceiling set by governors** — never a silent raid — and **a deliberately narrow published offer** whose breadth comes through partnership. Its design answers the intake we actually serve:

- **Entry criteria published in advance that do not exclude our own SEND and low-attainment leavers** (Section 0 canon) — the sixth form exists for our Year 11s first, not as a selective bolt-on.
- **A defined A-level core, not a full prospectus**: a published list of ~8–10 subjects chosen for our leavers' actual destinations and sized to run viable classes — any subject that cannot recruit a teachable group is delivered through the partnership below, not run at a class of three.
- **A Level 2 / resit route** (GCSE English and maths resits with the same tutoring architecture as KS4) and **one coherent vocational suite** — a single sector-aligned offer, aligned to the CAR's stepping-stone Level 1/2 qualifications and the incoming "V-level" third 16–19 route (see research/02-curriculum-design.md) — so a pupil leaving Y11 without grade 4 passes has a designed destination in-house, without pretending to staff every pathway at this scale.
- **Preparation-for-adulthood built in for EHCP students**: PfA outcomes from the statutory Year 9 annual review onward, and a **supported-internship route** with local employers for those for whom it is the right post-16 offer.
- **Partnership as the breadth mechanism**: the Feltham College-style collaboration with a strong local independent or selective institution (Hampton/LEH model) is load-bearing at this scale, not decorative — it delivers the minority A-level subjects, super-curricular teaching and Oxbridge/Russell Group preparation a 150-place sixth form cannot viably staff alone.

We **engineer destination identity from Reception** (Ark KSA's university-named cohorts; see research/15-ark-ksa.md), run a careers curriculum against all eight Gatsby benchmarks from EYFS to 18, and — critically for the KIPP finding that gains fade without continuity (see research/17-us-charters.md) — **extend support past 18** via a Feltham Futures-equivalent alumni/careers service.

## The three biggest risks

1. **The wrap-around layer raids the core budget or fails to prove its worth.** This is the central risk: the causal evidence for cradle-to-career *beyond a good school* is weak, and Reach's own P8 swung from +1.11 to +0.50 (see research/12-reach-feltham.md). *Mitigation:* a legally separate Foundation with ring-fenced income; a Best Start Family Hub bid to state-fund the spine; decade-length commitments with year-one leading indicators (the London Challenge lesson — see research/01-closing-the-gap.md); and measurement built in from day one — if the hub layer cannot evidence impact, it is scaled back, never the teaching.

2. **The magnet effect overwhelms the family/SEND team.** Genuinely inclusive schools in deprived areas attract up to 6x neighbours' EHCP load, and generous family support draws high-need families faster than funding follows (see research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md, research/03-send-evidence.md). *Mitigation:* staff family practitioners to a fixed ratio (1:300–400 disadvantaged) that scales with roll; negotiate £10k-per-place resourced-provision funding and IMF allocations with the LA early; hold the **contractual cap on EHCP consultations naming the school, tied to the ~24–36 funded base places** (Section 0 canon), with above-cap admissions negotiated with the LA as place-planning partner; and hold caseload caps rather than let quality degrade silently.

3. **Attendance and family spend chase an unproven evidence base.** We are committing real money (mentors, practitioners) to the weakest-evidenced area in education — the 2025 systematic review found mostly low-quality studies (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md, research/06-extended-services.md). *Mitigation:* run a weekly lost-learning dashboard spanning attendance, lesson removals, suspensions and SEND cohorts (Ted Wragg Trust model; see research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md), pre/post-test every intervention with named pupils and dosage tracking (the anti-NTP discipline), and treat any programme that cannot show movement within a year as a candidate for redesign — not an article of faith.
