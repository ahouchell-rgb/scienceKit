# Section 4 — SEND and Inclusion Model

## The design premise: plan for one-in-three, not one-in-five

We are building for a catchment where 25–40% of pupils will have identified SEND — on our canonical ~1,570 roll (2–18 all-through: ~50 nursery, 420 primary, 900 secondary, 150 sixth form; Section 0), that is 400–600 children. This is not pessimism. Inclusive schools in deprived areas act as magnets: NFER shows the highest-EHCP schools carry **up to six times** the EHCP load of their neighbours, and high-SEND schools disproportionately serve poor communities (see research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md; https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/high-send-schools-understanding-the-uneven-distribution-of-pupils-with-send-across-england-s-mainstream-schools/). Our two dominant needs will mirror the national EHCP profile — autism (33.5%) and speech, language and communication (22.1%) — the latter being the deprivation-facing need, with roughly one in five UK children behind on language, concentrated in poverty (see research/03-send-evidence.md). The SEND strategy and the disadvantage strategy are, by design, one strategy: 44% of the GCSE disadvantage gap is locked in by age 11, which is exactly why the 2-year-old entry point and early language work below carry so much weight (research/01-closing-the-gap.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/disadvantage-gap-locked-in-new-research-shows). We therefore design SEND provision as core infrastructure for a third of the school, not a bolt-on for a minority.

One structural fact shapes everything here: **two-thirds of each Year 7 (120 of 180) arrive from external primaries** (Section 0). The all-through advantage — a decade of SEND knowledge travelling with the child — applies in full only to the internal third; for the external majority, the canonical transition programme (structured SEND data handover with feeders, spring-term visits, a named key worker allocated *before* entry, staged induction for EHCP/vulnerable joiners) is the inclusion mechanism, and this directorate owns its SEND strand.

Critically, we build for the system arriving in **2029**, not the 2014 one. The February 2026 Schools White Paper replaces the EHCP-centric model with four tiers — universal, targeted, targeted-plus, specialist — with digital Individual Support Plans (ISPs) for most pupils and EHCPs reserved for the specialist tier, backed by £1.6bn of mainstream-inclusion funding and an expectation that every secondary hosts an "inclusion base" (https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10550/). Every structure below is designed to map onto ISPs and inclusion bases from day one.

## Tier 1 is the SEND strategy: adaptive teaching for all

The single highest-leverage decision is to make inclusion a whole-school *teaching* issue, owned by every classroom teacher, not a SEND department's problem. The EEF's anchor finding is blunt: "good teaching for pupils with SEND is good teaching for all" (see research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/send). We embed the EEF "Five-a-day" repertoire — explicit instruction, cognitive and metacognitive strategies, scaffolding, flexible grouping, and technology — into every teacher's instructional-coaching entitlement (the coaching engine itself is specified in the workforce section). The Five-a-day ingredients are also the toolkit's best whole-school bets for disadvantage — metacognition +7–8 months, with *larger* effects in studies with more disadvantaged pupils; oral language ~+6 — so Tier 1 serves both cohorts with one repertoire (research/01-closing-the-gap.md; research/02-curriculum-design.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit). We **ban differentiation-by-task and "must/should/could" capped objectives for the SEN-support majority**: the same ambitious curriculum, with support varied, not content lowered. (The narrow, criteria-gated exception for specialist-tier pupils is defined below — the ban must never become a barrier to lawful individualised provision.) Every teacher is trained across the Autism Education Trust tiers — including office, catering and transport staff, since 60% of teachers report being untrained for autistic pupils (research/03-send-evidence.md) — and to a communication-friendly classroom standard delivered by our in-house speech and language team. This entitlement is non-negotiable because we will run young: in the most disadvantaged schools 34% of teachers have under five years' experience (vs 20% in the least), so we build on the SEND-strengthened ITTECF/ECTE early-career framework rather than duplicate it (research/05-staffing-workforce.md).

## The inclusion directorate: one multidisciplinary team, no SEND silo

We replace the conventional SEND department with a single multidisciplinary **Inclusion Directorate** merging SEND, safeguarding, behaviour, attendance and family support, on the Dixons "Mountain Rescue" pattern (see research/13-dixons.md; https://www.ambition.org.uk/blog/a-mountain-rescue-approach-to-send-inclusion/). Attendance sits inside the directorate deliberately: it is the single biggest tractable lever on the gap — equalising disadvantaged pupils' absence would cut over four months from the age-16 gap, and FSM pupils are 2.5× more likely to be persistently absent (34.8% vs 14.1%) — and the promising interventions are relational and family-level, not compliance-and-fines, which is precisely this team's toolkit (research/01-closing-the-gap.md; https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2025-disadvantage/). It is led by an **NPQ-qualified SENCO/INCo who sits on SLT** (mandatory NPQ within three years of appointment). Specialist roles — SaLT, counsellor, key worker, mentor — do only what they are trained for and are strictly separated from teaching. We co-locate the specialists physically: an in-house speech and language therapist, brokered educational-psychology time, and counselling, to eliminate the NHS/LA waiting-list cliff edges that a deprived catchment cannot absorb (Haringey Learning Partnership model, research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md). Assess–Plan–Do–Review cycles are hard-wired into the whole-school calendar with obligatory review meetings, owned by classroom teachers, and — following Dixons' "game-changing" rule — we never train the SENCO without the headteacher alongside.

## Rebuild the TA workforce; buy teachers instead

The evidence here is unusually clear and financially decisive, and the EEF's updated *Deploying Teaching Assistants* guidance (March 2025, 52+ studies) has hardened it: TAs must **supplement, never replace, the teacher**; the lowest attainers must get *at least as much* teacher time as everyone else; TAs scaffold towards independence, not velcro to individuals. Unstructured 1:1 "velcro" support produces roughly **zero or negative** progress; TA-delivered *structured, time-limited* interventions add **+3–4 months (up to +6 for 1:1)** — all seven EEF trials positive (research/03-send-evidence.md; research/05-staffing-workforce.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/teaching-assistants). We therefore make two moves. First, we **abolish default 1:1 velcro support** and timetable the lowest attainers *more* teacher time, not less. Second, following Dixons and the Academy at Shotton Hall (P8 +0.58, disadvantaged pupils outpacing national peers), we **convert most of the TA salary budget into additional qualified subject teachers** so that intervention in English and maths is teacher-led and we can run flexible grouping — e.g. across a 60-pupil band, the strongest teacher takes 49 while a second runs a bespoke group of 11 (research/13-dixons.md; research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md). The TAs we retain are fewer, better-paid and better-trained — intervention specialists, not classroom cover — deployed only on structured programmes (~30 minutes, 3–5×/week, with training, scripts and entry/exit data), with joint teacher–TA preparation time timetabled and TAs inside the coaching entitlement (research/05-staffing-workforce.md). Every intervention keeps the anti-NTP discipline — named pupils, tracked dosage, protected ratios, a kill criterion — because the NTP's collapse to ~+1 month at scale, with only 35–47% of places reaching disadvantaged pupils, is what loose targeting costs (research/01-closing-the-gap.md). This is a cost-neutral reallocation in total payroll: teacher headcount rises, net spend does not.

## Two inclusion bases from day one — as services, not destinations

We open **two on-site inclusion bases** immediately, funded at the £10,000-per-place high-needs rate plus top-ups: an **autism/regulation base (12–18 places)** and an **SLCN/communication base (12–18 places)** — the canonical **~24–36 funded base places** in total (Section 0), which is also the contractual cap on EHCP consultations naming the school. Both are run as "inclusive services", not segregated safe spaces — the design test from Strogilos et al. (research/03-send-evidence.md; https://nasenjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1471-3802.12622). Three non-negotiable design rules, drawn from the strongest exemplars:

1. **Guaranteed ≥50% mainstream lesson time** on personalised timetables (Carr Manor's 12-place Mosaic provision; NAS Cullum Centres).
2. **Base specialists coach the whole school** — the base is a hub of expertise flowing outward into every classroom, not a room children disappear into (Cullum pattern).
3. **Written entry and exit criteria** and the explicit principle that a base is "a stepping stone, not a final destination", with mainstream classes restructured to receive pupils back (Ormiston's Specialist Internal Provisions; research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-mainstream-fund-2026-to-2027/inclusive-mainstream-fund-best-practice-for-schools--2).

The financial logic is overwhelming: an on-site base place costs ~£10k plus top-up against **~£61,500** for an independent special placement and ~£23,900 for a state special place (research/03-send-evidence.md). Every child kept in a well-run base funds substantial in-house capacity. We pursue NAS Autism Accreditation and the Inclusion Quality Mark as ongoing external QA.

## What is taught in base time — and the honest carve-out

"≥50% mainstream" only means something if the other up-to-50% is specified. Base time, Support Centre time and internal-AP time all run on **the same knowledge-rich curriculum spine as the mainstream classroom** — never a parallel, lower one — delivered as: **pre-teaching and re-teaching** of the current mainstream unit (vocabulary, worked examples, retrieval of prior content, so the child re-enters the lesson ahead, not behind); **SaLT-integrated communication work** planned jointly by the base teacher and our in-house therapist; and an explicit, taught **regulation curriculum** (not free time in a calm room). This is also the pragmatic reading of the policy clock: as an academy we will be statutorily bound to the new national curriculum from 2028 under the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, so there is no legal or educational room for base provision that drifts off-spine (research/02-curriculum-design.md; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/contents).

The carve-out, stated plainly: the differentiation-by-task ban and the no-withdrawal default apply to the **SEN-support majority**, for whom lowered content is the harm. For **specialist-tier pupils** — including children joining us from specialist provision who are working substantially below age-related expectations — we run a defined, criteria-gated **adapted pathway**: a semi-formal or heavily adapted offer where the EHCP/ISP requires it, with accreditation routes that lead somewhere (Entry Level, functional skills, and the stepping-stone Level 1 English/maths qualifications the Curriculum and Assessment Review has introduced precisely for this cohort — research/02-curriculum-design.md; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report). Entry to the pathway requires documented criteria, SENCO-plus-headteacher sign-off and termly review against exit criteria — so inclusion rhetoric never becomes a barrier to lawful individualised provision, and the pathway never becomes an unaccountable sink for pupils who are merely behind.

## Speech, language and literacy therapy in-house

SLCN is our universal deprivation-facing priority. We **employ a speech and language therapist directly** rather than waiting on NHS lists, run **universal language screening at every entry point (nursery, Reception and Year 7 — critically including the 120 external Year 7 joiners, whose language data does not arrive with them)**, and deliver a whole-school oracy and vocabulary curriculum as the Tier-1 offer for all — oral-language interventions carry ~+6 months in the toolkit, and a national oracy framework is coming out of the Curriculum and Assessment Review, so we build ahead of it (research/03-send-evidence.md; research/01-closing-the-gap.md; research/02-curriculum-design.md; https://speechandlanguage.org.uk/the-issue/). The literacy and numeracy catch-up architecture — universal NGRT screening, the three-tier decoding/fluency/comprehension pathway, and timetabled small-group tutoring — is specified in full in the literacy section (research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md); here we make one structural decision: the reading and numeracy intervention team **sits inside the Inclusion Directorate**, so its diagnostic data (GPC gaps, words-per-minute, comprehension) doubles as the evidence trail for ISP/EHC needs assessment.

## The inclusion unit, not the exclusion route

The 2024/25 national data (released July 2026) restates the problem starkly: SEN-support pupils are suspended at **over 4× the no-SEN rate (28.86 vs 6.78 per 100 pupils; EHCP 26.45)**, FSM pupils at ~5× (26.66 vs 5.41), and Timpson found 78% of permanent exclusions go to pupils with SEN, in-need status or FSM (research/04-behaviour-culture.md; https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions-in-england/2024-25). Exclusion is the main pipeline out of mainstream, and a high-expectations culture in a deprived catchment drifts toward repeat suspensions unless we engineer counter-pressure (the Dixons Allerton warning). The internal route has its own hidden risk: Thornton (BERJ 2026; 34,000+ pupils) found SEND and FSM pupils are roughly **twice as likely to be internally isolated even after controlling for behaviour** — so an unmonitored "internal" alternative simply relocates the disparity indoors (research/04-behaviour-culture.md; https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/berj.70049). We therefore build the internal alternative to exclusion into the model, instrumented from day one:

- A **time-limited (~6-week) internal Support Centre**, triggered by live data (behaviour points, internal removals, attendance), staffed by qualified teachers plus SEMH support and **curriculum-continuous on the mainstream spine** (see the base-curriculum rules above), with reintegration hinging on a formal loop that feeds what staff learn about each child back to *all* their teachers (Lister Community School model, research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md). A second referral triggers **automatic SEND/communication screening** — identification precedes sanction escalation, never the reverse (research/04-behaviour-culture.md).
- A small **on-site internal alternative provision** as the step above, so no child is off-rolled to external AP by default — a design the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 now effectively mandates, with revised statutory suspension/exclusion guidance from 26 July 2026 and governing-board review duties for off-site direction from 1 August 2026 (research/04-behaviour-culture.md; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/contents/enacted).
- **Regulation spaces** attached to the autism base and a **relational behaviour policy** with graduated, SEND-plan-documented enforcement — one public standard, but never an undifferentiated sanction ladder that punishes disability-related behaviours and breaches Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duties (see research/11-michaela.md). The RCT evidence disciplines the design: restorative practice works as a **language and repair layer on top of** a clear, consistently enforced system — as a *replacement* for consequences the London LEIP trial found null-to-negative effects — and the DfE Behaviour Hubs evaluation found the most-improved schools increased their focus on *rewarding* positive behaviour, not on harsher sanctions (research/04-behaviour-culture.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/behaviour). SLT-wide inclusion training (The Difference model) is the intervention that halved suspensions at Heritage High within a year (research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md). Suspensions, internal-isolation referrals, reward:sanction ratio and lost-learning are tracked on a **weekly dashboard** as first-class KPIs, disaggregated by SEND, FSM and ethnicity (Ted Wragg Trust model) — Thornton's 2× internal-isolation disparity is the named metric to beat.

## Co-production with families and protected curriculum access

Family engagement is the EEF's strongest lever (+4 to +5 months) but only *intensive, personalised* liaison works for disadvantaged families (research/06-extended-services.md; research/12-reach-feltham.md). Every new SEND family gets a **home visit and one named trusted adult** who persists across phases; support plans are **digital and family-visible**, co-produced in APDR reviews, mapping onto the coming ISPs. The Inclusion Directorate is line-managed alongside the family-support team specified in the extended-services section, so SEND and family casework are one conversation. Curriculum access is protected structurally: a **no-withdrawal-from-lessons default** (withdrawal only to facilitate return), flexible grouping rather than permanent low sets, and a standing governor-level guardrail against any practice that narrows the taught curriculum for lower attainers.

## The three biggest risks

1. **Magnet effect against a frozen high-needs block.** We will attract far more EHCP pupils than a typical school precisely because we are good, yet the high-needs NFF is frozen for 2026–27 (research/07-governance-structures.md). *Mitigation:* design provision to be viable largely within the notional SEN budget and the first-£6,000 rule; join and help lead the local SEND cluster early to access pooled inclusion funding and the £1.6bn Inclusive Mainstream Fund; negotiate base-place funding and top-ups with the LA before opening; and hold the canonical **contractual cap on EHCP consultations naming the school, tied to the ~24–36 funded base places** (Section 0), with beyond-cap admissions negotiated with the LA as strategic place-planning partner (councils can now direct admissions) — this is how the 6× magnet effect is bounded without off-rolling.

2. **The base becomes a "safe space" silo.** Bases drift toward segregation — over a third of unit schools report weak base–mainstream joint working (research/03-send-evidence.md). *Mitigation:* hard-code the ≥50% mainstream guarantee, written exit criteria, and the requirement that base specialists spend timetabled hours coaching mainstream colleagues; audit mainstream-time percentages termly and treat a falling figure as a red flag.

3. **Strict culture reproduces the SEND suspension gap.** Importing routines and high expectations (which benefit SEND pupils most) alongside a rigid sanction system would systematically punish disability — and fail the standalone graded **inclusion** area of Ofsted's report-card framework (from November 2025), which case-samples individual vulnerable pupils rather than taking policy on trust (research/04-behaviour-culture.md). *Mitigation:* SLT-wide inclusion training, relational repair and regulation spaces built in from day one, restorative conversations as universal language with the consequence system intact (the LEIP lesson), the internal Support Centre as the alternative to suspension, and weekly disaggregated suspension and internal-isolation monitoring with governor oversight — expecting no self-selection dividend, unlike Michaela.

## Honesty on the evidence

The causally robust numbers here are the EEF effect sizes for structured TA interventions (all seven trials positive), small-group tutoring, adaptive teaching ingredients and parental engagement. The behaviour-side evidence is more mixed than the confident tone of most inclusion writing admits: restorative practice has genuinely conflicting RCT results (positive-to-null in INCLUSIVE and Pittsburgh, null-to-negative in LEIP), relational whole-school policies rest largely on correlational and perception data, and the internal-unit and resourced-provision outcome claims are promising but largely self-reported case-study evidence, not trials (research/03-send-evidence.md; research/04-behaviour-culture.md; research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md). The NTP is the standing warning that even strong effect sizes die under loose implementation (research/01-closing-the-gap.md). We therefore build on the trialled ingredients, measure every intervention with named pupils, standardised pre/post data and exit criteria, and hold the placement-model and relational-policy claims as design hypotheses to be tested — not settled facts.
