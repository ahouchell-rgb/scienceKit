# Section 2 — Curriculum, Pedagogy and Assessment

## Design principle: an ambitious common curriculum, taught explicitly, adapted for all

Our intake will arrive well below the national picture — where ~25% of Year 7s nationally miss the expected standard in reading and maths, in a high-deprivation catchment it will be closer to 40–50% (see research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md). The temptation is to lower the academic line. We reject that. The evidence from every top-decile operator in a deprived catchment — Michaela (+2.55 P8), King Solomon Academy (+1.06–1.18), Dixons Trinity (+0.76), Star's Eden academies (+0.89 disadvantaged P8) — points the same way: a knowledge-rich, cognitively-demanding common curriculum, taught explicitly, with catch-up bolted *underneath* it rather than a diluted curriculum substituted for it (see research/11-michaela.md, research/15-ark-ksa.md, research/13-dixons.md, research/14-star-academies.md). The differentiation is in the *scaffolding and dosage of support*, never in the ambition of the content — EEF's "Five-a-day" adaptive-teaching repertoire (explicit instruction, scaffolding, cognitive/metacognitive strategies, flexible grouping, technology), not differentiation-by-task (see research/03-send-evidence.md).

We are equally honest about what curriculum alone can do. Schools explain roughly 14% of attainment variance, and **44% of the GCSE disadvantage gap is already locked in by age 11** (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md). Two consequences run through this section: the EYFS and primary phase are designed with the same seriousness as the secondary (point 1 below), and our theory of change targets moving the in-school gap by ~4–8 months — best-bet teaching, tutoring and attendance combined — with the larger remainder addressed through the family-hub and extended-services sections, not through rhetorical claims that pedagogy will erase a 19-month gap on its own.

## 1. EYFS and primary (Reception–Year 6): win the gap where it opens

The all-through case (Sections 1, 3, 5) rests on attacking language and reading deficits early, so the 2FE primary (60/year, 420 pupils — Section 0) is designed, not assumed:

- **EYFS: communication and language as the organising priority.** Universal language screening in Reception (WellComm-type) feeding the in-house speech-and-language therapy detailed in the SEND section, plus vocabulary-rich, story-rich provision — oral-language interventions run at ~+6 months and the gap is already 4.7 months at age 5 (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md).
- **A validated systematic-synthetic-phonics programme from the first week of Reception** (Little Wandle/Read Write Inc-type): daily phonics, matched decodable readers, and same-day "keep-up" support so catch-up never accumulates — phonics is +5 months with very secure evidence (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md). The Year 1 phonics screening check is treated as a floor, not a target.
- **KS1/KS2 curriculum spine from the same mastery providers as the secondary** (Ark Curriculum Plus / United primary programmes), giving a genuinely cumulative, knowledge-rich 2–16 curriculum specified down to core knowledge and vocabulary per unit. The best causal evidence for this bet is Grissmer et al.'s Core Knowledge lottery study: +0.24 SD in reading across grades 3–6, with a much larger effect in the single low-income school — direction solid, magnitude uncertain (see research/02-curriculum-design.md).
- **One pedagogy and one coaching model, 2–18.** Primary staff are trained on the same instructional playbook and daily review architecture (retrieval starters, whole-class reading, whole-class feedback) as the secondary, so the internal third of each Year 7 experiences real continuity — while the two-thirds external majority (Section 0) is re-baselined on entry (points 4–5 below).

## 2. Curriculum design: buy the spine, spend the saved hours on adaptation

We will **not write our own curriculum**. In a school where teacher time is the binding constraint and the SEND profile demands relentless adaptation, authoring from scratch is the wrong place to spend it. We adopt a bought-in, mastery-sequenced curriculum spine from day one — the highest-confidence workload win available (~3–4 hrs/teacher/week reclaimed), and since 84% of leavers cite workload, this is retention policy as much as curriculum policy (see research/09-edtech-ai.md, research/15-ark-ksa.md, research/05-staffing-workforce.md):

- **English and maths:** Ark Curriculum Plus (English Mastery, Mathematics Mastery) or United Curriculum, both nationally benchmarked and resourced. Honest expectation on effect size: the Maths Mastery EEF RCTs found only **+1 month** at secondary (n=7,712); English Mastery's second trial found **~+4 months** in reading over three years (https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/mathematics-mastery). We adopt these for *consistency, sequencing and workload*, expecting the compounding effect over a key stage, not a silver bullet.
- **Foundation subjects and a free base layer:** Oak National Academy as the adaptable, free curriculum backbone, edited by departments for our cohort.
- **Enrichment as entitlement, not decoration:** a guaranteed pledge of trips, instruments, performance, debate and clubs — pre-empting the Curriculum & Assessment Review's core enrichment entitlement, and answering the cultural-capital critique by curating powerful knowledge to include the community's own culture (see research/02-curriculum-design.md).

**Depth before breadth at KS3.** Following the Ark/KSA model, we protect **5–6 hours per week each of English and maths** in Years 7–8 — the single most protective structural decision for pupils arriving below age-related expectations (see research/15-ark-ksa.md). Foundation subjects follow a genuinely knowledge-rich, ambitious interpretation of the national curriculum — academies lose national-curriculum freedom under s.54 of the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, with the reformed curriculum published spring 2027 for first teaching September 2028, so the spine is audited against it from day one (see research/02-curriculum-design.md) — delivered through subject **booklets** rather than worksheets (the Michaela/Dixons model), which reduce photocopying, guarantee a shared knowledge base, and support absent or catch-up pupils.

## 3. Teaching model: cognitive-science-informed, with named routines

Every teacher is trained in and coached on one instructional model, grounded in Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction and cognitive load theory. The load-bearing, controversy-free instructional core we import wholesale from Michaela and Dixons (see research/11-michaela.md, research/13-dixons.md):

- **Explicit, teacher-led instruction from the front** — I-do / we-do / you-do, with heavy modelling and **worked-example fading** for novices.
- **Daily retrieval practice** — low-stakes recall ("brain dumps", quizzing) at the start of lessons, exploiting the testing effect.
- **Explicit metacognitive teaching** — planning, monitoring and evaluating modelled inside subject teaching; at +7–8 months (2025 Toolkit update) it is the highest-impact, lowest-cost strand, with *larger* effects in studies with more disadvantaged pupils (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md, research/02-curriculum-design.md).
- **Whole-class feedback, not written marking** — reclaims workload with no loss of impact; EEF rates feedback ~+6 months at very low cost (https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/feedback).
- **Named classroom routines** taught explicitly in a **Year 7 "bootcamp"** in week one (a taught behaviour-and-learning curriculum, re-rehearsed each term): a *Do Now* retrieval starter, *cold call* and *Show Call*, choral response, and turn-and-talk. Predictable, taught routines lower cognitive load and anxiety — disproportionately valuable for autistic and ADHD pupils — and consistency of routine is the strongest common thread in the behaviour evidence (see research/03-send-evidence.md, research/11-michaela.md, research/04-behaviour-culture.md).

One honest corrective: the classroom evidence for retrieval and spacing is far thinner than the laboratory evidence — of 21 retrieval-practice classroom studies in the EEF's cognitive-science review, only one used a regular class teacher. We therefore institutionalise these techniques **through materials, not exhortation**: centrally-written retrieval starters, spaced revisit maps in schemes of work, and worked-example-then-fade task design baked into the bought-in spine (see research/02-curriculum-design.md).

**Daily Morning Meeting (20–30 min, all pupils, Years 7–11; the primary phase runs the same daily-review architecture in class time)** — the Dixons ritual that does four jobs at once: retrieval practice, literacy/numeracy, culture reset, and staff-given **appreciations** pushed to families via app. This replaces woolly form time and is our daily relational touchpoint (see research/13-dixons.md).

**Staff development runs on coaching, not appraisal.** Following Dixons, we replace performance appraisal with universal incremental instructional coaching (Steplab-style, ~1 coach per 6–8 teachers) plus twice-weekly whole-staff deliberate practice. This is the best-evidenced lever we have on teaching quality: Kraft et al.'s meta-analysis finds coaching improves instruction by 0.49 SD and achievement by 0.18 SD (~0.10 SD at scale) (see research/13-dixons.md). We hold the claim with humility: Sims et al.'s 2025 meta-analysis finds the average PD effect in *preregistered* trials indistinguishable from zero, and coaching quality and focus beat dosage — so coach time is protected in middle leaders' directed time (~2–3 periods/week per coach) and the coaching itself is quality-assured, not just scheduled (see research/05-staffing-workforce.md). Fidelity of implementation — not brand choice — is the active ingredient across every intervention in this section.

## 4. Reading and oracy: a whole-school system with a diagnostic engine

Reading is a named senior-leader responsibility. Doing nothing is a decision to fail: fewer than one in five pupils below the KS2 standard reach a grade 4 in GCSE English without intervention (see research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md).

- **Universal screening in the first fortnight of Year 7** — all 180 entrants, essential because two-thirds arrive from external primaries (Section 0) and are re-baselined alongside the feeder-data handover in the transition programme — with a standardised adaptive test (NGRT-type), repeated at least twice yearly, with individual diagnostic follow-up (grapheme-phoneme gaps, words-per-minute, comprehension) for anyone below reading age ~9–10. This also pre-empts the statutory Year 8 reading test announced in the November 2025 Curriculum & Assessment Review response (with schools also expected — non-statutorily — to assess maths and writing in Year 8; expected alongside the 2028 curriculum rollout) (see research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md).
- **A three-tier reading pathway with explicit exit criteria** (https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/literacy-ks3-ks4):
  - **Tier 3** — daily systematic-synthetic-phonics decoding intervention (Fresh Start-type) for non-decoders, delivered by a *small, specialist-trained* team with fidelity monitoring. (Fresh Start went +3 months at efficacy to **−2 months** at scale — an implementation failure, not a programme failure.)
  - **Tier 2** — the HFL 8-week repeated-reading fluency protocol, twice weekly, for decoders below the **~90 wpm** comprehension threshold.
  - **Tier 1** — universal: daily 20-minute tutor-led reading of a challenging text (the Dixons "Robust Reading" model), disciplinary literacy in every subject, and explicit vocabulary teaching.
- **Oracy is a planned curriculum strand, not an accident of talk** — Voice 21-style talk protocols in lessons, ahead of the incoming national oracy framework; oral-language interventions run at ~+6 months, a strand strengthened by 34 new studies in the 2025 Toolkit update (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md, research/02-curriculum-design.md). The daily anchor is a **family lunch** (~£2.50/day, tables of six with an adult, pupil service rota, set discussion topic and public appreciations) — the Michaela mechanism, cheap structured talk and belonging, adaptable for social-communication needs (see research/11-michaela.md). Universal language screening and in-house speech-and-language therapy are detailed in the SEND section.

## 5. Year 7 catch-up: timetabled high-dosage tutoring, not after-school bolt-ons

Tutoring is the best-evidenced catch-up mechanism in education (+5 months 1:1, +4 months small-group; 96-RCT meta-analysis 0.29–0.37 SD), and in-school beats after-school (see research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md, research/17-us-charters.md). The National Tutoring Programme is the cautionary tale: diluted to ~+1 month at national scale, with only 35–47% of tutored pupils disadvantaged because schools spent the subsidy on whoever was convenient (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md). The NTP subsidy ended in August 2024, so this is now a **core budget line**, funded from the targeted-cohort Pupil Premium (Section 0):

- **Groups of 1:2–1:3, 3×30 min/week, in 10–12-week blocks, timetabled into the compulsory core day or the directed 15:30–16:30 targeted-support block** — which is compulsory for this cohort (Section 0), never voluntary twilight — staffed by permanent trained HLTAs/tutors (~£15–25k per FTE covering 40–60 places/year; budget ~£1,500–1,700/pupil on the Apollo 20 benchmark). The anti-NTP discipline applies throughout: named pupils, logged dosage, protected ratios, and a kill criterion per block (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md).
- **Staffing follows the EEF's 2025 TA guidance**: trained intervention specialists delivering scripted, structured programmes (+3–4 months; all seven EEF TA-intervention trials positive) — never general TAs velcroed to individual pupils, and the lowest attainers get *at least as much* qualified-teacher time as everyone else (see research/05-staffing-workforce.md).
- **Maths: no branded catch-up scheme.** Catch Up Numeracy left FSM pupils *two months worse off* at scale. Instead: a diagnostic mastery Year 7 curriculum with unit pre-testing, plus small-group teacher/HLTA tutoring for the bottom quintile targeting place value, fractions and multiplicative reasoning (EEF KS2–3 maths guidance).
- **Adaptive software (Lexia-type) only as a supervised supplement** — minimum 3×20 min/week, prioritised for FSM pupils where its effect doubles (+2 months). Never the core offer.
- The reading/numeracy intervention team sits **inside the inclusion directorate**, so diagnostic data doubles as the SEND evidence trail (see research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md).

## 6. Homework and self-study: engineered completion, not set-and-hope

Homework is **self-quizzing on knowledge organisers** plus adaptive online practice — the Michaela/Sparx model. But access without *completion* has zero effect (the Sparx finding), and home access cannot be assumed in this catchment (see research/09-edtech-ai.md). We therefore engineer completion:

- One adaptive practice platform per core subject (e.g. Sparx Maths — ~1 hr/week completed ≈ +20–30% of a GCSE grade), monitored on **minutes-per-week per pupil split by PP/SEND**.
- A **supervised homework club** in the voluntary 16:30–17:00 slot (on top of the directed 15:30–16:30 block — Section 0; see Extended Services section) and school-loan laptops plus data SIMs, so platform homework never depends on home connectivity (equity audit at intake).
- **Parental text-messaging** on tests and homework — a +1 month effect at ≤£6/pupil/year, the cheapest win in the section, deployed before any five-figure platform.

## 7. Internal assessment: formative core, standardised summative, no false flightpaths

Formative assessment is the core system, not the summative regime. EEF's Embedding Formative Assessment RCT delivered **+2 months on Attainment 8 at very low cost, with the biggest gains for the lowest-attaining third** (see research/10-assessment-accountability.md):

- **Daily** low-stakes retrieval quizzing, whole-class feedback, and EFA-style teacher learning communities.
- **Comparative judgement** (No More Marking) for extended writing — 0.96 reliability, standardised data without teaching-to-the-rubric.
- **Summative: 2–3 standardised, nationally benchmarked windows per year** (Smartgrade/United, NFER or GL), giving standardised scores and question-level analysis — *not* six data drops. We report **raw scores plus cohort averages only, and ban teacher-predicted GCSE grades and flightpaths before Year 10**, when full past papers begin (the Dixons model), to avoid spurious flightpaths and teaching-to-the-test.
- **CAT4 baseline on entry** — the leading replacement progress baseline while KS2-based Progress 8 is suspended (P8 suspended for the 2025 and 2026 results, returning for 2027).
- **Destinations as a headline internal KPI** — named tracking of every leaver for 12+ months, targeting closure of the 11.4pp disadvantaged sustained-destination gap; it is the outcome measure least distorted by intake.

## 8. KS4 offer and qualification strategy

We design for the **reformed Progress 8** (EBacc scrapped Nov 2025; consultation response summer 2026 for 2027–28 options): double-weighted English and maths, two science slots, and four "breadth" slots with a maximum of two vocational qualifications (see research/10-assessment-accountability.md).

- **An ambitious academic core for nearly all** — combined or triple science, a humanity, a language for the majority (Star runs 89% EBacc entry as aspiration, though the EBacc constraint is now gone) — with **vocational and arts routes designed as genuine pathways, never lowered expectations** (see research/14-star-academies.md).
- **Stepping-stone Level 1 English and maths qualifications** (a Curriculum & Assessment Review reform) for the small number for whom a GCSE-only diet would mean two years of guaranteed failure — an on-ramp to Level 2 and resits, never a terminal track (see research/02-curriculum-design.md).
- A **broader Computing GCSE including AI**, and strengthened digital literacy at every key stage, per the Curriculum & Assessment Review.
- **Assistive/adaptive tech** (text-to-speech, dictation, AI-adapted resources for SEND/EAL) built into universal Wave 1 provision, not reserved for exam access arrangements.

## Biggest risks and mitigations

1. **Implementation-fidelity fade at scale.** Across seven EEF efficacy-to-effectiveness scale-ups the mean effect collapsed from 0.25 to 0.01; Fresh Start went +3 to −2 months (see research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md). *Mitigation:* a named senior leader owns reading; a small, specialist-trained delivery team rather than everyone doing everything; standardised pre/post testing with prompt exit at threshold; and the universal coaching model as the fidelity engine — itself quality-assured, given the preregistration caveats on PD effects (see research/05-staffing-workforce.md).
2. **A demanding academic line disengaging a below-ARE, high-SEND cohort — driving exclusions.** No-excuses regimes demonstrably raise suspensions, and the 2024/25 national data show SEN-support pupils suspended at ~4× the no-SEN rate (28.86 vs 6.78 per 100) and FSM pupils at ~5× (see research/11-michaela.md, research/17-us-charters.md, research/04-behaviour-culture.md). We import Michaela's *instructional* core but explicitly **reject its undifferentiated sanction ladder** (Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duties; Ofsted's new inclusion grade) — noting honestly that warm-strict itself has no RCT evidence and is adopted on coherence with the routines-and-consistency evidence and operator track records (see research/04-behaviour-culture.md). *Mitigation:* adaptive teaching not lowered content; timetabled tutoring as the pressure valve; genuine vocational pathways; relational behaviour and nurture provision (detailed in the SEND and behaviour sections).
3. **Accountability distortion under a persistent deprivation penalty.** High-FSM schools are ~3× more likely to be graded below "expected standard" for achievement, and P8 is absent until 2027 (see research/10-assessment-accountability.md). *Mitigation:* a no-teaching-to-the-test guardrail (full-breadth entitlement for all sets, no early entry, governor review of any narrowing for lower sets); internal contextual tracking benchmarked against similar high-FSM schools; and destinations as the headline KPI Ofsted's "similar schools" model will reward.
