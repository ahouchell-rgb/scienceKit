# Section 3 — Structure, School Day and Culture

*This section sets the operational skeleton: phases and size, the timetable and extended day, transition, the behaviour and culture system, routines, enrichment, food, staffing, coaching and workload. It assumes the SEND provision model of Section 4 (research/03-send-evidence.md, research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md), the extended/family-support layer of Section 5 (research/06-extended-services.md), the funding envelope of Section 6 (research/07-governance-structures.md) and the assessment system of Section 2 (research/10-assessment-accountability.md), and does not re-argue them. All structural constants quoted here follow Section 0 (canon).*

## Phase and size: all-through 2–18, engineered small

We build an **all-through 2–18 school on one site**: a **~50-place nursery for 2–4-year-olds** (funded early-years places on the Reach model), a **2-form-entry primary (60/year, 420 pupils)** feeding a **6-form-entry secondary (180/year, 900 pupils at Years 7–11)**, plus a **150-place sixth form** — **~1,520 on roll at statutory age, ~1,570 including nursery** (Section 0). The all-through case is relational, not a proven effect size — there is no robust UK effect-size literature for all-through structure (research/12-reach-feltham.md) — but Reach Academy Feltham (60/year, 2–18, Outstanding 2024, P8 ~+0.85–0.97 at 35.6% FSM and 15% EHCP intake) and Ark King Solomon Academy (60/year, P8 +1.06 to +1.18 at 62–67% FSM) show it is compatible with top-decile outcomes for a deprived, high-SEND intake. It is also the design's answer to the hardest fact in the gap literature: **44% of the GCSE disadvantage gap is already locked in by age 11**, and schools explain only ~10–15% of attainment variance — so a school serious about a 19-month gap must start before Reception and act on family-level factors, not just teach Year 7 harder (research/01-closing-the-gap.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/disadvantage-gap-locked-in-new-research-shows). The mechanisms we want are concrete: continuity of relationship and assessment data across a decade, early language screening from Reception, and no transition cliff for pupils who grow up in the school (research/15-ark-ksa.md; https://reachacademyfeltham.com/cradle-to-career/).

**Stated honestly: most of the secondary intake is external.** A 2FE primary sends 60 pupils into a 180-place Year 7, so **only one-third of each Year 7 progresses internally; two-thirds (120 of 180) arrive from external primaries** (Section 0). The "no Year 6→7 cliff" claim therefore applies in full only to the internal third — for the external majority the answer is the transition programme below, not all-through structure. We chose 2FE over 3FE because a bigger primary raises cost without changing this basic arithmetic; the honest fix is a strong transition offer.

We deliberately do **not** copy the 50–60/year size itself: XP Doncaster's 1:10 staffing ratio (vs ~1:20 local) is "the model's hidden cost driver" and financially unreplicable at scale (research/16-xp-doncaster.md). Instead we **engineer smallness inside a viable structure**: crews of ~12 with one consistent adult across five years, grouped into year-halls of ~90, so "knowing every child" is structural without a 60-pupil budget. Six forms of entry is the smallest size that funds a broad KS4 option block and specialist teaching while staying human-scale.

## Transition: designed for the external two-thirds

Year 6→7 transition is a known SEND risk point, and two-thirds of our Year 7 will cross it from outside. So transition is a designed programme, not a July open evening (Section 0):

- **Enhanced transition for every external Year 7 joiner**: multiple summer-term visits, transition passports, structured SEND/attainment data handover with feeder primaries, and a summer induction bootcamp with crew and key-worker allocation *before* entry.
- **Extended programme for SEND/EHCP/vulnerable joiners**: spring-term visits, a named key worker allocated before entry, staggered/staged induction, and pre-transfer EHCP/ISP planning meetings — this is exactly where a high-SEND, high-anxiety intake would otherwise be lost in the first term.
- **Internal Year 6 pupils** carry a guaranteed progression right into Year 7 (counting within the 180 PAN) and a decade of assessment data; for them the cliff genuinely does not exist.

## The day: extended, programmed, honestly counted

Site open **07:45, closed 17:00**; **compulsory core day 08:00–15:30** — gross 7.5 hours × 5 = 37.5, **~33.3 directed hours/week net of a 50-minute daily lunch**, at and slightly above the DfE 32.5-hour expectation (Section 0). We do not claim more: a compulsory 08:00–16:30 day would be a 42.5-hour pupil week — ten hours beyond the expectation, unaffordable on a 2.5% pay uplift and a burnout machine. Extending time is worth +2 months on average and closer to **+3 months for disadvantaged pupils**, but only when the extra time is *programmed*, not padded — "what fills the extra time matters more than the extra time itself" (research/06-extended-services.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/extending-school-time). We pay staff **~2.5% above scale** for the longer core day, as Ark does for its own 08:30–16:30 staff day, **plus a separately paid extended-day allowance for each rota'd 15:30–16:30 session** — roughly 4–5% above scale in total for a typical teacher — rather than relying on goodwill (research/15-ark-ksa.md). And "honestly counted" applies to staff as well as pupils: a classroom teacher's **directed-time budget is published annually** — core attendance 07:50–15:40 across 190 pupil days (net of lunch), five INSET days and a capped meetings/practice/parents'-evening calendar, totalling ~1,400–1,450 hours — openly ~10–15% above the 1,265-hour STPCD benchmark and priced as such, not smuggled past it; the nine-day-fortnight pilot (below) would return most of that excess. The boundaries are hard: teachers are never directed before 07:50 or after 16:30, breakfast club is support-staff-run, the 15:30–16:30 duty is **capped at two sessions/week per teacher** on a published rota, and 16:30–17:00 is voluntary for staff as well as pupils.

The daily shape:
- **07:45–08:20 free breakfast club** (below) — now a statutory duty for primary-aged pupils under the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (research/01-closing-the-gap.md; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/contents).
- **08:00–08:25 Morning Meeting** for every pupil, Years 7–11 — retrieval/"brain dump" quizzing, literacy/numeracy drill, culture reset and staff-given **appreciations pushed to families** by app. This one ritual replaces woolly form time and does four jobs at once (research/13-dixons.md; https://www.dixonsat.com/why/coaching-and-practice).
- **Depth-before-breadth core**: 5–6 hours/week each of English and maths at KS3, the pillar most protective of pupils arriving below age-related expectations (research/15-ark-ksa.md).
- **15:30–16:30 directed-but-tiered block.** For the identified target cohort this is **compulsory, directed high-dosage tutoring and targeted academic support** (their directed week runs to ~38 hours); for everyone else it is a **guaranteed rotating enrichment entitlement**. Either way it is programmed and non-voluntary — and *this directed slot* is where the extended-time +2–3 months sits, because the effect attaches to targeted, programmed time and disadvantaged and SEND pupils are precisely those least likely to stay for optional twilight sessions (Section 0). Who staffs this hour, and what it costs, is specified under *Enrichment and food* below — not left implicit.
- **16:30–17:00: genuinely voluntary** homework club and clubs. We attribute no effect-size claim to this slot.

Tutoring runs with anti-NTP discipline. At national scale the National Tutoring Programme delivered only ~1 month's additional progress, and **only 35–47% of tutored pupils were disadvantaged** — schools spent the subsidy on whoever was convenient (research/01-closing-the-gap.md; https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/national-tutoring-programme-year-3-impact-evaluation/). So: named pupils, groups of ≤3, 3×/week, ~10-week data-targeted blocks, dosage tracked, and a kill criterion per intervention. Apollo 20 scheduled 70 min/day of 1:2 maths tutoring and bought a high-charter-sized maths gain at ~$2,000/pupil (research/17-us-charters.md; https://www.nber.org/papers/w17494). Post-NTP this is a core/pupil-premium budget line, not a subsidy (research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md).

## Culture: warm-strict, relationally grounded — an evidenced position

We take a clear position: **import the instructional and routine core of the "no-excuses" tradition, but reject its undifferentiated sanction ladder.** The evidence forces this split — and the research framing of "warm-strict vs relational" is itself a false binary: the EEF's behaviour evidence finds *both* teacher classroom-management skill *and* pupils' social-emotional skills effective, with **consistency and implementation quality mattering more than the specific policy choice** (behaviour interventions ~+3–4 months) (research/04-behaviour-culture.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/behaviour).

The load-bearing, transferable, controversy-free elements are: **taught routines with a Year 7 induction "bootcamp"** and termly re-induction (predictability lowers cognitive load and anxiety and is *more* valuable for autistic/ADHD pupils, not less); a **week-one Year 7 residential** to front-load belonging (XP's relational-proximity data ran +16 to +25pp above benchmark two weeks in); **crew as the primary pastoral unit**; **centralised behaviour administration** (same-day detentions logged and run by non-teaching staff, protecting teacher workload and guaranteeing certainty over severity — with a mandatory two-minute restorative conversation before release); visible SLT presence at every transition (Bennett's "flood the corridors" leadership finding); and a knowledge-rich curriculum with daily retrieval (research/11-michaela.md; research/16-xp-doncaster.md; research/04-behaviour-culture.md). Honest flag: warm-strict itself, silent corridors and centralised detentions have **no RCT evidence**; we adopt them for coherence with the routines/consistency evidence and operator track records, and commit to measuring suspension-equity gaps, minutes-lost surveys and belonging from day one (research/04-behaviour-culture.md). We also bank the DfE Behaviour Hubs finding that the most-improved schools increased their focus on **rewarding positive behaviour**, not harsher sanctions: reward:sanction ratio is tracked weekly, target ≥4:1 (https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/rewarding-good-behaviour-key-school-success-dfe-hubs-evaluation-finds).

What we will **not** import is a uniform demerit ladder applied without adjustment. Michaela's +2.55 P8 is real but comes from a ~10% SEND register (half the national rate) and a self-selecting intake — it is deprived-intake evidence, not high-SEND evidence (research/11-michaela.md). US no-excuses charters raise achievement (+0.25 SD/yr maths) *and* suspension days (research/17-us-charters.md), Ofsted found high repeat suspensions even at Dixons Allerton (research/13-dixons.md), and the national data show what drift costs: in 2024/25 FSM pupils were suspended at **26.66 per 100 vs 5.41** for non-FSM (~5×) and SEN-support pupils at 28.86, with 78% of permanent exclusions going to pupils with SEN, in-need status or FSM (research/04-behaviour-culture.md; https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions-in-england/2024-25). Demerits for involuntary, disability-related behaviours would breach Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duties and fail Ofsted's standalone **inclusion judgement** (live November 2025, with case-sampling of individual vulnerable pupils). We expect **no self-selection dividend**: our families won't opt into an extreme ethos, so we build the on-ramps Michaela doesn't need — nurture provision, an SEMH pathway and relational repair alongside a single, public standard whose *enforcement* is graduated and documented through SEND plans, with a **graduated-response check (communication/SEMH screening) hard-wired before any second suspension**. Restorative practice is used as universal repair language on top of clear consequences, never as the accountability system itself — the INCLUSIVE trial showed modest real benefits, but the London LEIP RCT of RP-as-replacement was null-to-negative (research/04-behaviour-culture.md; https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(18)31782-3/fulltext). The proof the inclusive version works: Heritage High halved suspensions in a year after whole-SLT inclusion training, and 58% of leaders on The Difference's course report reduced exclusions within a year (research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md; https://the-difference.com/programmes/for-schools/). **Suspensions are a first-class KPI** on a weekly lost-learning dashboard (attendance, lesson removals, internal-exclusion hours, suspensions, split by FSM/SEND), because high-expectations cultures in deprived catchments drift toward repeat suspension without deliberate counter-pressure — and **zero off-rolling by design**: no managed move without a documented best-interests case and destination-school confirmation, governor sign-off on every roll removal in Years 9–11, in line with the revised statutory suspension/exclusion guidance (from 26 July 2026) and the 2026 Act's off-site direction review duties (research/04-behaviour-culture.md).

## Enrichment and food

**Enrichment guarantee**, mapped to the DfE's eight enrichment benchmarks and pre-empting the Curriculum & Assessment Review's core enrichment entitlement (feeding Ofsted's personal-development area from September 2026): every pupil, every year, gets a funded instrument/performance opportunity, a competitive or physical activity, a residential/outdoor experience and a civic project — arts participation is +3 months and physical activity +2 months in the EEF toolkit (research/06-extended-services.md; research/02-curriculum-design.md; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report). Participation is **tracked at pupil level and prioritised for FSM/SEND pupils first, not last** — much of it delivered through the 15:30–16:30 entitlement rotation, so it reaches the pupils who never stay for optional clubs. That hour is staffed and priced, not conjured: ~750–800 non-target secondary pupils at ~1:20 is ~38–40 groups a day, covered by a mix of rota'd teachers (within the two-session weekly cap above, on the paid extended-day allowance), the inclusion/pastoral team, and **bought-in specialist coaches and peripatetic instructors** for the sport, music and outdoor strands — carried as a **named ~£0.15–0.25m/year line in the Section 6 budget** (≈19–20 externally staffed group-hours a day × 190 days at sessional coach rates), alongside the tutoring line.

**Food is pastoral infrastructure, not catering.** A **universal free breakfast club (07:45–08:20)** run as a calm, supervised, games-and-reading club (the mechanism behind the +2-month KS1 effect is the club, not the food): the primary phase draws the national funding that now backs the statutory breakfast duty, and we self-fund the secondary phase at ~£1/pupil/day marginal, using it as a daily attendance touchpoint (research/06-extended-services.md; research/01-closing-the-gap.md). A **daily family lunch** on the Michaela model (~£2.50/day, tables of six with an adult, pupil service rota, set discussion topic, public appreciations) gives cheap structured oracy and belonging — a daily, planned oracy slot ahead of the incoming national oracy framework (oral-language interventions are ~+6 months in the toolkit) — adapted for social-communication needs (research/06-extended-services.md; research/11-michaela.md; research/02-curriculum-design.md).

## Staffing structure and indicative ratios

The defining staffing choice: **convert most of the TA budget into additional qualified subject teachers.** Stated precisely, because it is easy to garble: the conversion is **cost-neutral in total staff spend — fewer TAs, more teachers, so net teacher headcount rises while net payroll does not.** Unstructured 1:1 "velcro" TA support is roughly zero-to-negative in the EEF evidence, whereas extra teacher capacity lets interventions be *teacher-led* — Shotton Hall reaches P8 +0.58 with above-average disadvantage precisely this way, and Dixons' "Mountain Rescue" runs flexible grouping (e.g. the strongest teacher takes 49 while a second runs a bespoke group of 11) within existing staffing spend (research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md; research/13-dixons.md; https://www.ambition.org.uk/blog/a-mountain-rescue-approach-to-send-inclusion/). The retained, smaller TA cadre follows the EEF's updated *Deploying Teaching Assistants* guidance (March 2025) to the letter: TAs supplement, never replace, the teacher; they are **never the default provision for EHCP pupils**; the lowest attainers are timetabled *at least as much* teacher time as everyone else; and TA hours are concentrated in scheduled, scripted, trained structured interventions (+3–4 months, up to +6 for 1:1), with joint teacher–TA preparation time in the timetable (research/05-staffing-workforce.md; https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/teaching-assistants).

Indicative structure for the secondary phase:
- **Pupil:teacher ratio ~1:16** overall (funded by the TA-to-teacher conversion), enabling 49/11-style flexible grouping in English and maths.
- **One multidisciplinary inclusion/pastoral/safeguarding department** ("Mountain Rescue"), combining SEND, behaviour and safeguarding under a DSL, with counsellors, key workers and mentors whose roles are **strictly separated from teaching** — an NPQ-qualified SENCo/INCo sits on SLT (research/13-dixons.md; research/03-send-evidence.md).
- **Crew leaders**: every teacher runs a crew of ~12; ~15 crews per year group.
- **Family-support practitioners** at ~1 FTE per 300–400 disadvantaged pupils (home visits, benefits/housing casework, early help), line-managed inside safeguarding (research/06-extended-services.md).
- A **Senior Attendance Champion at deputy-head level** (statutory requirement) leading forensic, relationship-based attendance work — named staff per family, first-day response, home visits — because attendance is the single biggest tractable lever: equalising disadvantaged pupils' absence would cut **over 4 months** off the age-16 gap, and FSM pupils are 2.5× more likely to be persistently absent (34.8% vs 14.1%); fines are the one approach with no supporting evidence (research/01-closing-the-gap.md; https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2025-disadvantage/). Plus a central, non-teaching behaviour-admin team.
- An **in-house ITT pipeline** via a SCITT/university partner, because the labour market is structurally against us: unfilled vacancies run at ~6 per 1,000 teachers (six times the 2010/11 rate), shortages concentrate in disadvantaged catchments, and the most disadvantaged schools run 34% of teachers with under five years' experience vs 20% elsewhere (research/12-reach-feltham.md; research/14-star-academies.md; research/05-staffing-workforce.md; https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/teacher-labour-market-in-england-annual-report-2025/). We plan to be ECT-heavy and engineer for it: mentors get **timetabled release, not goodwill time** (the ECF's known failure mode), riding the SEND-strengthened Early Career Teacher Entitlement from September 2025.
- **Bank the DfE money, add our own**: the targeted retention incentive pays up to **£6,000/year** to early-career maths/physics/chemistry/computing teachers in the most deprived 30% of schools — our catchment qualifies for the maximum — topped with a school-funded supplement for shortage-subject and SEND-specialist roles, alongside (never instead of) the working-conditions offer below (research/05-staffing-workforce.md; https://www.gov.uk/guidance/targeted-retention-incentive-payments-for-school-teachers).

## Coaching and staff development

We **replace performance appraisal entirely with universal, incremental instructional coaching**, plus **twice-weekly whole-staff deliberate practice** in two fixed afternoon sessions (~15:35–16:20, inside the published directed-time budget, on days when the 15:30–16:30 block runs on its support-staff/external-coach rota) — not in pre-08:00 briefings, which would quietly stretch every teacher's day past what the pay uplift buys. This is the strongest causally-grounded lever available: Kraft et al.'s meta-analysis of 60 causal studies found coaching improves instruction by 0.49 SD and achievement by 0.18 SD, and it is the Dixons engine (research/13-dixons.md; https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/mkraft/files/kraft_blazar_hogan_2018_teacher_coaching.pdf). We resource **~1 trained coach per 6–8 teachers** with coaching load timetabled into middle-leadership roles (~2–3 protected periods/week per coach — protected coach time is the binding constraint), run coaching on a technique-level platform (Steplab), and pair it with the frequent low-stakes observation that distinguishes effective charters (research/05-staffing-workforce.md; research/17-us-charters.md). Honest flags: coaching effects shrink at scale (~0.10 SD on achievement), more hours did not predict bigger effects (quality and focus beat dosage), and Sims et al.'s 2025 meta-analysis found the average PD effect in *preregistered* trials is not significantly different from zero — so we treat coaching as a well-theorised mechanism to implement carefully, hold expectations honest and instrument delivery (research/05-staffing-workforce.md; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543231217480).

## Workload design so teachers stay

Retention is a first-order design constraint — large MATs show 16.9% secondary turnover (research/07-governance-structures.md), and leavers cite **workload (84%), wellbeing (75%) and pupil behaviour (44%) ahead of pay**, with teachers reporting "too much time" on behaviour up from 50% to 59% since 2022 (research/05-staffing-workforce.md; https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/working-lives-of-teachers-and-leaders) — and a ~33-hour directed pupil week keeps it acute. We attack the top three leaving drivers at source, which costs less than salary premiums: **adopt, do not author, a mastery-sequenced curriculum** (Ark Curriculum Plus / Oak), the highest-confidence workload win at ~3–4 hours/teacher/week saved, spending the reclaimed time on SEND adaptation, not resource-making (honest flag: England's mastery-programme trials show modest attainment effects of ~+1 month — we buy the curriculum for workload and coherence, and rest the attainment case on the toolkit's mastery-learning strand, +5 months) (research/09-edtech-ai.md; research/15-ark-ksa.md; research/02-curriculum-design.md); **knowledge-rich booklets with whole-class feedback and no written marking** (research/11-michaela.md); **centralised behaviour admin** so teachers sanction without administering; **summative assessment cut to 2–3 windows/year** with automated analytics and a ban on additional local data drops (research/15-ark-ksa.md); and **AI for staff workload now** (Oak/Aila show ~3–4 hrs/week savings) while keeping pupil-facing AI inside evaluated trials only (research/09-edtech-ai.md). We add deliberate flexibility: PPA-from-home and part-time-friendly timetabling from day one, and a **pilot of the Dixons nine-day fortnight in year two** (anchor day, ECT access to senior staff protected) evaluated against the EEF Teacher Choices trial — promising self-report on turnover and sickness, RCT pending (research/05-staffing-workforce.md; https://www.dixonsat.com/articles/how-were-giving-teachers-a-nine-day-fortnight). The extended-day pay package (2.5% above scale plus paid 15:30–16:30 sessions), the £6k targeted retention incentive and the coaching-not-appraisal culture complete the retention offer — and Ofsted's report-card framework now explicitly inspects staff workload and wellbeing, turning these guardrails into an inspection asset.

## The three biggest risks

1. **Financial fragility of double-staffing and the extended day.** Dixons' pure double-staffing "proved financially unsustainable" and diluted to flexible grouping, and the high-needs NFF is frozen for 2026–27 (research/13-dixons.md; research/07-governance-structures.md). *Mitigation:* hold the TA→teacher conversion cost-neutral in total payroll (fewer TAs, more teachers — flexible grouping, no net growth in staffing spend); fund the pay package, the enrichment-hour staffing line and tutoring from deprivation-weighted NFF income (secondary ~£8,200–8,800/pupil) plus Pupil Premium modelled **on the targeted (≤£7,400/Ever-6) cohort only — ~£0.48m whole-school, not the ~45% FSM6 headline** (Section 0; Section 6); and stress-test provision against a frozen high-needs block.
2. **Warm-strict drift into exclusion and hidden internal exclusion.** In a high-SEND intake with no self-selection dividend, strict culture risks the repeat-suspension pattern Ofsted flagged at Dixons Allerton and the US charter evidence documents — and the newest UK evidence shows the quieter failure mode: SEND and FSM pupils are roughly **twice as likely to be internally isolated even after controlling for behaviour** (research/04-behaviour-culture.md; https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/berj.70049). *Mitigation:* suspensions as a governor-level KPI; the weekly lost-learning dashboard including internal-exclusion hours split by FSM/SEND; reward:sanction ratio tracked at ≥4:1; the graduated-response SEND check before any second suspension; a time-limited (~6-week), staffed, curriculum-continuous internal support centre — an inclusion unit with automatic SEND screening on second referral, not a punitive isolation room — as the alternative to suspension; whole-SLT inclusion training (The Difference); and graduated SEND-plan-documented enforcement of one public standard, all inside the tightened 2026 statutory guidance and Ofsted's inclusion case-sampling.
3. **Staff burnout from the long day.** *Mitigation:* the published directed-time budget with hard boundaries (nothing directed before 07:50 or after 16:30, the two-session weekly cap on extended-day duties, breakfast club support-staff-run), the workload package above (bought curriculum, no written marking, centralised behaviour admin, capped assessment windows, AI tools, flexible working and the nine-day-fortnight pilot), the extended-day pay package plus targeted retention incentives, and a grow-your-own ITT pipeline with timetabled mentor release to reduce dependence on a thin external labour market in which disadvantaged schools are structurally last in the queue (research/05-staffing-workforce.md).
