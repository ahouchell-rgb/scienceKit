# Section 7 — Admissions and Transition

*Admissions is where an "inclusive" school either proves itself or quietly selects — via the front door, or via "we're full" gaming mid-year. All numbers are canonical (Section 0). The legal frame is the School Admissions Code 2021, still the statutory code in force ([DfE](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/school-admissions-code--2)) — but we design for the successor code the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 requires, under which local authorities gain the power to direct academies to admit and the Schools Adjudicator can set an academy's PAN where an objection is upheld ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/contents); [Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/childrens-wellbeing-and-schools-act-the-policies-signed-into-law/); [VWV briefing](https://www.vwv.co.uk/insights/articles/new-childrens-wellbeing-act-what-are-the-implications-for-academies-and-maintained-schools/)). A school built to be inclusive should be indifferent to that shift: nothing below relies on academy admissions freedoms.*

## Published admission numbers

| Phase | PAN | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery (age 2–4) | ~50 EY places | Not a "relevant age group" under the Code; separate EY admissions policy; **no onward right to Reception** |
| Reception | **60** | 2FE primary |
| Year 7 | **180** | 6FE; 60 internal progression + 120 external |
| Year 12 | **~75** | Sixth form is 150 places across Years 12–13 (Section 0); published entry criteria, identical for internal and external applicants |

PANs will not be reduced quietly in lean years: a falling roll is answered by recruitment and quality, not PAN cuts — under the 2026 Act the Adjudicator could in any case restore a PAN over our heads, taking parental preference into account ([Ward Hadaway](https://www.wardhadaway.com/insights/education-law-digest/childrens-wellbeing-and-schools-act-2026-changes-to-academies/)).

## Oversubscription: we band, and here is the honest case

**Position: fair banding at Year 7 for the 120 external places, with straight-line distance as the in-band tiebreak and random allocation as the final tiebreak. Reception does not band** — there is no validated group ability test normed for 3–4-year-olds at the point of application (banding in practice exists only at secondary transfer — [TES](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/school-admissions-how-fair-is-fair-banding)), the statutory Reception Baseline Assessment cannot lawfully be used for admissions, requiring pre-schoolers from the most deprived families to sit a test is exactly the participation barrier we mitigate at Year 7, and a 60-place intake is too small to band meaningfully anyway. Reception oversubscription uses criteria 1–2 below, then straight-line distance with random allocation as the final tiebreak.

The problem banding solves is real. A distance-only rule lets housing wealth buy proximity, and a school that becomes popular drifts affluent: Sutton Trust work shows top-performing comprehensives take far fewer FSM pupils than their catchments contain, and identifies banding and ballots as the two admissions tools that produce measurably more balanced intakes ([Sutton Trust, *School Places: A Fair Choice?*](https://www.suttontrust.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/School-Places.pdf); [Sutton Trust/LSE, *Banding and Ballots*](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED559241)). Under fair banding all applicants sit a standardised, non-selective test (CAT4-type) and places are allocated equally across ability bands, so the intake matches the comprehensive ~45%-FSM6 planning assumption the whole financial and pedagogical model rests on (Sections 0, 1). The Code explicitly permits banding, provided the test is not used to select the highest-ability applicants and the arrangements, including test details, are published (Code 2021, paras 1.25–1.27).

**The honest counter-evidence, and our mitigations.** The Sutton Trust also warns that banding "may harm the poorest" if the test becomes a participation barrier — deprived families are the least likely to get a child to a Saturday test at an unfamiliar school ([Sutton Trust](https://www.suttontrust.com/news-opinion/all-news-opinion/fair-banding-school-places-may-harm-poorest/)). So: (1) we band on the **ability profile of applicants**, not a national profile; (2) tests are sat **in feeder primaries during the school day** wherever the primary consents, with multiple sittings and home-visit follow-up — a child who never sits the test is placed by teacher-assessment banding, never rejected; (3) we lobby the LA for the single coordinated area-wide banding test that Sutton Trust and Comprehensive Future both call for.

**Order of criteria** (after statutory admissions of EHCP pupils naming the school, who are admitted outside these criteria):

1. Looked-after and previously looked-after children, including those adopted from state care abroad (mandatory first priority, Code para 1.7).
2. Siblings of pupils on roll.
3. At Year 7: fair-banding allocation across bands, with straight-line distance as the in-band tiebreak and random allocation as the final tiebreak. At Reception: straight-line distance, then random allocation.

No aptitude places, no interviews, no supplementary forms beyond what the Code allows — every optional selective instrument is a socio-economic filter in practice.

## Internal progression rules

- **Nursery → Reception: no automatic right, no priority.** The Code is unambiguous: nursery attendance neither guarantees nor may confer priority for a Reception place, and parents must apply through the LA's coordinated scheme ([Code 2021](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/60ebfeb08fa8f50c76838685/School_admissions_code_2021.pdf)). Every nursery admission letter says so loudly, because families assuming a place manufactures grievance at exactly the relationship we most need. What the nursery *does* confer is everything else: four years of language screening, SEND identification and family relationship that follow the child wherever they enrol.
- **Year 6 → Year 7: guaranteed internal progression.** Pupils on roll in our Year 6 transfer automatically into Year 7 without application, as pupils moving between phases of the same all-through school; the 60 internal pupils count within the 180 PAN, leaving 120 places — the two-thirds external majority stated honestly in Section 0 — and banding applies only to those external 120.
- **Year 11 → Year 12:** published academic entry criteria, identical for internal and external students (Code para 2.6), deliberately calibrated so our own SEND and low-prior-attainment leavers have a route in via Level 2 as well as Level 3 study programmes. A sixth form that excludes its own inclusion cohort would falsify the whole design.

## In-year admissions and the backfill commitment (Bet 7)

High-expectations schools in deprived areas leak pupils and then quietly decline to replace them; casual vacancies are where inclusion reputations die. Our published commitment: **every casual vacancy in every year group is filled**, processed within the Code's in-year timescales (decision within 10 school days, LA notified within 2), with no waiting-list manipulation and no "no space in the band" excuses — in-year admission is first-come against the vacancy, not re-banded. Mid-year joiners are disproportionately vulnerable, so every in-year joiner gets a compressed version of the transition machinery below: a data-handover call with the previous school inside the first week, language and reading screening inside a fortnight (Section 4), a named key adult and a peer "first-friend" from day one, and a six-week review with family present. An in-year joiner who arrives in November and is invisible by January is our failure, not theirs.

## Fair Access Protocol

Full participation, published in the admissions policy and reported annually to governors with numbers admitted. FAP exists precisely to place the children other schools avoid — the previously excluded, the unplaced, the vulnerable — with allocation decisions due within 20 school days ([DfE FAP guidance, 2021](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6124ab6ae90e0705410757e8/FAP_Guidance.pdf)). A school claiming the inclusion mantle takes its FAP share *first*, above PAN where necessary, and resources each FAP admission through the Inclusion Directorate as a supported induction, not a favour. The 2026 Act's direction power will make this obligatory for academies anyway; we do voluntarily now what the law will shortly require ([VWV](https://www.vwv.co.uk/insights/articles/new-childrens-wellbeing-act-what-are-the-implications-for-academies-and-maintained-schools/)).

## EHCP consultations — the cap, stated legally honestly

The magnet effect is a financial risk, not a hypothetical: inclusive schools in deprived areas carry up to 6× their neighbours' EHCP load ([NFER](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/high-send-schools-understanding-the-uneven-distribution-of-pupils-with-send-across-england-s-mainstream-schools/)). But the law permits no unilateral "cap on consultations": the LA may consult us on any plan, we respond within 15 days, and if the school is named in Section I we **must admit** (Children and Families Act 2014, s.43; [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/6/part/3/enacted)). The cap therefore operates through the only two lawful channels:

1. **Commissioned base places.** The ~24–36 resourced-provision places (Section 0) are commissioned by the LA at £10,000/place plus top-ups under a written agreement fixing place numbers, entry criteria and annual review ([high-needs operational guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/high-needs-funding-arrangements-2025-to-2026/high-needs-funding-2025-to-2026-operational-guide)). The LA cannot name a child into a base place that does not exist.
2. **Evidence-based consultation responses.** Once base and mainstream specialist capacity is genuinely full, we respond citing incompatibility with the efficient education of others or efficient use of resources (CFA 2014 s.39(4)) — substantiated with provision maps and staffing data, because an unevidenced refusal deserves to be overridden.

What we will never do is the third, common channel: discouraging parents pre-consultation. Consultation volume is reported termly to governors and to the LA place-planning partnership — capacity honestly declared in exchange for commissioning honestly funded.

## The enhanced Year 7 transition programme

The Year 7 dip in attainment, engagement and wellbeing is well-documented and falls hardest on disadvantaged and SEND pupils ([EEF, "Defying the dip"](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/eef-blog-defying-the-dip-one-schools-approach-to-supporting-pupils-transition-between-primary-and-secondary-school); [Chartered College summary](https://my.chartered.college/research-hub/the-transition-from-primary-to-secondary-school-recommendations-from-research/)). Because two-thirds of our Year 7 arrive from external primaries, the transition programme *is* our all-through claim for the majority — Section 0 forbids pretending otherwise. Honesty flag: transition programmes rest on promising quasi-experimental and qualitative evidence, not trials; we pre/post measure wellbeing and reading at entry and treat the programme as an evaluated intervention.

**Universal track (all 120 external joiners):**
- **Structured data handover** with every feeder primary in the summer term: standardised attainment, SEND and safeguarding records, attendance history and a teacher-voice pen portrait per child — a named transition lead visits every feeder sending ≥3 pupils.
- **Multiple visits**, not one induction day: two summer-term taster days plus a parents' evening held *at feeder primaries* where feasible (removing the travel barrier for the families least likely to come to us).
- **Summer induction bootcamp** in the final holiday week: routines, the Morning Meeting, reading and maths baselining, crew allocation — so day one of September is rehearsal, not novelty.
- **Crew/key-worker allocation before entry** (Carr Manor coaching-group pattern, research/18): every joiner knows the name and face of their trusted adult before September.

**Extended track (SEND, EHCP, FAP and vulnerable joiners — designed for ~30–40 children/year):**
- **Spring-term start**: visits from the spring term of Year 6, including parent-and-child sessions in the bases.
- **Named key worker allocated before entry**, who attends the child's final Year 6 annual review or ISP meeting in the feeder school.
- **Pre-transfer planning meetings** for every EHCP/ISP joiner: feeder SENCO, our Inclusion Directorate, family and (where involved) SaLT/EP, producing a one-page profile and environment plan before day one.
- **Staged induction**: part-timetable starts, early lunch passes, base-anchored first weeks with the guaranteed ≥50% mainstream trajectory (Section 4) made explicit to families from the outset.

Internal Year 6 pupils skip none of the relational machinery — crews, baselining, key adults — they simply start it years earlier. That, not the absence of an application form, is the real all-through dividend.
