# Section 1 — Vision and Design Principles

## Purpose

This school exists to prove that a child born into poverty and difference in England can still leave, at 18, with the qualifications, character and destination of a child born into advantage — and that this can be done at scale, at public cost, without selecting the intake or exporting the hard cases. It is designed for the community that the current system serves worst: high deprivation and high SEND together. The disadvantage gap at GCSE stood at **19.0 months in 2025 — the joint-widest since 2013** (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md; [EPI Annual Report 2025](https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2025-disadvantage/)). The National Audit Office has judged the SEND system "financially unsustainable" — high-needs spending up 58% in a decade to £10.7bn with *no* improvement in outcomes, EHCP demand up ~140% since 2015, and pupils on SEN support finishing 17 months, and EHCP holders 27.5 months, behind their peers at 16 (see research/03-send-evidence.md; [EPI, *Breaking Down the Gap* 2025](https://epi.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Breaking-down-the-gap.pdf)). We are not designing a school that copes with this population. We are designing one for which this population is the whole point.

We are also honest about what a school alone can do. **44% of the GCSE disadvantage gap is already locked in by age 11**, and multilevel studies find schools explain only ~10–15% of variance in attainment (~14% of Progress 8) (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md; [EEF/EPI](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/disadvantage-gap-locked-in-new-research-shows)). Our theory of change therefore targets moving the in-school gap by **4–8 months** through attendance, best-bet teaching and targeted tuition — and attacks the larger out-of-school share through the age-2 start, the family hub and place-based work, rather than claiming schooling alone will close a 19-month gap.

The single strategic conviction underneath everything below: **inclusion and high attainment are not a trade-off to be balanced but the same problem solved once.** The best evidence — the EEF's *SEN in Mainstream* guidance and Dixons' "Mountain Rescue" model — says the thing that helps a child with SEND most is not a separate pedagogy but ordinary teaching made excellent (see research/03-send-evidence.md, research/13-dixons.md; [EEF SEND guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/send)). Ofsted's November 2025 report cards, which grade *inclusion* as a standalone area on a five-point scale, now reward this design structurally rather than penalising it (see research/07-governance-structures.md).

## The intake we are designing for

Concrete planning assumptions, not aspirations (canonical numbers in Section 0):

- **All-through, 2–18, deliberately human-scale.** **~1,520 pupils of statutory age (~1,570 with the ~50-place nursery): a 2FE primary (60/year, 420 pupils), a 6FE secondary (180/year, 900 pupils) and a 150-place sixth form** — with secondary year groups of 180 engineered to feel smaller through schools-within-a-school. The 2-year-old start is the design's sharpest answer to the finding that 44% of the gap is set before Year 7 (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md), it enables SEND identification from age two, and it buys a 16-year relationship with each family — the mechanism that carries King Solomon Academy to Progress 8 of +1.06 to +1.18 and Reach Feltham to +0.9 at 35%+ FSM (see research/15-ark-ksa.md, research/12-reach-feltham.md). We are honest on two counts: all-through structure has no proven effect size of its own (the case is relational continuity, and Reach proves it is *compatible* with top-1% outcomes); and because a 2FE primary feeds a 6FE secondary, **only one-third of each Year 7 progresses internally — two-thirds (120 of 180) join from external primaries.** The "no Year 6→7 cliff" claim therefore holds in full for the internal third only; for the external majority the mechanism is the enhanced transition programme (Section 0, point 5), not the structure.
- **~45% FSM6, and we will push registration higher — for families and the FSM factor, not for Pupil Premium.** When free-school-meal eligibility extends to all Universal Credit households in September 2026, aggressive auto-enrolment saves families ~£500/child/year and attracts the NFF FSM factor (~£295 transitional–£505 standard per pupil). But **Pupil Premium does not follow the expanded eligibility** — DfE has confirmed it stays tied to the pre-2026 targeted (≤£7,400/Ever-6) cohort — so PP income is modelled on that cohort only: **~£0.48m** (~126 primary pupils × ~£1,480 + ~270 secondary × £1,075), not on the ~45% headline FSM6 figure (see Section 0; research/06-extended-services.md).
- **Plan for ~15% EHCP intake and a much larger SEND register.** Reach admits children from specialist provision at ~15% EHCP and sometimes spends double the attached funding; NFER warns that genuinely inclusive schools in deprived areas attract up to **6× the EHCP load** of their neighbours (see research/12-reach-feltham.md, research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md). We budget and negotiate admissions for that magnet effect from day one rather than being surprised by it. The dominant needs will be autism (33.5% of EHCPs) and speech, language and communication (22.1%; ~1 in 5 children behind on language, concentrated in poverty) — so oral language is a universal, not a specialist, concern.
- **Funding modelled phase-correctly (2026–27 NFF):** secondary ~£8,200–8,800/pupil deprivation-weighted, primary ~£5,000–6,000, 16–19 ~£4,000–4,800/student, early-years hourly funding for the nursery, plus the ~£0.48m targeted Pupil Premium — a whole-school core income envelope of **~£12.0–12.5m** (Section 0). The high-needs block is frozen for 2026–27, so provision must be viable largely within the notional SEN budget and the first-£6,000 rule (see research/07-governance-structures.md).

## What "success" means

We reject headline Progress 8 as the sole measure — it is biased against deprived, SEND-heavy intakes (2024 disadvantage gap ~0.7) and cannot even be calculated for the 2025–26 cohorts (see research/10-assessment-accountability.md). Success is a basket:

1. **Attainment, contextualised.** Strong English and maths passes for nearly all, benchmarked on contextualised measures (the Fairer Schools Index that puts six Star secondaries in its top 50) and, when P8 returns in 2027, judged against similar-intake schools (see research/14-star-academies.md, research/10-assessment-accountability.md).
2. **Inclusion, measured as retention.** The suspension gap closed (SEN-support pupils are suspended at ~4× the no-SEN rate — 28.86 vs 6.78 per 100 in 2024/25; FSM pupils at ~5× — see research/04-behaviour-culture.md), **zero unexplained exits**, EHCP children kept and thriving in mainstream, and a graded-good-or-better Ofsted inclusion judgement.
3. **Destinations.** Every leaver in a sustained education, employment or training destination 12+ months on — the outcome measure least distorted by intake — targeting closure of the 11.4pp disadvantage gap (83.2% vs 94.6%) (see research/10-assessment-accountability.md).
4. **Attendance and community outcomes.** Attendance is the single biggest tractable lever on the gap: equalising disadvantaged pupils' absence with their peers' would cut **over 4 months** from the age-16 gap, and FSM pupils are 2.5× more likely to be persistently absent (34.8% vs 14.1%) (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md). We target attendance at or above national median for FSM/EHCP pupils, treated as a relationship problem, not a compliance problem — named staff per family, first-day response, home visits — plus high FSM registration and families engaged through intensive personalised liaison (see research/06-extended-services.md).

## Non-negotiable design principles

1. **Inclusion is a whole-school teaching strategy, not a department.** Every teacher is trained and coached in the EEF "Five-a-day" (explicit instruction, cognitive/metacognitive strategies, scaffolding, flexible grouping, technology) — the same strategies that top the Toolkit for disadvantage (metacognition +7–8 months, oracy +6), so the SEND and disadvantage strategies are one strategy (see research/01-closing-the-gap.md, research/02-curriculum-design.md). No differentiation-by-task, no capped "must/should/could" objectives.
2. **Mainstream-first, with specialist capacity on site.** Bases coach the whole school; children spend the majority of their time in mainstream lessons. The design test is Strogilos's "inclusive service, not safe space."
3. **Teacher quality is the only lever that compounds.** We invest in people over programmes and kit — universal instructional coaching, not appraisal.
4. **High expectations delivered relationally, never punitively.** One public standard, enforced through routines, consistency and repair: the behaviour evidence says the "what" matters less than implementation consistency, that the most-improved schools increase *rewards* rather than sanctions (we track a ≥4:1 reward:sanction ratio), and that restorative practice works as a repair layer on top of clear consequences, not as their replacement (see research/04-behaviour-culture.md). An undifferentiated sanction ladder would breach Equality Act duties and fail the inclusion judgement.
5. **Evidence over brand, fidelity over novelty.** Nothing is bought without a named problem, an evidence check and a usage-based evaluation plan — because branded programmes fade from +0.25 to +0.01 at scale when fidelity slips, and even cognitive-science techniques with exceptional lab evidence show smaller, mixed effects in real classrooms when implemented as slogans (see research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md, research/02-curriculum-design.md).
6. **The school is neighbourhood infrastructure**, wrapping food, family, health and enrichment around learning — but on separately-funded footing so services never raid the core budget.
7. **We measure ourselves honestly and publish the uncomfortable numbers** — suspensions, internal-exclusion hours, exits and attendance by SEND and FSM status — as first-class KPIs.

## The evidence-backed bets the whole design rests on

**Bet 1 — Universal instructional coaching replaces performance appraisal.** Every teacher gets personalised, incremental coaching plus twice-weekly whole-staff deliberate practice, protected in the calendar — with coaching capacity costed into middle-leadership time, not goodwill. This is the highest-confidence input we have: Kraft et al.'s meta-analysis gives coaching 0.49 SD on instruction and 0.18 SD on achievement, Dobbie & Fryer found teacher feedback/coaching among the five practices explaining ~45% of charter effectiveness, and Dixons abolished appraisal entirely to do this. Honest flag: effects shrink at scale, quality and focus beat dosage, and Sims et al.'s 2025 meta-analysis found the average PD effect in *preregistered* trials indistinguishable from zero — so we treat coaching as the best-evidenced mechanism available, implemented carefully, not a guaranteed effect size (see research/13-dixons.md, research/17-us-charters.md, research/05-staffing-workforce.md; [Kraft et al. 2018](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/mkraft/files/kraft_blazar_hogan_2018_teacher_coaching.pdf)).

**Bet 2 — Convert the TA budget into qualified-teacher capacity.** Unstructured 1:1 "velcro" TA support delivers roughly zero-to-negative progress; extra qualified subject teachers running flexible grouping (Shotton Hall's +0.58 P8, Dixons' Mountain Rescue) do not. The EEF's updated *Deploying Teaching Assistants* guidance (March 2025) is unambiguous: TAs supplement, never replace, the teacher, and the lowest attainers get *at least as much* teacher time as everyone else. We keep a small, well-trained TA team for *structured* interventions only (~30 min, several times weekly, entry/exit data, scripts and training — worth ~+3–4 months, up to +6 for 1:1) and spend the rest on teachers (see research/03-send-evidence.md, research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md, research/05-staffing-workforce.md; [EEF TA interventions](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/teaching-assistant-interventions)).

**Bet 3 — Two on-site inclusion bases from day one, run as services.** An autism/regulation base and an SLCN base (12–18 places each, **~24–36 funded base places in total**), funded at £10k/place plus top-ups, with dedicated teacher leads, written entry/exit criteria and a guaranteed majority of mainstream lesson time — the Cullum Centre / Carr Manor pattern, architected for the coming 2029 tiered system (universal→targeted→targeted-plus→specialist) rather than the 2014 EHCP model. Every child kept out of a £61,500 independent placement funds substantial in-house capacity (see research/03-send-evidence.md, research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md).

**Bet 4 — Oral language and literacy as the universal deprivation-facing spine.** Screen every child's language at entry (Reception and Y7), employ speech-and-language therapy in-house rather than waiting on NHS lists, and run a three-tier reading pathway with explicit exit criteria plus daily in-timetable tutoring. Small-group tuition is +4 months (+5 for 1:1), oracy interventions +6, and in-school beats after-school in the meta-analytic evidence. The National Tutoring Programme is our cautionary tale: at national scale it delivered ~1 month and only 35–47% of tutored pupils were disadvantaged — so every intervention here has named pupils, protected ratios (≤1:3, 3+ sessions/week), dosage tracking and a kill criterion. The NTP subsidy is gone, so this is a permanent core-budget commitment, not a grant (see research/03-send-evidence.md, research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md, research/01-closing-the-gap.md; [EEF small-group tuition](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/small-group-tuition)).

**Bet 5 — A knowledge-rich curriculum with daily retrieval and formative assessment, and no teaching-to-the-test.** We import Michaela's controversy-free instructional core (subject booklets, daily retrieval, self-quizzing homework, whole-class feedback instead of written marking) onto an ambitious interpretation of the national curriculum — auditing from day one against the reformed curriculum due spring 2027, first teaching September 2028, which the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 makes binding on academies. The causal case is strengthening: the Core Knowledge kindergarten-lottery study found +0.24 SD in reading over grades 3–6, with the largest effect in its one low-income school — direction solid, magnitude uncertain (see research/02-curriculum-design.md). We institutionalise cognitive science through centrally-written materials rather than exhortation, because the EEF's applied review shows classroom effects are smaller than the lab promises when fidelity slips. Assessment is formative (EEF Embedding Formative Assessment: +2 months on Attainment 8, cheapest gains, strongest for the lowest-attaining third), with termly nationally-standardised low-stakes tests, a KS3 rigorous enough for the incoming statutory Year 8 reading test (and for the Year 8 maths and writing assessment the government expects schools to run, non-statutorily), and a ban on predicted GCSE grades before Y10 (see research/11-michaela.md, research/10-assessment-accountability.md).

**Bet 6 — An extended, programmed day wrapped in cradle-to-career services.** Site open 07:45–17:00, with a free breakfast club from 07:45 (now statutory for primaries under the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 — we run it all-through) and a daily 25-minute Morning Meeting (retrieval, culture, appreciations) opening the **compulsory core day of 08:00–15:30 (~33.3 directed hours/week)**. The 15:30–16:30 block is **directed-but-tiered**: compulsory high-dosage tutoring and targeted academic support for the identified target cohort, a guaranteed rotating enrichment entitlement for everyone else — programmed either way. The EEF's +2–3 months for disadvantaged pupils attaches to *this directed, targeted block*, not to voluntary twilight time, precisely because disadvantaged and SEND pupils are the least likely to stay for optional sessions (Section 0). The wrap-around (family hub, attendance mentors, MHST) is run by a legally-separate foundation on philanthropic and Best Start Family Hub state funding, the Reach Feltham split (see research/06-extended-services.md, research/12-reach-feltham.md, research/13-dixons.md).

**Bet 7 — Relational behaviour with backfill and radical transparency.** High-expectations cultures in deprived catchments reliably drift toward repeat suspensions (Ofsted flagged this at Dixons Allerton; Success Academy suspended at ~7× city rates), and nationally FSM pupils are suspended at ~5× and permanently excluded at ~6× the rate of their peers (2024/25). We commit to in-year admissions and backfill by design (Section 0), internal alternatives to suspension — a staffed, time-limited, curriculum-continuous inclusion unit rather than punitive isolation, since SEND/FSM pupils are ~2× as likely to be isolated *even after controlling for behaviour* — SEND screening before any second suspension, regulation spaces, and published exit/suspension/isolation data with internal alarms on FSM/SEND/ethnicity gaps. The 2026 Act's tightened exclusion guidance and off-site-direction review duties make this inspectorially as well as ethically non-negotiable (see research/11-michaela.md, research/17-us-charters.md, research/13-dixons.md, research/04-behaviour-culture.md).

**Bet 8 — Character and destination identity broaden the definition of success.** A Star-style earnable diploma (attendance, behaviour, community service), university-named cohorts from Reception, and a careers curriculum against all eight Gatsby benchmarks give SEND and low-prior-attainment pupils a credible success currency beyond GCSE grades — and make attendance a celebrated, rewarded virtue rather than an enforcement problem (see research/14-star-academies.md, research/15-ark-ksa.md).

## The biggest risks, and how we mitigate them

**Risk 1 — The magnet effect breaks the finances.** An openly inclusive school in a deprived area can attract up to 6× its neighbours' EHCP load just as the high-needs NFF is frozen (2026–27) and after Dixons found pure double-staffing financially unsustainable. *Mitigation:* design provision to be viable within the notional SEN budget and the first-£6,000 rule; use flexible grouping, not literal double-staffing; secure the ~24–36 £10k base places and top-ups before opening, with a contractual cap on EHCP consultations naming the school tied to those funded places (Section 0); and make the local authority a strategic partner on place-planning and resourced-provision commissioning, since councils can now direct admissions and hold £3bn for specialist places (see research/03-send-evidence.md, research/07-governance-structures.md).

**Risk 2 — The high-expectations culture becomes exclusionary.** Every operator we admire (Michaela, the US charters, Dixons) carries documented exclusion or attrition risk; SEN-support pupils start ~4× more likely to be suspended, and warm-strict itself has no trial evidence — we adopt it on coherence with the routines/consistency evidence and operator track records, not RCT proof. *Mitigation:* import the instructional core but explicitly *reject* the undifferentiated sanction ladder; build an SEMH pathway, nurture provision and relational repair alongside the standard (restorative language universally, but never as a replacement for consequences — the LEIP null result); treat suspensions, isolation hours and reward:sanction ratios as governor-level KPIs with published data and alarms; and expect no self-selection dividend, because our families will not opt into an extreme ethos the way Michaela's do (see research/11-michaela.md, research/17-us-charters.md, research/04-behaviour-culture.md).

**Risk 3 — We over-promise and under-replicate.** Flagship effects rarely replicate — Dixons' trust-wide P8 is +0.11 against its flagship's +0.76, XP Doncaster fell from +0.21 to −0.77 when project-based learning displaced explicit teaching, and Reach's own P8 swung from +1.11 to +0.50. *Mitigation:* treat these schools as existence proofs, not templates; make implementation fidelity the active ingredient with a senior leader owning each core system; set public tripwires (e.g. contextualised progress ≥ 0 within four years of the first cohort); build standardised pre/post measurement in from day one; and set governor expectations for a 3–5-year horizon to positive progress — remembering that London Challenge took a decade and Opportunity Areas failed in five years (see research/13-dixons.md, research/16-xp-doncaster.md, research/12-reach-feltham.md, research/01-closing-the-gap.md).

**Risk 4 — We cannot staff it.** Teacher shortages concentrate exactly in catchments like ours: unfilled vacancies run at six times the 2010/11 rate, and the most disadvantaged schools run on far less experience (34% of teachers under five years vs 20% in the least disadvantaged). Leavers cite workload (84%), wellbeing (75%) and behaviour (44%) ahead of pay. *Mitigation:* the retention offer *is* the design — centralised SLT-run behaviour systems, no written marking, centrally-resourced curriculum and universal coaching attack the top three leaving drivers directly; we bank the £6,000 DfE targeted retention incentive our deprivation profile qualifies for and budget our own supplement for shortage and SEND-specialist roles; we accept an ECT-heavy workforce and engineer around it with timetabled mentor release; and we pilot flexible working (Dixons-style nine-day fortnight) as an evaluated pilot, not an article of faith (see research/05-staffing-workforce.md).
