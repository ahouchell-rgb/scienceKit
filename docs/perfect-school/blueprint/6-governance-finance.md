# Section 6 — Governance, Finance and Accountability

This section fixes the legal vehicle, the governance and leadership machinery, the admissions position, the money, and the accountability dashboard. It costs the **canonical school: an all-through 2–18 comprehensive of ~1,520 pupils on statutory roll (~1,570 including the ~50-place nursery)** — a ~50-place early-years nursery, a 2FE primary (420), a 6FE secondary (900) and a 150-place sixth form — at ~45% FSM6 headline in a high-deprivation, high-SEND catchment (see Section 0 canon). The wider curriculum, SEND and wrap-around layers are designed in Sections 2, 4 and 5; here we design the corporate spine that carries them and prove the whole design is affordable. Every major choice is anchored in the research briefs and in the post-2024 policy settlement: the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (Royal Assent 29 April 2026), the abolition of the free-school presumption, Ofsted report cards (live 10 Nov 2025), the November 2025 Curriculum and Assessment Review final report, and the February 2026 Schools White Paper on SEND.

## 1. Structural vehicle: found the school inside a mid-size, inclusion-covenanted MAT — not standalone, not maintained

The evidence is unambiguous that **structure is not the lever; operator quality is**. EPI finds no systematic MAT-vs-LA performance difference, with within-type variation worth ~5 GCSE grades between the best and worst groups (see research/07-governance-structures.md; https://epi.org.uk/publications-and-research/the-features-of-effective-school-groups/). After the CWS Act, academies and maintained schools now face near-identical rules on national curriculum, QTS (Sept 2027), a pay floor, and admissions cooperation — so the badge decides little. Note one hard constraint the Act adds: under s.54, **academies must teach the reformed national curriculum** (published spring 2027, first teaching Sept 2028), so any claim of "curriculum freedom" is time-limited and the trust must budget for the 2028 transition (research/02-curriculum-design.md; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/21/contents).

I therefore reject two options. **A standalone single-academy trust (SAT)** cannot afford the machinery the evidence says actually produces top-decile outcomes in deprived catchments: a universal instructional-coaching engine, a centralised data engine, and back-office capacity (see research/13-dixons.md; research/15-ark-ksa.md; research/17-us-charters.md — Dobbie & Fryer's five tenets, of which teacher coaching and data-driven instruction are two). **A large MAT (10+ schools)** buys that capacity but carries EPI's documented downside for exactly our cohort: higher persistent absence, suspension rates, unexplained exits, and teacher turnover (~19.5% in large MATs vs 14.4% median LA).

**Position: open the school through the LA-run presumption competition (from Sept 2026 the only realistic route besides an LA-proposed maintained school), as the flagship or an early school of a mid-size (roughly 5–12 school), regionally clustered, inclusion-focused MAT** — either an existing strong operator (Dixons/Star/Ark-type) or a new trust formed around this design. The trust must sign a binding **inclusion covenant**: zero unexplained exits, published termly suspension rates, and acceptance of LA-directed admissions (a power councils gained in June 2026). This captures the large-MAT disadvantaged-attainment advantage while contracting away its exclusion risk. Assume the **LA provides site and capital** either way.

**Split the architecture (Reach Feltham model, see research/12-reach-feltham.md).** The trust runs the school on its GAG budget; a **legally separate charitable Foundation** runs the wrap-around hub (family team, breakfast/HAF, hub services) on philanthropic and contract income of roughly £0.4–1m/year, so extended services can never raid the core teaching budget. Note that free breakfast-club provision is now a **statutory duty for the primary phase** under the CWS Act 2026 (research/01-closing-the-gap.md), so the universal-breakfast element sits on the core budget while the Foundation carries the discretionary family/HAF layer. Bid to host a state-funded **Best Start Family Hub** on site (every LA by April 2026, SEND practitioners funded) to convert most of that philanthropic layer into public money.

## 2. Governance design: Academy Trust Handbook 2025, built for MAT-level Ofsted from day one

Build to the [Academy Trust Handbook 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academy-trust-handbook/academy-trust-handbook-2025-effective-from-1-september-2025) and the coming CWS-Act MAT inspection (from ~2027), not retrofitted later:

- **Members (≥5, majority independent of the board)** holding the trust to its charitable objects.
- **Skills-based trustee board** with a genuine SEND/inclusion specialist, a qualified finance trustee, and a safeguarding lead. An **Audit & Risk Committee** and a **CFO** are mandatory; executive pay must follow a documented, defensible policy.
- **Ark-style earned autonomy** (see research/15-ark-ksa.md): a scheme of delegation that explicitly labels which policies are mandatory-and-central (safeguarding, inclusion covenant, assessment windows, behaviour framework) versus customisable, with low-notice internal peer-review visits rather than data drops.
- A **Local Governing Body** that is advisory on strategy but real on community voice: reserved seats for **parents, a SEND parent, and a community representative**.
- **Board KPIs mirror the six Ofsted report-card areas, with inclusion listed first** — because inclusion is now a standalone graded judgement (see research/10-assessment-accountability.md; research/04-behaviour-culture.md) and it is what this school is built to win on. Board duties also now include **statutory review of any off-site direction** (from 1 August 2026) and compliance with the **revised suspension/exclusion guidance** (from 26 July 2026, research/04-behaviour-culture.md) — both wired into the termly cycle rather than handled ad hoc.

## 3. Leadership structure

A Headteacher (executive head across phases, with heads of school for primary and secondary) leads an SLT deliberately weighted toward inclusion and pastoral care:

- **NPQ-qualified SENCO/INCo on SLT** leading a distributed inclusion team, with Assess-Plan-Do-Review owned by classroom teachers (see research/03-send-evidence.md). Never train the INCo without the Head alongside (Dixons rule).
- **A single multidisciplinary Inclusion/Pastoral/Safeguarding directorate** ("Mountain Rescue" model, research/13-dixons.md; research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md), DSL-led, combining SEND, safeguarding, behaviour, attendance, counselling, the in-house speech-and-language therapist and the family team — so specialist roles (counsellor, key worker, mentor) are strictly separated from teaching and there is no waiting-time cliff edge. Because the DSL cannot also personally lead behaviour, SEND, attendance and family support, the directorate is resourced with **deputy DSLs and named DSL cover spanning the trust and the Foundation** (safeguarding detail in Section 5).
- **A Deputy-Head-level Senior Attendance Champion** (statutory requirement, see research/06-extended-services.md) — load-bearing given attendance is the single biggest tractable lever on the gap (equalising disadvantaged absence would cut the age-16 gap by >4 months, research/01-closing-the-gap.md).
- **No performance appraisal.** Replace it with universal incremental instructional coaching plus twice-weekly whole-staff deliberate practice, protected in the calendar (Dixons; Kraft et al. 0.49 SD instruction / 0.18 SD achievement). Honest evidence flag: coaching is the best-evidenced CPD available but its effects shrink at scale and Sims et al.'s 2025 meta-analysis finds the average PD effect in *preregistered* trials near zero (research/05-staffing-workforce.md) — so coach **quality and focus are budgeted ahead of raw coaching hours**, with protected coach release (~2–3 periods/week per coach) treated as the binding constraint.
- **Grow-your-own pipeline** via school-based ITT with a SCITT/university partner (Star, Reach) — a tough catchment cannot rely on the open labour market, where the most disadvantaged schools already run with 34% of teachers under five years' experience vs 20% in the least disadvantaged (research/05-staffing-workforce.md).

## 4. Admissions

Admissions is load-bearing for this design — it determines whether the intake actually matches the ~45%-FSM6, high-SEND planning assumption, and it is where Bet 7 (in-year backfill) and the SEND magnet-effect mitigation live. The published position (see Section 0 canon, point 4):

- **PAN per phase:** Nursery ~50 (no automatic onward right); **Reception 60**; **Year 7 180**; **Sixth form 150** (published academic entry criteria that do **not** exclude the school's own SEND/low-attainment leavers — an L2/resit and vocational route sits behind the sixth form, Section 5).
- **Fair banding, not distance-first.** Oversubscription is resolved by a standardised CAT4-type test on application, allocating across ability bands so the intake matches the comprehensive planning assumption rather than skewing via a distance-only rule. Order: (1) looked-after/previously looked-after; (2) siblings; (3) fair-banding allocation, with **straight-line distance as the in-band tiebreak**. EHCPs naming the school are admitted via LA consultation **outside** the oversubscription criteria.
- **Nursery→Reception:** nursery attendance **confers no Reception place** (legally required; blocks backdoor selection); families apply through the normal round.
- **Year 6→Year 7:** internal Year 6 pupils hold a **guaranteed progression right** to Year 7, counting within the 180 PAN — **60 internal, 120 external**. The "no Year 6→7 cliff" claim therefore holds in full only for the internal third; the external two-thirds are served by the enhanced transition programme (Section 5), not by all-through structure.
- **In-year admissions / backfill (Bet 7):** a published commitment to **fill every casual vacancy in every year group** — no "full" gaming — including through the **Fair Access Protocol** (full participation, published), the anti-off-rolling counterpart to the exit dashboard.
- **EHCP consultation cap:** a **contractual cap on EHCP consultations naming the school, tied to the ~24–36 funded base places**. Beyond the cap, admission is negotiated with the LA as place-planning partner — this is how the 6× SEND-magnet effect is bounded without off-rolling.

## 5. Indicative annual budget (steady state, ~1,520 pupils, 2026–27 rates)

Rebuilt from the ground up for the **canonical 2–18 school** on 2026–27 phase-correct funding rates (Section 0 canon; NFF 2026–27 formulae, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/schools-block-funding-formulae-2026-to-2027/schools-block-funding-formulae-2026-to-2027; research/07-governance-structures.md). The arithmetic is shown per line.

| Income line | Basis (arithmetic) | £ (indicative) |
|---|---|---|
| Early years | ~50 places on the EY hourly formula (2-year-old entitlement; 3–4 universal/extended hours) — a separate funding stream | ~£0.35m |
| Primary NFF | 420 × ~£5,500 (basic entitlement £4,064, deprivation/LPA/EAL-weighted to ~£5,000–6,000) | ~£2.3m |
| Secondary NFF | 900 × ~£8,200–8,800 (basic £5,686 KS3/£6,410 KS4, deprivation-weighted) | ~£7.4–7.9m |
| Sixth form (16–19) | 150 × ~£4,400 (ESFA 16–19 NFF base + disadvantage/retention uplifts, ~£4,000–4,800) | ~£0.66m |
| Pupil Premium (**targeted ≤£7,400 / Ever-6 cohort only**) | ~126 primary × £1,480 (= £0.19m) + ~270 secondary × £1,075 (= £0.29m) | ~£0.48m |
| High needs | ~24–36 base places @ ~£10k (= £0.24–0.36m) + per-pupil top-ups above the £6k threshold | ~£0.5–1.0m |
| **Total core income (on-GAG)** | | **~£12.0–12.5m** |
| *Foundation (separate entity): wrap-around hub* | *philanthropic + contract income, ring-fenced, off-GAG* | *£0.4–1.0m* |

Two corrections this rebuild makes explicit. First, **Pupil Premium is modelled on the targeted cohort only** — it is **not** paid on the September-2026 Universal-Credit FSM expansion (DfE confirmed; research/01-closing-the-gap.md). Newly eligible "expanded-FSM" pupils deliver family savings (~£500/child/year) and the NFF FSM factor (~£295 transitional–£505) only, so pushing FSM registration higher is worth doing for families and the FSM factor but adds **no PP**. Applying £1,075 to all ~540 FSM6 pupils (the old error) overstated PP as ~£0.58m; the correct targeted figure is **~£0.48m**. Second, the previous draft costed only an 11–16 slice with **no** EYFS, primary, sixth-form or primary-PP lines; those phases are now funded above.

Indicative spend priorities (tiered EEF model — most spend on teaching, then targeted academic support, then wider strategies; research/01-closing-the-gap.md):

- **Staffing ~76–78% (~£9.4m)**, with the deliberate structural choice to **convert most of the TA budget into extra qualified subject teachers** for flexible grouping in English and maths (Dixons/Shotton Hall; research/13-dixons.md; research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md). This is **cost-neutral in total staff spend** — fewer TAs, more teachers, so net teacher headcount rises but net payroll does not — retaining a *smaller, better-trained* TA team deployed only on scheduled, scripted structured interventions (+3–4 months when done well; zero-to-negative for unstructured 1:1 "velcro" support, per EEF's 2025 Deploying TAs guidance, research/03-send-evidence.md; research/05-staffing-workforce.md).
- **Retention engineered around working conditions first, money second.** The top leaving drivers are workload, wellbeing and behaviour, not pay (research/05-staffing-workforce.md), so the centralised behaviour system, whole-class-feedback (no written-marking) policy and centrally-resourced curriculum are the primary retention spend. On top, **bank the DfE targeted retention incentive** (up to £6,000/yr for early-career STEM teachers — our deprivation profile qualifies for the maximum) and budget a modest school-funded supplement (~10–16% of salary, per the Chilean evidence) for shortage-subject and SEND-specialist roles.
- **Timetabled high-dosage tutoring** as a core, permanent line (the NTP subsidy ended Aug 2024, so this is now own-budget): groups of ≤3, 30 min, 3×/week in 10–12-week blocks, at ~£1,500–1,700/pupil all-in (Apollo 20 benchmark, research/08-literacy-numeracy-catchup.md; research/17-us-charters.md). **Cohort and cost, stated explicitly:** ~120–160 named places a year, prioritised from the entry screening data and weighted to Year 7 catch-up — an annual line of **~£0.18–0.27m**, delivered on Section 2's staffing model (permanent trained HLTAs/tutors, ~£15–25k per FTE covering 40–60 places, so ~3–4 tutor FTEs sitting inside the staffing block above). **Funding split:** ~£0.15m charged to Pupil Premium — leaving roughly two-thirds of the ~£0.48m PP envelope for the tiered teaching and wider-strategy spend — with the balance carried on the deprivation-weighted NFF. The cohort is deliberately *not* the full ~396-pupil targeted-PP cohort: tutoring everyone eligible (~£0.6m) would swallow PP outright, and the NTP showed that undifferentiated coverage dilutes the effect anyway. The NTP lesson is the discipline this line enforces: **name the pupils, track dosage, protect the ≤1:3 ratio** — at national scale the NTP delivered only ~1 month because only 35–47% of places reached disadvantaged pupils (research/01-closing-the-gap.md).
- **Coaching + deliberate-practice time** funded ahead of smaller classes or generic spend — traditional inputs are uncorrelated with effectiveness (Dobbie & Fryer).
- **Cheap, evidence-led wins**: embedding formative assessment (+2 months A8 at very low cost), whole-school metacognition/oracy routines (research/02-curriculum-design.md), standardised assessment licences, and parental text-messaging (~£6/pupil/year, +1 month maths) before any five-figure EdTech platform (research/09-edtech-ai.md; research/10-assessment-accountability.md).

Every child kept out of a **~£61.5k independent special placement** (vs ~£23.9k state special vs ~£10k-plus-top-up on-site base, research/03-send-evidence.md) funds substantial in-house capacity — the on-site inclusion base is the educational *and* the financial win.

## 6. Data and accountability dashboard

Three tiers, deliberately low-stakes on pupils and high-stakes on the system:

1. **Weekly "lost-learning" dashboard** (Ted Wragg model, research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md): attendance, lesson removals, suspensions and internal-inclusion-unit days, disaggregated for SEND/PP/social-care cohorts and against the FSM/SEND/ethnicity exit alarms that guard against off-rolling (research/17-us-charters.md). Suspension equity is a first-class metric, not a footnote: nationally FSM pupils are suspended at ~5× and EHCP pupils at ~4× the rate of their peers (DfE 2024/25, research/04-behaviour-culture.md), and Thornton (2026) finds SEND/FSM pupils ~2× as likely to be internally isolated *even after controlling for behaviour* — that 2× disparity is the internal-exclusion metric to beat. **Attendance leads the dashboard** as the single biggest tractable lever (research/01-closing-the-gap.md).
2. **One standardised, nationally benchmarked assessment window per term (max twice-yearly per subject at KS3)** — Smartgrade/United, NFER or GL — giving standardised scores and question-level analysis, feeding a **CAT4 baseline** as the interim progress anchor while KS2-based Progress 8 is **suspended for the 2025 and 2026 results and returns for 2027** (research/10-assessment-accountability.md). **No teacher-predicted GCSE grades or flightpaths before Y10 full past papers** (Dixons: raw scores + cohort averages only). **Comparative judgement** (No More Marking, 0.96 reliability) for extended writing.
3. **Destinations as a headline KPI**: named tracking of every leaver for 12+ months, targeting closure of the 11.4pp disadvantaged sustained-destination gap — the outcome least distorted by intake. Alongside GCSE outcomes, governors watch **leading indicators from year one** (attendance, reading age, oracy), because the honest theory of change is that an excellent school moves a disadvantaged cohort by months, not that it erases a 19-month gap alone (research/01-closing-the-gap.md).

Board KPIs map one-to-one onto the six report-card areas, inclusion first.

## 7. Ofsted-readiness without teaching to the framework

Publish a strong **inclusion strategy** (mandatory Sept 2026) and pre-empt Ofsted's "similar schools" contextual model by benchmarking ourselves against high-FSM comparators and evidencing **progress from starting points** — because raw national comparison will read as "needs attention" for a deprived intake (above-average-FSM schools are ~3× more likely to fall below "expected standard" for achievement, research/10-assessment-accountability.md). The renewed framework (from 10 Nov 2025) grades **curriculum and teaching** and **inclusion** as separate areas on a five-point scale (research/02-curriculum-design.md; research/04-behaviour-culture.md), with inclusion case-sampling of individual vulnerable pupils — so readiness is a by-product of doing the inclusion, coaching and formative-assessment work well, not a separate performance. Institute a **no-teaching-to-the-test guardrail**: full curriculum breadth for all sets, no early entry to game measures, governor-level review of any practice that narrows the taught curriculum for lower attainers.

## 8. The three biggest risks

1. **Frozen high-needs funding meets the SEND magnet effect.** The high-needs NFF is suspended for 2026–27 (allocations rolled forward), yet inclusive schools in deprived areas can attract up to **6× neighbours' EHCP load** (NFER, research/18-send-inclusion-exemplars.md). *Mitigation:* design tier-1/targeted provision viable **within the notional SEN budget and the first-£6,000 rule**; cap resourced-base places contractually (the admissions cap in §4); secure £10k-per-place base funding, top-ups and cluster-pooled Inclusion Fund allocations from the LA *before* opening; treat the LA as a place-planning partner, not an adversary.

2. **High-expectations culture drifts into repeat suspensions**, failing the new standalone inclusion grade (Dixons Allerton flagged by Ofsted; Success Academy at ~7× city rates; SEND pupils already suspended at ~4× and FSM at ~5×, research/13-dixons.md; research/17-us-charters.md; research/04-behaviour-culture.md). *Mitigation:* a **warm-strict spine with a relational repair layer** — centralised same-day SLT-run detentions (certainty over severity) with a mandatory 2-minute restorative conversation before release, a tracked reward:sanction ratio targeting ≥4:1 (Behaviour Hubs finding); suspensions as a board KPI with internal alarms; a **staffed internal inclusion unit** (time-limited, curriculum-continuous, SEND-screened on second referral) in place of punitive isolation; the published backfill/in-year commitment (§4); and graduated, SEND-plan-documented enforcement — a communication/SEMH screen **before any second suspension** — rather than an undifferentiated sanction ladder (Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duty; revised statutory exclusions guidance from 26 July 2026, research/04-behaviour-culture.md; research/11-michaela.md).

3. **A new school lacks improvement capacity, and turnarounds lag.** Even Dixons' trust-wide P8 (+0.11) sits far below its flagship (+0.76), and turnarounds take 3–5 years to reach positive progress (research/13-dixons.md; research/14-star-academies.md). The comparators tell an honest timescale story: the London effect took ~a decade of coherent school improvement, while five-year Opportunity Areas showed no attainment impact (research/01-closing-the-gap.md). *Mitigation:* found the school inside an established inclusion-focused MAT with a ready coaching and data engine rather than building from zero; own the teacher pipeline via school-based ITT; **write governance and funding commitments for the long haul**; and set governors an honest, dated tripwire — **P8 (redesigned) ≥ 0 within four years of the first Year 11 cohort** — with contextualised interim leading indicators (attendance, reading age, oracy) until then.
