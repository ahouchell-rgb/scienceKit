# Evidence Index — Perfect School Project

Quick-reference bank for the 22 research briefs in `research/01-*.md` to `research/22-*.md`. Each brief was compiled July 2026 and verified against the post-2024 UK policy landscape (Curriculum & Assessment Review, Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, SEND Schools White Paper, Ofsted's renewed framework). Full brief = full citations; this index pulls out what each one establishes and its load-bearing claims, with source URLs where available.

---

## Closing the gap

### 01 — What Actually Closes the Disadvantage Attainment Gap
**Establishes:** the GCSE disadvantage gap (19.0 months, 2025) is mostly set before secondary school and schools explain only a minority of the variance — so a school must combine best-bet teaching with attendance and family-level work, not expect curriculum alone to close it.
- 44% of the GCSE gap is locked in by age 11; schools explain only ~10–15% of attainment variance ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/disadvantage-gap-locked-in-new-research-shows), [IZA](https://docs.iza.org/dp11372.pdf))
- EEF Toolkit best bets: metacognition +7–8 months, feedback +6, oracy +6, phonics +5, 1:1 tuition +5, small-group +4 ([EEF Toolkit](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit))
- National Tutoring Programme delivered only ~+1 month at scale, and only 35–47% of places reached disadvantaged pupils — effect sizes don't survive loose implementation ([NFER Y3](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/national-tutoring-programme-year-3-impact-evaluation/))
- Equalising attendance alone would close >4 months of the age-16 gap; FSM pupils are 2.5x more likely to be persistently absent (34.8% vs 14.1%) ([EPI 2025](https://epi.org.uk/annual-report-2025-disadvantage/), [Health Foundation](https://www.health.org.uk/evidence-hub/education/relationship-between-school-absences-and-attainment))
- Pupil premium money alone didn't narrow the gap; London Challenge-era, decade-long, well-led school improvement did more ([Gorard et al.](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02671522.2019.1677759))

### 08 — Early Reading & Numeracy Catch-up at Secondary Transfer (Year 7)
**Establishes:** roughly a quarter of Year 7 nationally (far higher in deprived catchments) arrives below expected standard, and the answer is diagnosis-then-tiered-intervention, staffed and timetabled — not a branded catch-up scheme bought off the shelf.
- ~25%/26% of Year 7 below expected standard in reading/maths (2025 KS2 data) ([DfE](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/key-stage-2-attainment/2024-25-revised))
- 1:1 tuition +5 months, small-group +4 months; optimal dosage ~30 min, 3–5x/week, ~10 weeks ([EEF Toolkit](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/one-to-one-tuition))
- Programme effects fade badly at scale: across 7 EEF efficacy-to-effectiveness scale-ups, mean effect fell from 0.25 to 0.01 SD — implementation fidelity, not brand, is the active ingredient ([EEF review](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED625479.pdf))
- HFL's KS3 fluency protocol (8 weeks, twice-weekly repeated reading) reports ~18 months' comprehension-age gain, though this is pre-post, not RCT data ([HFL](https://www.hfleducation.org/reading-fluency/key-stage-3-ks3-reading-fluency-project/impact-and-outcomes))
- Catch Up Numeracy went from +0.21 SD at efficacy to null (and −2 months for FSM) at effectiveness scale — explicitly not recommended ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/catch-up-numeracy-2015))

## Curriculum & assessment

### 02 — Curriculum design for disadvantaged learners
**Establishes:** a knowledge-rich, cumulative curriculum is itself a literacy and equity intervention, and the 2025 Curriculum & Assessment Review reshapes the statutory floor a new school must be built against.
- Core Knowledge lottery study: +0.24 SD reading over grades 3–6 (much larger, ITT ≈0.94 SD, in the one low-income school — treat magnitude as uncertain) ([Grissmer et al. 2023](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai23-755))
- Mastery learning: EEF rates +5 months, but England's own Mathematics Mastery RCTs found only ~+1 month, not significant — mastery works only when the reteach loop is real
- Retrieval/spacing lab evidence is strong (Adesope et al., g≈0.50–0.61) but the EEF's 2021 classroom review found much smaller, mixed real-classroom effects ([EEF cog-sci review](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/evidence-reviews/cognitive-science-approaches-in-the-classroom))
- New national curriculum due spring 2027 (first teaching Sept 2028); academies must teach it under the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 ([CAR final report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/curriculum-and-assessment-review-final-report))
- Michaela: Progress 8 +2.55 (2024), highest in England 3rd year running, on a knowledge-rich/retrieval model ([Michaela results](https://www.michaela.education/about-us/exam-results))

### 10 — Assessment & Accountability: Ofsted Report Cards, Progress 8 and Deprivation
**Establishes:** Ofsted's new report cards and P8 both still penalise deprived intakes even after reform, so a new school's accountability strategy must be built around inclusion, context, and internal formative assessment rather than raw comparison.
- Above-average-FSM schools are almost 3x more likely to be graded below "expected standard" for achievement; Ofsted itself concedes the disadvantage-grade relationship ([Schools Week](https://schoolsweek.co.uk/ofsted-admits-relationship-between-disadvantage-and-achievement-judgment/))
- P8 cannot be calculated for 2025/2026 GCSE cohorts (KS2 tests cancelled 2020–21); returns 2026/27 under a reformed model (EBacc scrapped) ([PolicyBristol](https://www.bristol.ac.uk/policybristol/policy-briefings/progress-8-2025-6/))
- Contextualising P8 (FSM/EAL/SEND/mobility) cuts schools below the −0.5 floor from ~300 to ~240 ([FFT Datalab](https://ffteducationdatalab.org.uk/2024/05/contextualising-progress-8/))
- EEF's Embedding Formative Assessment RCT (140 schools): +2 months Attainment 8, biggest gains for lowest-prior-attainment third ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/embedding-formative-assessment))
- Comparative judgement (No More Marking) gives writing-assessment reliability up to 0.96 without rubric-driven teaching-to-the-mark-scheme ([study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/0969594X.2019.1700212))

### 20 — Department-Level Curriculum, Assessment and Feedback Operations
**Establishes:** the classroom-level machinery (retrieval starters, knowledge organisers, whole-class feedback) that makes curriculum and assessment evidence actually run week to week, with government policy itself now discouraging comment-by-comment marking.
- Retrieval practice: only 2 of 10 reviewed study techniques rated "high utility" (practice testing, distributed practice); classroom effect sizes (~0.5) now closely match lab effects (0.62) ([Agarwal et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9))
- DfE's 2016 Marking Policy Review Group concluded detailed written marking of every piece of work is not effective feedback, and Ofsted does not require any specific marking volume ([DfE](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/594027/6.2798_DFE_MB_Reducing_Teacher_Workload_Pamphlet_20161207_print.pdf))
- Homework at secondary rated +5 months (EEF), strongest when it's retrieval on already-taught content, not new independent learning
- Michaela's weekly-quiz-plus-cumulative-recall cadence, and Dixons' "messy marking"/whole-class feedback, are both concrete named alternatives to individual marking
- No causal RCT directly compares whole-class feedback to traditional marking on attainment — the case rests on workload-neutral-or-better outcomes plus general feedback evidence, not a dedicated trial

## Behaviour & culture

### 04 — Behaviour and School Culture in High-Deprivation Contexts
**Establishes:** consistency, routines and leadership — not any single named policy (warm-strict, restorative practice, centralised detentions) — are what the evidence actually supports; several popular components (warm-strict, centralised detentions) are evidence-thin but operationally coherent.
- EEF rates behaviour interventions +3–4 months on average with wide variation by quality; both classroom-management skill *and* pupil SEL skills are independently effective ([EEF/Moore review](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED612210.pdf))
- Restorative practice evidence is genuinely mixed: Lancet/INCLUSIVE found small bullying reduction (−0.08) but no effect on aggression; London LEIP trial found null-to-negative effects ([Lancet](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(18)31782-3/fulltext))
- FSM suspension rate 26.66 vs 5.41 per 100 non-FSM (~5x); SEN-support pupils suspended at 28.86 vs 6.78 no-SEN ([DfE 2024/25](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions-in-england/2024-25))
- Timpson Review: 78% of permanent exclusions go to pupils with SEN, in-need status, or FSM ([Timpson Review](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5cfe7d8de5274a0906be72c8/Timpson_review.pdf))
- Internal exclusion: 8.3% of pupils isolated in a typical week; SEND/FSM pupils ~2x more likely even after controlling for behaviour ([Thornton 2026](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/berj.70049))

### 19 — Behaviour Systems, Operationally: Minute-to-Minute in the Department and Classroom
**Establishes:** the near-universal operational shape (a 3–5 stage consequence ladder, centralised at the serious end) has almost no direct trial evidence behind its precise mechanics, but is the converged practice of every named high-performing operator studied.
- The C1–C4 consequence-ladder structure recurs near-verbatim across dozens of published UK secondary policies ([example policies](https://www.caldew.cumbria.sch.uk/uploads/1090/Behaviour_Ladder_-_Sep_24..pdf/))
- From 26 July 2026, DfE statutory guidance requires schools to record classroom "removals" as a distinct, monitored category for the first time ([HCR Law](https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/dfe-issues-new-statutory-guidance-on-suspensions-and-school-exclusions/))
- 313,008 suspensions and 3,715 permanent exclusions in autumn term 2024 alone; FSM rates run 4–5x non-FSM ([SecEd](https://www.sec-ed.co.uk/content/news/school-suspensions-autumn-2024-dfe-data))
- Behaviour-specific praise at 4:1–5:1 praise:reprimand ratios has genuine applied-behaviour-analysis evidence, unlike most of the ladder mechanics ([synthesis](https://www.skillbuildersaba.com/blog/how-to-use-behavior-specific-praise-effectively))
- ~7 minutes lost per 30 minutes of teaching to low-level misbehaviour, unchanged since 2022 (National Behaviour Survey 2024/25)

## SEND & inclusion

### 03 — SEND Provision That Works in Mainstream Settings
**Establishes:** the SEND system is being redesigned around the individual (four-tier model, EHCPs reserved for the specialist tier) starting ~2029, and the causal evidence base for what actually works remains thin — plan for the trialled ingredients (explicit instruction, oral language, structured TA interventions), not for placement-model claims.
- February 2026 Schools White Paper proposes universal→targeted→targeted plus→specialist tiers with digital Individual Support Plans; EHCPs reserved for the specialist tier from ~2029 ([Commons Library CBP-10550](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10550/))
- 638,700 EHCPs (Jan 2025, +10.8% y/y, demand +140% since 2015); SEN support 14.8% of pupils ([DfE](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/education-health-and-care-plans/2025))
- Attainment gap at 16: SEN support pupils ~17.1 months, EHCP pupils ~27.5 months behind ([EPI 2025](https://epi.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Breaking-down-the-gap.pdf))
- TA-led structured interventions +4 months; unstructured/"velcro" deployment ≈zero or negative ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/teaching-assistant-interventions))
- 1.9m UK children (~1 in 5) behind with talking and understanding, concentrated in deprived areas — SLCN is the second most common EHCP need at 22.1% ([Speech and Language UK](https://speechandlanguage.org.uk/the-issue/))

### 18 — SEND inclusion exemplars: English mainstream schools and trusts
**Establishes:** several named English schools already combine high deprivation with genuinely strong inclusion, using resourced provisions as a "bridge not a silo" and inclusion led from SLT level — largely case-study evidence, not RCT, but the closest thing to an operational blueprint available.
- Carr Manor (Leeds, all-through): Outstanding in all five areas 2025; 12-place Mosaic resourced provision with ~50% mainstream lesson time ([school report](https://carrmanor.org.uk/2025/03/10/ofsted-inspection-2025/))
- Shotton Hall (Peterlee): P8 +0.58 in 2024, disadvantaged pupils progressing faster than peers, using qualified-subject-teacher-led intervention instead of TAs ([GOV.UK IMF](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inclusive-mainstream-fund-2026-to-2027/inclusive-mainstream-fund-best-practice-for-schools--2))
- Heritage High School: halved suspensions within a year after SLT-wide Inclusive Leadership training ([Who is Losing Learning?](https://the-difference.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/FINAL-Who-is-Losing-Learning-Solutions-Report.pdf))
- High-SEND schools can hold up to 6x the EHCP load of neighbouring schools, and disproportionately serve disadvantaged communities ([NFER 2025](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/high-send-schools-understanding-the-uneven-distribution-of-pupils-with-send-across-england-s-mainstream-schools/))
- Ormiston Academies Trust's Specialist Internal Provisions: dedicated teacher lead, explicit entry/exit criteria, "a stepping stone, not a final destination" ([OAT case study](https://inclusioninpractice.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Ormiston_Case_Study-vF.pdf))

### 21 — SEND at the classroom and department level
**Establishes:** the EEF's "Five-a-day" adaptive-teaching repertoire and disciplined TA deployment are the load-bearing mechanisms at classroom level, and exclusion data shows current practice is not, in fact, reconciling consistency with disability adjustment.
- EEF's Five-a-day: explicit instruction, cognitive/metacognitive strategies, scaffolding, flexible grouping, technology — solid evidence for each component, but the "bundle" itself is professional consensus, not a trialled intervention ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/supporting-high-quality-teaching-for-pupils-with-send))
- Permanent exclusion rate: SEN support 0.14, EHCP 0.11, vs whole-cohort 0.04 — SEN-support pupils excluded at ~3.5x the overall rate ([DfE](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/suspensions-and-permanent-exclusions-in-england/2023-24))
- "Velcro TA" pattern (permanent 1:1) produces *less* progress than no TA at all, via TA-talk substitution and learned helplessness ([EEF MITA guidance](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/teaching-assistants))
- Statutory duty for digital Individual Support Plans (Targeted/Targeted Plus tiers) is coming via National Inclusion Standards (early rollout 2025/26, full 2028) ([Commons Library CBP-10550](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10550/))
- There is essentially no experimental literature on how to run a consistent behaviour system with disability adjustments — this brief flags that gap explicitly rather than dressing up borrowed confidence

## Staffing & coaching

### 05 — Staffing and Workforce in a Hard-to-Staff, High-Deprivation Catchment
**Establishes:** recruitment/retention in deprived schools is a working-conditions problem before a pay problem, and money only works for attraction in large, sustained doses.
- Leavers cite workload (84%), poor wellbeing (75%), inspection pressure (64%), behaviour (44%) — ahead of pay ([DfE Working Lives](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/working-lives-of-teachers-and-leaders))
- In the most disadvantaged schools, 34% of teachers have under 5 years' experience vs 20% in the least disadvantaged — an experience-inequality problem, not just a headcount one ([Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-7222/))
- A Chilean bonus worth ~16% of salary raised retention of high-performers in disadvantaged schools by 17–21pp; England's targeted retention incentive gives up to £6,000/yr for STEM ECTs in the most deprived 30% of schools ([Elacqua et al. 2022](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1338424), [DfE guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/targeted-retention-incentive-payments-for-school-teachers))
- Instructional coaching: 0.49 SD on instruction, 0.18 SD on attainment (60 causal studies) — but preregistered PD trials show near-zero average effect, a real caveat to the "best-evidenced CPD" claim ([Kraft et al. 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/0034654318759268), [Sims et al. 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543231217480))
- Dixons' nine-day fortnight (full pay, no reduction in teaching time) reports lower turnover/sickness after year one, self-reported, RCT pending ([Dixons](https://www.dixonsat.com/articles/how-were-giving-teachers-a-nine-day-fortnight))

### 22 — Department Leadership, Instructional Coaching and Quality Assurance
**Establishes:** weekly, department-embedded instructional coaching (not termly CPD or graded observation) is the best-evidenced lever for teacher development, provided it is run with discipline and honest expectations about effect size.
- Coaching effects shrink at scale (large at-scale programmes ~0.10 SD on achievement vs 0.18 SD average) and more hours did not predict bigger effects — quality/focus beats dosage ([Kraft et al. 2018](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/mkraft/files/kraft_blazar_hogan_2018_teacher_coaching.pdf))
- EEF's Effective PD guidance: 14 mechanisms across 4 categories explain why coaching outperforms generic INSET ([EEF guidance](https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/eef-guidance-reports/effective-professional-development/EEF-Effective-Professional-Development-Guidance-Report.pdf))
- Dixons replaced appraisal entirely with universal coaching plus twice-weekly whole-staff deliberate practice ([dixonsat.com](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/coaching-and-practice))
- Ofsted's 2025 framework moves away from high-stakes "deep dives" toward learning walks and professional conversation — licensing ungraded, developmental internal QA ([Schools inspection toolkit](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/690b26c69456634d9795fde0/Schools_inspection_toolkit.pdf))
- NFER found the ECF/mentoring reform has a null effect on retention overall; protected mentor time, not the national entitlement, is what moves practice ([NFER evaluation](https://www.nfer.ac.uk/publications/evaluation-of-the-early-roll-out-of-the-early-career-framework/))

## Governance & funding

### 07 — Governance and Structural Options for a New School in England, 2025–26
**Establishes:** structure (MAT vs maintained) does not itself drive outcomes — variation within each type dwarfs variation between types — and the practical routes to opening a new school changed sharply in 2026 as the free-school programme was wound down.
- EPI: no systematic difference between MATs and LAs as categories; the gap between top and bottom school groups is worth ~5 GCSE grades across a pupil's subjects ([EPI 2018](https://epi.org.uk/report/school-performance-multi-academy-trusts-local-authorities/))
- Larger MATs (10+) get better attainment for disadvantaged pupils but also show higher persistent absence, suspensions and unexplained exits — EPI recommends judging groups on inclusion as well as attainment ([EPI 2024](https://epi.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Effective_school_groups_final-240404-2.pdf))
- From September 2026 the free-school presumption is abolished; two live routes remain — an LA presumption-style competition, or (from Sept 2026) an LA-proposed maintained school
- Illustrative NFF budget for a 1,200-place, 45% FSM6 secondary: core income plausibly £8,200–8,800/pupil (~£10–10.5m) plus ~£580k pupil premium
- High-needs NFF is suspended for 2026–27 pending SEND reform — a real planning risk for a high-SEND school ([high needs guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/high-needs-funding-arrangements-2026-to-2027/high-needs-funding-2026-to-2027-operational-guide))

## Extended services & family

### 06 — The School as More Than Lessons: Extended Services, Food, Enrichment, Attendance and Family Support
**Establishes:** what fills extended time matters more than the extra time itself, breakfast clubs work mainly through the calm/social mechanism (not just food), and attendance interventions remain the weakest evidence area relative to the scale of the problem.
- EEF/IFS Magic Breakfast trial: ~+2 months KS1 progress from free universal breakfast clubs, plus behaviour gains — though a 2019 randomisation-code error downgraded it from RCT to control-group study ([EEF/IFS](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/magic-breakfast))
- Extending school time rated +2 months average, disadvantaged pupils closer to +3, but only for targeted before/after-school programmes, not blanket longer hours ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/extending-school-time))
- Persistent absence (10%+ missed) autumn 2024/25: 17.8% overall, ~29.9% FSM, 33.3% EHCP ([DfE](https://explore-education-statistics.service.gov.uk/find-statistics/pupil-absence-in-schools-in-england/2024-25-autumn-term))
- School-Home Support family practitioners: 58% of supported children improved attendance (avg +26 days, +46 for severely absent) — charity-reported, not RCT ([SHS](https://www.schoolhomesupport.org.uk/our-impact-improving-persistent-absence/our-impact-reports))
- Trauma-informed whole-school approaches have a weak empirical base; the YEF's UK trial of Trauma Informed Schools UK suffered attrition too severe for any recommendation ([YEF evaluation](https://youthendowmentfund.org.uk/funding/evaluations/trauma-informed-schools-uk/))

### 12 — Case study: Reach Academy Feltham and the Reach Foundation cradle-to-career model
**Establishes:** the most complete UK "cradle-to-career" implementation — small, all-through 2–18, wrapped in a separately-funded family hub — achieves top-decile results at 35%+ FSM, but the wrap-around layer's *independent* causal contribution (beyond the school itself) remains unproven.
- Harlem Children's Zone comparator (Dobbie & Fryer): middle-school entrants gained ~0.23 SD/year in maths, but the school itself drove most of the measured gain — the wrap-around layer's added value is not isolated ([Dobbie & Fryer](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/5-dobbie-fryer-paper.pdf))
- Reach 2025 GCSEs: best-ever results, 44.8% of grades at 7+, at 35.6% FSM and ~15% EHCP intake in a recent Y7 ([Reach 2025 results](https://reachacademyfeltham.com/gcses-2025/))
- EEF rates parental engagement at +5 months, low cost, 94 studies — the strongest-evidenced layer of the model ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/early-years/toolkit/parental-engagement))
- The wrap-around Children's Hub costs ~£420k/year (£3.36m/8yr grant); Best Start Family Hubs rollout (every LA by April 2026) means a new school can now partly state-fund this layer rather than fundraise it entirely
- Reach's own P8 has swung from +1.11 (2017) to +0.50 (2023) back to ~+0.85–0.97 (2024) — even a flagship school fluctuates year to year

## EdTech

### 09 — EdTech and AI in Disadvantaged Schools
**Establishes:** technology is a delivery mechanism, not an intervention in itself — EdTech's average effect on disadvantaged pupils is *lower* than the overall average — and AI evidence is currently strong on teacher workload but weak on pupil outcomes.
- EEF 2025 meta-analysis: EdTech pooled effect d≈0.40 (secondary d≈0.57), but the effect for disadvantaged pupils is lower than overall — a "double-edged sword" ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/evidence-reviews/edtech-interventions-for-disadvantaged-pupils))
- Sutton Trust's *Artificial Advantage?*: private-school teachers 2x as likely to have formal AI training as state (45% vs 21%); within state sector, deprived schools trail affluent ones (18% vs 26%) ([Sutton Trust 2025](https://www.suttontrust.com/news-opinion/all-news-opinion/state-schools-falling-behind-in-new-ai-digital-divide/))
- Oak's Aila shows ~3 hrs/week reported planning-time savings; a proper RCT reports autumn 2026 — pupil-facing AI tutoring evidence is explicitly "limited," per the government's own admission ([Tes](https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/limited-evidence-ai-tutoring-tools-government-admits))
- Sparx Maths: ~1hr/week completed homework over a year associated with +20–30% of a GCSE grade — but access alone had no effect, only sustained completion did (correlational) ([Cambridge](https://www.educ.cam.ac.uk/research/programmes/sparx/))
- DfE staff tech-confidence has fallen (secondary leaders reporting >75% staff confident: 58%→44%, 2023–2025) even as infrastructure improves ([DfE TiSS](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/technology-in-schools-survey-report-2024-to-2025))

## Case studies (named operators)

### 11 — Case study: Michaela Community School (Brent)
**Establishes:** England's highest-P8 school (+2.55, 2024) proves routines + knowledge-rich curriculum + consistency work at a deprived, majority-minority intake, but its ~10% SEND register (vs ~19% nationally) means it is deprived-intake evidence, not high-SEND evidence, and several signature elements (lunch isolation, uniform sanctions) are unsafe to import wholesale.
- P8 +2.55 (2024), 3rd consecutive year as England's highest ([Michaela results](https://www.michaela.education/about-us/exam-results))
- Only ~10% on SEND register vs ~19% nationally ([Good Schools Guide](https://www.goodschoolsguide.co.uk/uk-schools/review/michaela-community-school))
- US "no excuses" causal evidence (lottery studies): +0.25 SD/yr maths, +0.17 SD/yr literacy, but also *more* days suspended ([Cheng et al. 2017](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15582159.2017.1286210))
- April 2024 High Court upheld Michaela's prayer-ritual ban as lawful, following what the school described as violence and racial harassment of staff — evidence the model can generate serious community friction
- A uniform sanction ladder without adjustment is legally and ethically unsafe for a high-SEND intake under Equality Act reasonable-adjustment duties

### 13 — Case study: Dixons Academies Trust
**Establishes:** Dixons combines strong instructional-coaching evidence with a distinctive "Mountain Rescue" mainstream-first SEND model (no TAs, no withdrawal, multidisciplinary pastoral department) — but trust-wide P8 (+0.11) is far weaker than the flagship school (+0.76), showing replication is real but diluted.
- Trust intake: 45% disadvantaged, 20% SEN, 26% EAL — roughly double national disadvantage rates ([Inclusion in Practice](https://inclusioninpractice.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Dixons_Case_Study.pdf))
- Dixons Cottingley (first year of Mountain Rescue): P8 +0.65 (school record), SEND P8 +0.03 (first positive since 2018)
- No TAs at Trinity — TA budget converted into additional qualified-teacher capacity for flexible grouping
- Coaching replaces appraisal entirely; twice-weekly whole-staff deliberate practice ([dixonsat.com](https://www.dixonsat.com/why/coaching-and-practice))
- Ofsted found suspensions at Dixons Allerton "high," with re-suspension of the same pupils — the strict-culture model carries an exclusions risk Mountain Rescue mitigates but does not eliminate

### 14 — Case study: Star Academies (formerly Tauheedul Education Trust)
**Establishes:** a Blackburn-born trust posts some of England's best P8 scores in some of its poorest wards, with results holding for disadvantaged pupils specifically — but its flagship results come from oversubscribed, self-selecting single-sex Muslim-ethos free schools; its clean non-faith takeover test (Highfield, Blackpool) went into special measures.
- Trust topped MAT tables at +0.74 P8 (2019), +1.42 (2018); TIGHS first nationally at P8=2.16 ([Star](https://staracademies.org/news-story/star-academies-top-of-the-class-in-mat-performance-tables/))
- Eden Girls' Manchester: P8 +0.92 for disadvantaged pupils; Eden Boys' Birmingham East +0.89 ([Curriculum substack](https://curriculum.substack.com/p/top-93-schools-for-disadvantaged))
- Star schools "consistently secure the lowest absence rates in England" — attendance treated as a rewarded virtue via the Tauheedul Baccalaureate (Bronze→Platinum on Attendance/Behaviour/Community)
- Highfield Leadership Academy (Blackpool, non-faith takeover): P8 −0.95 (2018), still Requires Improvement in 2024 — the faith start-up template exports imperfectly
- EBacc entry 89.3% vs 40% nationally (2019) — the trust does not lower academic ambition for a poor intake

### 15 — Case study: King Solomon Academy & Ark Schools
**Establishes:** Ark is one of the few MATs with externally verified evidence (Sutton Trust *Chain Effects*) of closing the disadvantage gap network-wide, and KSA's all-through, small-year-group, depth-before-breadth model produces top-1% P8 at 62–67% FSM — though there is no RCT of the KSA model itself.
- Ark disadvantaged P8: −0.09 vs −0.57 national disadvantaged average ([Ark](https://arkonline.org/news/Progress8-Ark-schools-in-top-1percent))
- KSA P8 +1.06 to +1.18 in recent years, ~62–67% FSM, secondary year groups of just 60 pupils
- Ark's Mathematics Mastery EEF RCTs: only +1 month pooled across 127 schools — modest, honestly reported by the network's own evidence base
- English Mastery second trial: ~+4 months reading after 3 years — better than the maths equivalent ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/english-mastery-accelerator-fund))
- Ark cut summative assessment from six to two-three windows a year network-wide, reducing workload while keeping comparable data

### 16 — Case study: XP School Doncaster and the XP Trust
**Establishes:** the crew/relational layer of expeditionary learning works exceptionally (Ofsted Outstanding for Behaviour & Personal Development in both inspections) but the expeditionary-curriculum delivery layer has repeatedly failed to secure GCSE-level subject knowledge, and three consecutive below-average P8 years is a trend the school's own leaders have had to correct.
- P8 +0.21 (2019) → −0.77 (2022) → −0.59 (2023) — a real decline, not statistical noise given the trend length ([Snobe/DfE](https://snobe.co.uk/schools/xp-school))
- EEF's RCT of "Learning through REAL Projects" (the PBL model XP is built on) found no positive impact on literacy/engagement and a possible *negative* effect for FSM pupils ([EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/projects-and-evaluation/projects/project-based-learning))
- The underlying US EL Education model has genuine quasi-experimental evidence: ~5–7 months reading, ~3–10 months maths gains over 2–3 years — but that is the whole model (expeditions + crew + intensive coaching), not PBL alone
- Ofsted downgraded XP from Outstanding (2017) to Good (2023) specifically because older pupils had "significant gaps in subject knowledge" — the school has since added discrete specialist maths teaching
- Staff:student ratio ~1:10 vs ~1:20 LA average — the model's hidden cost driver, financially unreplicable at scale

### 17 — Case study: High-performing US charter networks (KIPP, Success Academy, Uncommon Schools)
**Establishes:** the strongest causal evidence anywhere in this evidence bank comes from US lottery studies, and Dobbie & Fryer's five-practice index (feedback, data-driven instruction, tutoring, more time, high expectations) — not inputs like class size — explains ~45% of the variance in charter effectiveness, and transfers to ordinary district schools when injected directly.
- KIPP: +0.36 SD maths, +0.21 SD reading after 3 years (43-school lottery evaluation) ([Mathematica](https://www.mathematica.org/publications/kipp-middle-schools-impacts-on-achievement-and-other-outcomes-full-report))
- Apollo 20 (five-practice bundle injected into 20 failing Houston district schools): +0.15–0.18 SD/yr maths at ~$2,000/pupil/yr — comparable to attending a high-performing charter ([QJE 2014](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/129/3/1355/1817328))
- KIPP middle-school-only had ~zero effect on degree completion; KIPP middle+high school combined was associated with 39% vs 20% degree completion (+19pp, exploratory not lottery-randomised)
- Success Academy suspends far above NYC norms (one school 25% vs ~3% state average); the 2015 "Got to Go" list of disruptive pupils became a national scandal — the direct English analogue is off-rolling, now an Ofsted red flag
- Miron (2011): 40% of Black male pupils left KIPP middle schools between grades 6–8, with lower backfill rates than districts — a live, partly unresolved attrition critique

## Evidence quality and gaps

**What's genuinely strong across this bank:** the EEF Toolkit's core instructional strands (metacognition, feedback, phonics, tuition), teacher coaching's effect on instruction quality (Kraft et al.'s 60-study meta-analysis), lottery-based US charter/HCZ evidence, and the exclusions/attendance disparity data (DfE statistics) are all high-confidence, causally grounded numbers repeated across multiple briefs.

**What's weaker than it's often presented as:** whole-school warm-strict culture, centralised detentions, restorative practice as a system (vs. an add-on layer), trauma-informed packages, resourced-provision/SEN-unit outcomes, all-through 2–18 structure per se, and most named-operator school-level results (Michaela, Dixons, XP, Reach, Star, KSA) rest on single-school or single-trust case studies with real selection effects — self-selecting intakes, oversubscription, exceptional founding leadership — not RCTs. Several briefs (04, 18, 19, 21, 22) explicitly flag that the operational mechanics beloved of high performers (exact consequence-ladder shape, 5-minute on-call targets, "five-a-day" as a bundle) have essentially no direct trial evidence behind their precise configuration, only coherence with the causal evidence on consistency and routines.

**Known gaps and honesty flags repeated across the briefs:**
- "Months of progress" as a metric for converting EEF effect sizes is itself contested (Jones critique, cited in brief 06).
- Effect sizes shrink sharply at scale across nearly every domain reviewed (coaching, mastery curricula, catch-up reading programmes, PD generally) — efficacy trials routinely fail to replicate at effectiveness scale.
- SEND causal evidence is thin almost everywhere it matters most for this project: resourced-provision outcomes, adaptive teaching as a construct, "five-a-day" as a bundle, and how to reconcile consistent behaviour systems with disability-driven adjustments all rest on professional consensus and case studies, not trials.
- Several named-operator claims are self-reported (Oak's workload savings, School-Home Support's attendance gains, Eedi's vendor-commissioned RCT) and should be weighted accordingly.
- Policy is moving fast and some of what this bank cites will be superseded: Progress 8 is suspended until 2027, high-needs NFF is suspended for 2026–27, the free-school presumption ends September 2026, and SEND reform implementation runs to ~2029–2035 — any design built on this evidence needs a re-verification pass before build, not just at compilation (July 2026).
